The Union Executive

1. Introduction

One of the most important organs of the Indian government is the Union Executive. Its primary function is to ensure that the laws passed by the Parliament are properly enforced and administered.

  • The Union Executive consists of the President, the Vice-President, the Prime Minister, and the Council of Ministers.
  • Together, they form the Political Executive, who are assisted in their day-to-day operations by a body of Permanent Executives (civil servants).

2. The President of India

The President of India is the constitutional head of the Republic of India and is regarded as the first citizen of the country. All executive actions of the Union Government are formally taken in the President's name.

Fact Dr. Rajendra Prasad was the first President of India (1950–1962). He was deeply influenced by Mahatma Gandhi, played a major role in the making of the Indian Constitution, and was awarded the Bharat Ratna in 1962.

Qualifications

To be eligible for the office of the President, a candidate must meet the following criteria:

  • Must be a citizen of India.
  • Must be at least 35 years of age.
  • Must not hold any office of profit under the Central or State government, or any public authority.
  • Must be qualified for election as a member of the Lok Sabha.
  • Must not be a member of the Parliament or any State Legislature.

Presidential Election and Term

Concept Electoral College: The President is elected indirectly by a temporary body called the Electoral College. It consists of the elected members of both the Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha, the Legislative Assemblies of the states, and the Union Territories of Delhi and Puducherry.
  • Term: The President is elected for a term of five years and is eligible for re-election.
  • Resignation: Can resign from office at any time by addressing a letter of resignation to the Vice President.
  • Impeachment: Can be removed before the term ends strictly for the violation of the Constitution through a detailed process called impeachment.

Powers of the President

  • Executive Powers: Appoints the Prime Minister, Council of Ministers, Attorney General, CAG, Election Commissioners, UPSC members, State Governors, and Ambassadors.
  • Financial Powers: Ensures the annual budget is placed before Parliament. Money Bills can only be introduced with the President's prior recommendation.
  • Judicial Powers: Appoints the Chief Justice of the Supreme Court and High Courts. Possesses the power to grant pardon (Article 72) or reduce sentences. Enjoys judicial immunity (no criminal proceedings during the term).
  • Diplomatic Powers: Represents India in international forums. All international treaties and agreements are negotiated and concluded in the President's name.
  • Military Powers: Serves as the Supreme Commander of the Armed Forces. Appoints the chiefs of the Army, Navy, and Air Force, and has the authority to declare war or conclude peace.
Important Emergency Powers: The President deals with three types of emergencies: National Emergency (Article 352), State Emergency / President's Rule (Article 356 & 365), and Financial Emergency (Article 360).

Limitations of the President

The President is the nominal head (head of the State, not the government) and exercises powers on the advice of the Council of Ministers. While the President can send a bill or piece of advice back for reconsideration once, they are bound to act according to it if the Parliament or Council passes it a second time.

3. The Vice-President of India

The Vice-President is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha and cannot hold any other office of profit.

Qualifications and Term

  • Must be a citizen of India and at least 35 years old.
  • Must be qualified to be elected as a Rajya Sabha member.
  • Elected by an electoral college consisting of members of both Houses of Parliament.
  • Holds office for a five-year term.
  • Can be removed by a resolution passed by a majority in the Rajya Sabha and agreed to by the Lok Sabha.

Powers and Functions

  • Presiding Officer: Presides over Rajya Sabha sessions, maintaining order, and exercises a casting vote in the case of a tie.
  • Acting President: Takes over as the Acting President in the absence of the President due to death, resignation, or removal. During this period, they enjoy all the emoluments and immunities of the President.

4. The Prime Minister of India

The Prime Minister is the head of the government and the real executive authority. Appointed by the President, the PM advises the President on the appointment of all other ministers.

Relations with the President

  • Article 74: Mandates a Council of Ministers to aid and advise the President, working under the leadership of the PM.
  • Article 75: The PM is appointed by the President, and other ministers are appointed by the President on the PM's advice.
  • The PM acts as the vital link of communication between the President and the Council of Ministers regarding all administrative decisions.

Relations with the Council of Ministers

  • The PM allocates portfolios among ministers and coordinates the work of various departments.
  • Has the authority to change Cabinet members or demand a minister's resignation at any time.

Relations with the Parliament

  • Acts as the leader of the Lok Sabha and the chief spokesperson of the government on the floor of the house.
  • Advises the President on summoning and proroguing Parliament sessions and can recommend the dissolution of the Lok Sabha.

Role in Foreign Affairs

The PM plays a critical role in shaping the nation's foreign policy. They represent India in international forums (like the UN, SAARC, Commonwealth) and hold vital talks with world leaders to negotiate issues related to the welfare of the nation.

5. Working of the Council of Ministers

  • Collective Responsibility: The Council is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha. A vote of no-confidence against one minister is treated as a vote against the entire Council of Ministers.
  • Cabinet Solidarity: Ministers must function together as a team. If a minister strongly disagrees with a Cabinet decision, their only honorable option is to leave gracefully.

6. Permanent Executives (Civil Servants)

While the PM and ministers act as temporary political executives, the ongoing, day-to-day administration is handled by Permanent Executives.

  • They are highly trained government employees who do not belong to any political party.
  • Candidates are recruited through the rigorous UPSC examinations to join services like the IAS, IFS, and IPS.
  • They provide expert administrative guidance and assist the political executives at both the central and state levels.
