DISEASES AND FIRST AID

1. Health and Disease

Normally, a person is said to be healthy if he or she is not suffering from any disease. Health is defined as a state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being, and not merely an absence of disease or infirmity.

Concept

Disease is a departure from normal health through structural or functional disorders of the body.

2. Categories of Diseases

There are two major categories of diseases: Communicable and Non-communicable.

Communicable or Infectious Diseases

These diseases are spread from an infected person to a healthy one. They are caused by pathogens (microorganisms) such as viruses, bacteria, fungi, worms, and protozoa. These diseases can spread through air, water, food, physical contact, cuts, sexual contact, and from insects like mosquitoes and flies.

Methods of Disease Transmission

  • Direct method: Direct contact with an infected person (e.g., chicken pox, measles, and fungal infections).
  • Indirect method: Touching and sharing items used by the infected person (like a towel, handkerchief, or same bed). Contaminated food and water also cause transmission of intestinal diseases.
  • Vectors: Organisms that carry germs from a source of infection but themselves do not get the infection.
Fact

Vectors like mosquitoes gain the germs of blood of a malaria patient while feeding on them, and transfer these germs into the blood of a healthy human being when they bite them.

3. Common Communicable Diseases

  • Malaria: Caused by a protozoan (Plasmodium) and transmitted by the bite of the female Anopheles mosquito. Symptoms include intermittent high fever accompanied by violent shivering.
  • Dengue & Chikungunya: Viral diseases spread by the bite of the Aedes mosquito (specifically Aedes aegypti). Symptoms include fever, headache, muscle, and acute joint pain.
  • Measles: A highly contagious viral infection of the respiratory system causing nasal discharge, fever, and eruption of red papules.
  • AIDS: Caused by HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus). It leads to severe weight loss and a breakdown of the body's normal defense against infection. Spread primarily through sexual contact and infected syringes.
  • COVID-19: Caused by the SARS-CoV-2 Virus. Symptoms include fever, chills, cough, and fatigue. Preventive measures include isolation, sanitization, and vaccination.
Important

Preventive Measures: To avoid mosquito-borne diseases, maintain public hygiene by ensuring proper disposal of domestic wastes and checking that there is no stagnant water around homes, as these act as a breeding ground for mosquitoes.

4. Vaccination and Immunization

Immunization is an artificial way of achieving protection from infections by the introduction of dead or weakened germs into one's body.

Concept

Vaccination is the practice of artificially introducing germs or germ substances into the body for developing resistance to particular diseases. Scientifically, this practice is called prophylaxis, and the material introduced is called the vaccine.

Methods of Preparing Vaccines

  • Using killed germs (e.g., TAB vaccine for typhoid, Salk's vaccine for poliomyelitis).
  • Using living weakened germs (e.g., BCG vaccine for tuberculosis, measles vaccine).
  • Using fully virulent living germs (e.g., vaccine for smallpox).
  • Using Toxoids, which are extracts of toxins secreted by bacteria made harmless (e.g., vaccines for diphtheria and tetanus).

The idea of vaccination was conceived by Edward Jenner (1749-1823) who observed that milkmaids in contact with cowpox rarely suffered from smallpox.

5. First Aid

First aid is the immediate care or help given to a patient at the time of a medical emergency before he or she is taken to a doctor or hospital.

Common First Aid Treatments

  • Burns: For superficial burns, pour cold water over the burnt area and cover with a sterile dressing. Apply oily substances (ointment, butter, vaseline). Do not cut blisters.
  • Snake bite: Tie it tightly with a tourniquet above the spot to prevent the spreading of venom (poison) into the blood stream. Squeeze out some blood from the wound.
  • Sting (Bee or Wasp): Pull out the sting, squeeze out some blood to force out the venom, and apply some alkali like baking soda on that spot.
  • Swallowing poison: Induce vomiting by making the patient drink as much salt water as possible.
  • Bleeding: Raise the affected body part, press the cut surface with clean cotton/wool, and if possible apply a mild antiseptic.
  • Fractures: Do not move the fractured part or limb. Lay the victim comfortably and tie a sling to rest the arm.
  • Unconsciousness: Loosen the clothes and let fresh air come into the room. If breathing stops, artificial respiration should be started.
  • Eye: If anything falls in the eye, do not rub it. Wash them gently with clean water.
Important

First aid is temporary assistance. Always seek proper professional medical treatment immediately after providing first aid!
