FOOD PRODUCTION

Food is the prime need of all living beings. From hunting and gathering, humans gradually shifted to cultivating food and domesticating animals. Today, food is obtained from plants, animals, and even through the aid of various microorganisms.

1. Microorganisms in Food Production

Microorganisms (or microbes) are organisms too small to be seen with the naked eye. While many are harmful and cause diseases, several are highly beneficial in the food industry.

Uses of Bacteria

  • Curd Formation: Curd is made from milk by the action of bacteria, predominantly Lactobacillus. These bacteria multiply in warm conditions, converting milk sugar (lactose) into lactic acid, which causes milk to curdle.
  • Cheese Making: Similar to curd, cheese is produced using specific bacteria to curdle the milk, followed by processing, salting, and ripening.
  • Tea and Tobacco Curing: Bacteria are used to remove bitterness and add a specific flavor and aroma to tea leaves and tobacco.
  • Alcohol and Vinegar: Specific bacterial actions are required to produce alcohol and vinegar commercially.
  • Agriculture: Bacteria living in the root nodules of leguminous plants help in nitrogen fixation, increasing soil fertility naturally.

Uses of Fungi

  • Yeast: A unicellular fungus crucial in baking and brewing. It undergoes fermentation, breaking down sugars to produce alcohol and carbon dioxide. The release of carbon dioxide gas makes bread dough rise and gives baked goods their fluffy texture.
  • Mushrooms: These are macro-fungi. Many varieties (like button mushrooms) are edible and highly nutritious, being rich in proteins.
Important Warning about Mushrooms: Never eat wild mushrooms growing naturally in fields or woods, as many varieties are highly poisonous.

2. Agriculture: The Primary Source of Food

Agriculture is the science of growing plants, cultivating crops, and raising animals for food and other human needs. Plants of the same kind grown on a large scale are called crop plants.

Types of Cultivated Crops

Crop Category Examples
Cereal Crops Rice, Wheat, Barley, Maize
Pulses Gram, Peas, Beans
Oil Seed Crops Groundnut, Mustard, Sunflower
Root & Tuber Crops Sweet potato, Carrot, Potato, Tapioca
Sugar & Plantation Crops Sugarcane, Coffee, Tea, Rubber
Concept Food Crops vs. Cash Crops:
• Food Crops: Cultivated primarily to feed humans (e.g., cereals, pulses).
• Cash Crops: Cultivated primarily for commercial purposes and profit (e.g., rubber, coffee, cotton).

Crop Seasons in India

  • Kharif Crops: Sown during the monsoon season (June/July) and harvested in autumn. They require a lot of water and a hot, humid climate. Examples: Rice, Maize, Soybean, Cotton.
  • Rabi Crops: Sown in winter (October/November) and harvested in spring. They require cooler temperatures and moderate water. Examples: Wheat, Mustard, Gram, Peas.

3. Horticulture

Horticulture is the branch of agriculture that deals with the cultivation of vegetables, fruits, ornamental plants, and flowers, often for aesthetic or high commercial value.

  • Vegetables: Cabbage, Cauliflower, Tomatoes, etc.
  • Fruits: Mango, Grapes, Apples, etc.
  • Ornamental & Flowers: Coleus, Rose, Jasmine, Marigold.

4. Organic Farming & Green Revolution

Organic Farming

Due to the harmful effects of chemical fertilizers and pesticides on human health and soil, many farmers are shifting to organic farming. This system relies on natural organic manure (prepared from plant and animal wastes) and biological pest control instead of synthetic chemicals. It maintains soil vitality and produces healthier food.

The Green Revolution

Introduced in India in the early 1960s, the Green Revolution was a period when agricultural productivity increased drastically. This was achieved by:

  • Using High Yielding Varieties (HYV) of seeds.
  • Application of modern chemical fertilizers and pesticides.
  • Improved irrigation methods.
Fact M.S. Swaminathan, an Indian scientist, played a pivotal role in developing and implementing the Green Revolution in India, making the country self-sufficient in food grains, particularly wheat.

5. Animal Husbandry

The branch of biology dealing with the feeding, shelter, caring, and breeding of domesticated animals is called Animal Husbandry. Farm animals are collectively known as livestock.

Milch (Milk-Yielding) Animals

Animals reared for milk are called milch animals (e.g., cows, buffaloes, goats). Buffaloes are the major source of milk in India, providing rich, nutritious milk.

  • Draught breeds: Males are used for heavy labor (ploughing, transport); females yield less milk.
  • Dairy breeds: High milk-yielding females; males are poor workers.
  • Dual-purpose breeds: Females give a good quantity of milk, and males are good for draught work.
Fact The White Revolution (Operation Flood): Spearheaded by Dr. Verghese Kurien, this movement drastically increased milk production in India through dairy cooperatives, making milk and its products accessible nationwide.

Meat and Egg-Yielding Animals

  • Poultry Farming: Rearing birds like chickens, ducks, and turkeys for meat (broilers) and eggs (layers). Breeds are categorized into indigenous (Desi) and exotic (foreign) breeds. Proper housing, lighting, and feeding are essential for optimal yield.
  • Piggery: Rearing pigs for meat (pork, bacon, ham) and animal fat (lard).
  • Sheep & Goats: Rared for mutton, wool, and skins.

Other Commercial Animal Rearing

  • Pisciculture (Fish Farming): Rearing fish commercially in tanks or enclosures. Fish are an excellent source of protein and fish liver oil (rich in Vitamins A and D).
    • Freshwater fish: Rohu, Catla, Mrigal.
    • Marine fish: Tuna, Cod, Sardine.
  • Apiculture (Bee Keeping): Rearing of honey bees in artificial hives (apiaries) for the commercial production of honey and beeswax. A colony consists of one Queen, a few male Drones, and thousands of sterile female Workers.
  • Sericulture: Rearing of silkworms (Bombyx mori) for the production of raw silk. The larvae feed heavily on mulberry leaves and spin a protective cocoon around themselves, from which the silk thread is extracted.
