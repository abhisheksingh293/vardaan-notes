Minerals

1. Introduction to Minerals

Nature has assisted in the development of human civilizations by providing vital resources like water, land, and wildlife. One of the most important resources required for building a country's industrial base is minerals.

Concept A mineral is a naturally occurring, pure inorganic substance found within the Earth's crust that possesses definite chemical properties. Almost the entire Earth's crust is made up of minerals found in rocks and soils. Minerals from which metals can be profitably extracted are known as ores.

Our modern civilization has heavily evolved using minerals and energy resources. They provide raw material to various industries to build basic infrastructure like roads, cars, computers, and fertilizers.

2. Types of Minerals

Based on their composition, minerals are broadly classified into two main categories: Metallic and Non-metallic.

A. Metallic Minerals

These are minerals that contain raw metals. They generally have a shiny appearance and are primarily found in igneous and metamorphic rocks.

  • They are ductile (can be drawn into wires) and malleable (can be beaten into sheets).
  • They are good conductors of heat and electricity.
  • They have a high melting point and are typically found in a solid state at room temperature.

Metallic minerals are further divided into:

  • Ferrous Minerals: These contain an iron content (e.g., Iron ore, Manganese).
  • Non-Ferrous Minerals: These do not contain iron (e.g., Copper, Gold, Silver, Bauxite).

B. Non-Metallic Minerals

These minerals do not contain metals. They have a dull appearance and are generally found in sedimentary rocks.

  • They are neither ductile nor malleable.
  • They are poor conductors of heat and electricity.

C. Mineral Fuels (Fossil Fuels)

These include essential energy-producing fuels like coal, petroleum, and natural gas.

Fact Fossil fuels were formed from the dead and decaying remains of plants and animals that were trapped under the Earth millions of years ago. Intense heat and pressure transformed this organic matter into fossil fuels. Petroleum is often referred to as Black Gold because of its immense value and wide range of derivatives (plastics, cosmetics, fuels).

3. Extraction of Minerals: Mining

Minerals are generally buried below the surface of the Earth and need to be brought out for human use. The process of extracting minerals from the Earth is called mining.

Types of Mining

Depending on the depth and location of the mineral deposits, different mining techniques are employed:

  • Quarrying: Used when minerals are found very close to the Earth's surface. The upper layer is simply removed, and minerals are dug out.
  • Surface Mining (Open-cast Mining): Involves the removal of surface vegetation, dirt, bedrock, and other top layers to reach shallow ore deposits (e.g., coal and salt).
  • Underground Mining (Shaft Mining): A deep hole or shaft is dug to extract valuable minerals that lie at considerable depths, such as gold, silver, iron, and diamonds.
Important Drilling is a highly specialized extraction method. When minerals like petroleum (mineral oil) or natural gas are located far below the earth's surface or under the ocean floor, deep wells are bored to pump them out. This distinct process is called drilling, not mining.

4. Need for Conservation of Minerals

Minerals are an exhaustible (non-renewable) resource. This means they are present in limited quantities on Earth. Because they take millions of years to form naturally, their rate of replenishment is incredibly slow compared to our rapidly increasing rate of consumption.

If we continue exploiting them at the current pace, these resources will eventually deplete, halting industrial progress and daily conveniences.

Methods of Conservation

To ensure minerals are available for future generations, we must adopt sustainable practices:

  • Recycling: Reusing scrap metals and materials to reduce the need for fresh mining.
  • Reducing Wastage: Improving mining and processing technologies so that less raw material is wasted during extraction.
  • Replacing / Substitutes: Using alternative, renewable resources (like solar or wind energy instead of coal) wherever possible.
