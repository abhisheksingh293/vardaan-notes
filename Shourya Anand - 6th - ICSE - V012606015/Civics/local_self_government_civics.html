Local Self-Government In Rural Areas

Concept

Panchayat is derived from the Sanskrit words pancha meaning "five" and ayat meaning "assembly". Historically, a panchayat was a council of five village elders who settled disputes. Today, it forms the basis of rural self-governance in India.

1. Need for Local Self-Government

India is a massive country with a population of over one billion people, divided into 28 states. While the Central Government sits in New Delhi and handles national issues (like defense and foreign policy), it cannot fully understand or manage every local issue. This is why local self-governments were created.

Advantages of Local Self-Government:

  • Local Knowledge: Local representatives understand their own problems best (e.g., where a well is needed or which roads require repair).
  • Active Participation: Villagers participate in their own development by helping with tasks like cleaning ponds, building canals, and making decisions.
  • Leadership Training: Local bodies train people in administration, preparing them for higher offices.
  • Reduced Burden: They drastically reduce the workload of the state and central governments by addressing local issues efficiently and quickly.

2. The Panchayati Raj System

After India's independence, leaders like Mahatma Gandhi strongly advocated for the revival of panchayats to involve the common man in governance. Based on the recommendations of the Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1956), India adopted a clear three-tier system:

  • Village Level: Gram Panchayat
  • Block Level: Panchayat Samiti
  • District Level: Zila Parishad
Fact

In 1992, the Constitution of India made it mandatory to strengthen panchayats by passing two critical Acts. These Acts ensured regular elections and reserved one-third of all seats for women, as well as proportionate seats for Scheduled Castes (SC) and Scheduled Tribes (ST).

3. The Village Level

The Gram Sabha

The Gram Sabha acts as the general assembly of the village. It meets at least twice a year to discuss village development.

  • Membership: All adult residents of the village who are 18 years and above are automatic members.
  • Role: It elects the members of the Gram Panchayat and actively supervises their day-to-day functioning.
  • Accountability: It reviews the Panchayat's accounts and has the power to remove non-performing members through a vote of no confidence.

The Gram Panchayat

The Gram Panchayat is the executive committee of the village, elected by the Gram Sabha for a term of five years. It usually consists of 5 to 30 members depending on the village population.

  • Pradhan / Sarpanch: The elected head who calls for and leads the Panchayat meetings. The Up-Pradhan acts as the deputy in their absence.
  • Panchayat Secretary (Sevak): A permanent, salaried government employee who maintains official records and keeps account of the expenditures.

Key Functions of the Gram Panchayat:

  • Civic Amenities: Supplying clean drinking water, running primary health centers, maintaining public wells, tanks, and tubewells.
  • Agricultural Support: Aiding farmers by supplying quality seeds and fertilizers, treating cattle, and managing irrigation facilities.
  • Administrative Duties: Keeping records of births and deaths, supervising primary schools, and reporting complaints to higher authorities.
  • Optional Functions (If funds permit): Organising fairs, running libraries, and managing local village markets.

Sources of Income: Taxes on houses, shops, fairs, and markets; fees collected from the sale and purchase of cattle; and crucial grants and loans from the State Government.

The Nyaya Panchayat

The Nyaya Panchayat acts as a local village court. Usually, one Nyaya Panchayat covers three or four villages.

Important

The primary purpose of the Nyaya Panchayat is to provide quick and inexpensive justice. It only hears minor civil and criminal cases. If a villager is unhappy with the verdict, they can appeal to higher authorities like the District Court or Sessions Court.

4. The Block Level (Panchayat Samiti)

The second tier of the Panchayati Raj system is the Panchayat Samiti (or Block Samiti). A block is formed by grouping together several neighboring villages (typically around 100 villages).

  • Composition: It is not directly elected by the people. Members include the Sarpanchs of all villages in the block, local MPs and MLAs, and specially reserved representatives for women, SC, and ST.
  • Leadership: The members elect a Block Pramukh (Chairperson) and a Vice-Chairperson for a five-year term.
  • Executive Officer: The State Government appoints a Block Development Officer (BDO) to oversee the executive functioning of the block.

Functions: They execute development programs across villages in the block, promote small-scale industries, encourage literacy and education, and obtain funds from the state government for block-level projects.

5. The District Level (Zila Parishad)

The Zila Parishad is the third and highest tier of the Panchayati Raj. It supervises and coordinates the activities of the Gram Panchayats and Block Samitis under its jurisdiction.

  • Composition: Consists of the Chairpersons of all Block Samitis in the district, local MPs, MLAs, MLCs, and representatives from cooperative societies.
  • Administration: The government appoints a Deputy Commissioner to look after the Zila Parishad's overall working, along with a permanent Secretary to maintain records.

Functions: The Zila Parishad prepares a comprehensive development plan for the entire district. It implements the Five Year Plans at the district level, coordinates state grants among block samitis, and takes steps to increase agricultural yields.
