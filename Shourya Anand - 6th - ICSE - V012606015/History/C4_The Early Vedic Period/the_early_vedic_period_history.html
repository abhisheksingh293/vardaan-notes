THE EARLY VEDIC PERIOD

1. Origin of the Aryans

After the decline of the Harappan civilisation, new settlers came to North-west India around 1500 BCE. They were called the Aryans. Scholars have various theories about their origin.

  • The most widely accepted belief is that they came from Central Asia, near the Caspian Sea.
  • According to historians, one group broke away from the main stock of Indo-Europeans in Central Asia and moved eastwards. They are called Indo-Aryans.
  • They first migrated to present-day Iran and then entered India, settling in the region of the Sapta Sindhu, between 1500 and 1000 BCE.
Concept

Brahmavarta: The Sapta Sindhu region where Aryans initially settled was called Brahmavarta or the 'Land of the Gods'.

Aryavarta: As the population increased, they moved eastward and settled in the Ganga-Yamuna plains, later known as Aryavarta or the 'Land of the Aryans'.

Gradually, they spread across northern and western India up to the Vindhya Mountains.

2. Early Vedic Period

Our chief source of information is the Rigveda, composed around 1500 BCE. The Vedas are the earliest literary records of the Aryans. Therefore, this period in history is also known as the Vedic Period, divided into the Early and Later Vedic periods.

Fact

There are four vedas: Rigveda, Yajurveda, Samaveda, and Atharvaveda. The word 'Veda' means knowledge. These were passed down orally for generations and are called Shruti ('that which is heard').

  • The Rigveda, the oldest, has 1028 slokas (hymns), grouped into ten books.
  • It provides insight into the political, social, economic, and religious life of the early Aryans.
  • Our information about early Aryans is also based on excavations at Bhagwanpura in Haryana and three other sites in Punjab, which have revealed remains of pots dating from 1500-1000 BCE.

3. Life of the People

Political Structure

The Aryans gave up their nomadic lifestyle and lived in settled tribes called janas. The land they lived in was called janapada.

  • Villages (gramas) were the smallest units of a jana and consisted of several families.
  • Tribes often fought to protect their most valuable resource—cattle.
  • Each tribe was headed by a chieftain called rajan, chosen for his strength and wisdom. His main duties were to protect the tribe and maintain peace. He could be removed if he became unfit to rule.

The rajan was supported by:

  • Senani: Commander-in-chief
  • Rajpurohita: High priest and chief adviser
  • Gramani: Village headman
Important

Two popular assemblies, the sabha and the samiti, advised the rajan and helped control his power. The oldest Rig Vedic assembly was known as Vidhata. It included women too.

Social Life

The Aryans lived in joint families (kula) led by the eldest male member, meaning these families were patriarchal.

  • Women were respected and took part in yajnas along with their husbands.
  • Some learned women composed hymns, debated, and even participated in assemblies.
  • Women could choose their husbands in a ceremony called swayamvara.
  • There was no practice of child marriage or sati.
  • Widow remarriage was allowed.

Emergence of the Caste System

Occupations started to divide Aryan society into different groups called varnas:

  • Brahmins: Priests who performed rituals and imparted education.
  • Kshatriyas: Warriors who fought wars and protected the people.
  • Vaishyas: Farmers, traders, and craftsmen.
  • Shudras: Labourers and servants (including dasas); did menial jobs and were the original inhabitants.
Concept

This varna system was flexible during the Early Vedic Period. There were no restrictions on changing occupations, inter-marriage, or dining together. A Rigvedic verse highlights this: "I am a poet, my father is a doctor and my mother grinds corn."

4. Economy

Agriculture and cattle-rearing were the main occupations of the early Aryans. They ploughed their fields with the help of wooden ploughs drawn by oxen.

  • Barley, wheat, fruits, and vegetables were grown.
  • The cow was considered very valuable.
  • Horses, introduced from Central Asia, were used in battles and to pull chariots.
  • Trade was conducted using gold and oxen as mediums of exchange.
  • Other occupations: Hunting, pottery, spinning and weaving, carpentry, metallurgy (copper and bronze), and leather crafting.
  • Shipping was limited to the navigation of rivers for inland trade.

Dress and Amusements

The Aryans dressed similarly to the Harappans.

  • Men wore a dhoti and a shoulder cloth, and tied turbans.
  • Women wore sarees and woollen upper garments.
  • Both genders wore ornaments made from gold, silver, and other metals.
  • The Aryans were fond of outdoor life. Music, dancing, chariot racing, hunting, gambling, and game of dice were their favourite pastimes. Flute and drums were used for music.

5. Religion

In the Early Vedic Period, Aryans worshipped forces of nature as gods and goddesses. These gods were classified into three groups according to their abode:

  • Gods of the sky: Mitra and Varuna
  • Gods of mid-air: Indra and Maruts
  • Gods of the Earth: Agni and Soma
Fact

Important Gods:
- Indra: The most important God; God of rain and war, who led the Aryan armies to victory.
- Agni: God of fire, to whom all offerings were made.
- Surya: Sun God.
- Varuna: Upheld the cosmic order.
- Yama: God of death.
- Soma: God of a plant from which a special intoxicating drink was extracted for special occasions.

There were no temples or idols; instead, worship took place in the open, accompanied by sacrifices and hymns in praise of the elements (Earth, fire, water, air, and sky) to get earthly happiness and success in battle.

  • The whole family took part in simple yajnas, offering milk, ghee, and grain to the gods.
  • Fire held a special place of esteem in the religion.

6. Other Contributions

The Aryans contributed to architecture through early ideas recorded in Sanskrit verses, later compiled as Vastu Shastra, possibly the world's earliest treatise on architecture. Its legendary author was Maya Danava.

Vastu Shastra blends five elements—air, water, fire, Earth, and space—to promote harmony between humans and their environment. It continues to influence architecture today.
