Properties of Triangles

1. Introduction to Triangles

Concept

A Triangle is a simple closed two-dimensional figure bounded by three line segments. It is the simplest polygon. A triangle has three vertices, three sides, and three interior angles.

In the figure above, ∆ABC has:

  • Vertices: A, B, and C.
  • Sides: AB, BC, and CA.
  • Angles: ∠BAC, ∠ABC, and ∠BCA (often written simply as ∠A, ∠B, and ∠C).

2. Angle Sum Property of a Triangle

Fact

The Angle Sum Theorem states that the sum of the lengths of the three interior angles of any triangle is exactly 180°.

∠A + ∠B + ∠C = 180°

This is one of the most fundamental properties used in geometry to find missing angles. If you know any two angles of a triangle, you can easily calculate the third by subtracting their sum from 180°.

3. Exterior Angle Property

Concept

If any side of a triangle is extended, the angle formed outside the triangle is called an Exterior Angle. The interior angles opposite to it are called Interior Opposite Angles.

Fact

Theorem: An exterior angle of a triangle is equal to the sum of its two interior opposite angles.

∠ACD = ∠A + ∠B

4. Triangle Inequality Properties

Not every three lengths can form a triangle. The lengths of the sides must satisfy specific mathematical rules.

Important

Rule 1 (Sum): The sum of the lengths of any two sides of a triangle is always greater than the length of the third side.
AB + BC > AC | BC + CA > AB | CA + AB > BC

Rule 2 (Difference): The difference between the lengths of any two sides is always less than the length of the third side.

ICSE Tip: You will often be given three numbers (e.g., 3cm, 4cm, 8cm) and asked if they can form a triangle. Check the sum of the two smallest numbers (3+4=7). Since 7 is NOT greater than 8, these lengths cannot form a triangle.

5. Properties of Right-Angled Triangles

In a right-angled triangle, the side opposite to the right angle (90°) is the longest side and is called the Hypotenuse. The other two sides are called the legs (Base and Perpendicular).

Fact

Pythagoras Theorem: In a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides.

(Hypotenuse)² = (Base)² + (Perpendicular)²
AC² = AB² + BC²

6. Medians and Altitudes

  • Median: A line segment joining a vertex of a triangle to the mid-point of its opposite side. A triangle has 3 medians, and they intersect at a point called the Centroid.
  • Altitude (Height): A perpendicular line segment drawn from a vertex to its opposite side. A triangle has 3 altitudes, and they intersect at a point called the Orthocenter.
