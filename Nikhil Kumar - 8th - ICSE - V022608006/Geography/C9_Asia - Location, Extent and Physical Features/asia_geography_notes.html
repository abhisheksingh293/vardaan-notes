Asia: Location and Physical Features

1. Introduction & Extent

Asia is the world's largest and most populous continent, situated primarily in the eastern and northern hemispheres. It is often referred to as the "continent of contrasts" due to its extreme variations in relief, climate, natural vegetation, religion, and culture.

  • Area: Approximately 44.4 million sq. km (occupies slightly more than one-third of the Earth's total land area).
  • Population: Hosts about 4.3 billion people, representing nearly two-thirds of the current global population, making it the most densely populated continent.
  • Latitudinal Extent: Between 10°S and 80°N.
  • Longitudinal Extent: Between 26°E and 170°W.
  • Dimensions: The continent spans 8560 km from North to South, and stretches 9700 km from East to West.
Concept

Eurasia: Because Asia is a continuous landmass with the continent of Europe, the combined territory of both continents is geologically and historically referred to as Eurasia.

Boundaries of Asia

Asia is bordered by oceans on three sides and natural boundaries on the fourth:

  • East: Pacific Ocean
  • South: Indian Ocean
  • North: Arctic Ocean
  • West (Europe Border): Separated by the Ural Mountains, the Caspian Sea, and the Caucasus Mountains.
  • North America Border: Separated by the narrow Bering Strait.
  • Africa Border: Connected via the Sinai Peninsula.

2. Political Divisions

The vast continent is comprised of 48 countries of significantly varying sizes.

  • Largest Countries: Russia and China are among the largest in the world.
  • Smallest Countries: Maldives and Singapore.
  • Regional Groupings: East Asia (e.g., Japan, China), South Asia (e.g., India), South-East Asia (e.g., Thailand, Vietnam), West Asia (e.g., Saudi Arabia, Iran), and Former USSR countries (e.g., Kazakhstan, Georgia).
Fact File

Extreme Features of Asia:

  • Largest Lake: The Caspian Sea.
  • Highest & Largest Plateau: The Tibetan Plateau (often called the "Roof of the World").
  • Deepest Freshwater Lake: Lake Baikal (1637 m).
  • Coldest Place: Verkhoyansk, Russia (recorded temperatures around -70°C).
  • Hottest Place: Jacobabad, Pakistan (recorded temperatures around 50°C).
  • Wettest Place: Mawsynram, Meghalaya, India (annual rainfall of 11,873 mm).
  • Largest Delta: The Ganga-Brahmaputra delta (India-Bangladesh).

3. Physical Divisions

The topography of Asia is extremely diverse and can be classified into five major physical divisions:

3.1 The Northern Lowlands

Located bordering the Arctic Ocean, extending from the Ural Mountains to the Bering Sea. It is divided into two primary plains:

  • Siberian Plain: The largest continuous plain in the world, sloping northwards. It is drained by three major rivers: Ob, Yenisei, and Lena. Because these rivers flow into the frozen Arctic Ocean, their lower courses freeze in winter, causing water to spread and form massive swamps and marshes.
  • Turan Plain: Located in the south around the Aral Sea. It is drained by the Amu Darya and Syr Darya rivers.
Concept

Inland Drainage: The Turan Plain is a landlocked area where rivers drain into an inland sea (the Aral Sea) instead of flowing into an open ocean. This creates an area of inland drainage.

3.2 The Central Highlands

A complex system of young fold mountains and intermontane plateaus located south of the Northern Lowlands. The ranges converge at nodal points known as knots.

  • The Pamir Knot: Radiates the Hindu Kush, Kirthar, and Suleiman to the west, and the Karakoram, Tien Shan, Kunlun Shan, and Himalayas to the east.
  • The Armenian Knot: Radiates the Elburz and Zagros eastwards, and the Caucasus, Pontic, and Taurus westwards.
  • The Himalayas: The highest mountain range globally, culminating in Mount Everest (8848 m).
  • Intermontane Plateaus: Plateaus enclosed by mountain ranges, such as the Anatolia Plateau (Turkey), Plateau of Iran, and the Tibetan Plateau.

3.3 The Southern Plateaus

Located south of the Central Highlands, these are vast, distinct blocks made of old, stable, hard crystalline rocks. They are significantly older than the central fold mountains.

  • Plateau of Arabia: A dry, steep desert sloping eastward towards the Persian Gulf.
  • Plateau of Peninsular India: Rises sharply from the Arabian Sea (Western Ghats) and slopes gently eastward. It is the source of major rivers like the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, and Kaveri.
  • Shan and Yunnan Plateaus: Highly dissected plateaus located in Myanmar and China, drained by the Salween, Mekong, and Si-kiang rivers.

3.4 The Great River Valleys

Formed by extensive alluvium deposits brought down by rivers originating in the snow-capped Central Highlands. These fertile plains are densely populated.

Important

These incredibly fertile river valleys have historically acted as the cradles of ancient civilisations, such as the Mesopotamian, Indus Valley, and Chinese civilisations.

  • Tigris-Euphrates Valley: Located in Iraq, also known as the Mesopotamian Lowlands. Both rivers drain into the Persian Gulf.
  • Indus Valley: Built by the River Indus and its five tributaries (Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej) across India and Pakistan.
  • Ganga-Brahmaputra Valley: Spans India and Bangladesh, creating the most fertile and largest delta globally.
  • Great Plains of China: Formed by eastward-draining rivers like the Huang He, Chang Jiang (Yangtze), and Si-kiang.
  • Manchurian Plains: Formed by the northward-flowing Amur River.

3.5 The Island Groups

Eastern and South-Eastern Asia features numerous island nations and groups (archipelagos) spanning the Indian and Pacific Oceans.

  • Major Islands: Sri Lanka, Bahrain, Taiwan, Japan, Philippines, and the Andaman & Nicobar Islands.
  • Indonesian Archipelago: The largest archipelago in the world, extending 5120 km. It contains over 13,677 islands (including Sumatra, Java, Borneo, Bali, and Sulawesi).
  • Geological Activity: Many of these islands are formed from submerged fold mountains or volcanic activity. They are part of the Fiery Ring of the Pacific, a highly active volcanic and seismic zone containing famous volcanoes like Mt. Fujiyama (Japan) and Mt. Mayon (Philippines).
