LIGHT

1. Introduction to Light

Concept

Light is a form of energy that affects our eyes to produce the sensation of vision. It makes objects visible to us.

There are two main types of sources of light:

  • Natural Sources: Found in nature, e.g., the Sun, stars, and fireflies. The Sun is the main natural source of light for Earth.
  • Artificial Sources: Man-made sources, e.g., electric bulbs, candles, torches, and kerosene lamps.

2. Luminous and Non-Luminous Bodies

Objects around us can be classified based on whether they emit their own light:

  • Luminous Bodies: The bodies which emit their own light are called luminous bodies. Examples include the Sun, stars, a burning candle, and a glowing electric bulb.
  • Non-Luminous Bodies: The bodies which do not emit light by their own, but become visible when light from a luminous body falls on them. Examples include the Moon, Earth, a chair, and a book.
Fact

The Moon is non-luminous. We see the moon shining at night because it reflects the sunlight that falls on its surface.

3. Mediums of Light Propagation

Depending upon the behavior of substances towards light, they are classified into three types:

  • Transparent Substance: A substance that allows light to pass through it easily. Objects can be seen distinctly across it. Examples: Glass, air, water.
  • Translucent Substance: A substance that allows only a part of the light to pass through it. Details of an object cannot be seen distinctly. Examples: Ground glass, tracing paper, greased paper.
  • Opaque Substance: A substance that does not allow any light to pass through it. We cannot see through them. Examples: Wood, metals, bricks.

4. Ray and Beam of Light

A straight line traveling in any one direction with an arrowhead is called a ray of light. A group of light rays given out from a source is called a beam of light.

Types of Light Beams

  • Parallel beam: Light rays traveling parallel to each other. Example: Light from a very far distance like the Sun.
  • Divergent beam: Light rays spreading out from a single point source. Example: Light from a bulb or torch.
  • Convergent beam: Light rays coming towards a single point to meet.

5. Rectilinear Propagation of Light

Concept

The property of light by which it travels in a straight line is called the Rectilinear Propagation of Light.

Experiment

If you look at a lighted candle through a straight hollow tube, the flame is visible. However, if the tube is bent, the flame is no longer visible. This proves that light travels in a straight line and cannot bend around corners.

6. Pinhole Camera

A pinhole camera is a simple application of the rectilinear propagation of light. It consists of a box with a small pinhole on one side and a tracing paper screen on the opposite side.

Characteristics of the Image Formed:

  • The image is real (it is formed on a screen).
  • The image is inverted (upside down).
  • The image is generally smaller in size than the object.
Important

Effect of Distance: If the distance between the pinhole and the screen increases, the size of the image increases. If the object is moved further away from the pinhole, the image size decreases.

7. Shadows

When an opaque object is placed in the path of a light source, it obstructs the passage of light and produces a dark patch on a screen behind the object. This dark patch is called a shadow.

Parts of a Shadow:

  • Umbra: The completely dark portion of the shadow where no light reaches from the source. It is the region of total darkness.
  • Penumbra: The partially dark portion of the shadow surrounding the umbra. It receives some light from the source.
Important

For a point source of light, only an umbra is formed. For an extended (larger) source of light, both an umbra and a penumbra are formed.

8. Eclipses

Eclipses are natural examples of the formation of shadows in space due to the rectilinear propagation of light.

Solar Eclipse

A Solar eclipse occurs on a new moon day when the Moon comes directly between the Sun and the Earth. The Moon casts its shadow on the Earth. It can be Total, Partial, or Annular.

Lunar Eclipse

A Lunar eclipse occurs on a full moon night when the Earth comes directly between the Sun and the Moon. The Earth casts its shadow on the Moon.
