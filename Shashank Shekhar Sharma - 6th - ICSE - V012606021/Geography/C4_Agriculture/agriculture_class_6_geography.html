Agriculture

1. Introduction to Agriculture

Agriculture is derived from Latin words where 'Ager' means field or soil and 'cultura' means cultivation. It is one of the oldest economic activities practiced by humans.

Concept

Agriculture refers to the practice of cultivating soil to grow crops and rearing animals like cattle, sheep, goats, pigs, horses, camels, chickens, and ducks.

Agriculture is essential because it provides food for human beings and fodder for animals. In addition, it supplies raw materials for industries such as the textile industry, sugar industry, and food processing industry. Despite living in the industrial age, more than half of the world's population remains directly or indirectly involved in agricultural activities.

2. Factors Influencing Agriculture

For the healthy growth and development of crops, specific conditions are necessary. The following factors highly influence agriculture:

  • Arable Land: This is the land on which crops can be grown. Lowlands, plains, river basins, and deltas are highly suitable. However, certain crops like tea and coffee prefer the slopes of hills.
  • Fertile Soil: Soil anchors the roots of the plants, providing nutrients and moisture. Without fertile soil, large amounts of artificial fertilizers must be added to maintain productivity.
  • Favourable Climatic Conditions: Rainfall, moisture, and temperature dictate what type of crops can grow in an area. Hot, wet tropical areas favor rice, while cooler, drier areas are better for wheat.
  • Other Factors: Availability of labour, manure, fertilizers, good quality seeds, proper irrigation facilities, machinery, and transport networks all dictate the success of farming in a region.

3. Types of Agricultural Practices

Based on climatic conditions, soil types, and water availability, different methods of farming are practiced worldwide:

A. Subsistence Farming

This type of farming is practiced primarily to fulfill the direct needs of the farmer and their family.

Fact

A large number of farmers in India practice subsistence farming. The plots are usually small, scattered, and cultivated using traditional tools rather than modern machinery.

Subsistence farming depends heavily upon rainfall, the natural fertility of the soil, and environmental conditions. It is further divided into two types:

  • Intensive Subsistence Farming: The farmer cultivates a small plot of land using simple tools and more manual labour. It aims for maximum output from a limited land area.
  • Shifting Farming: A patch of forest land is cleared by cutting trees and burning them. Crops are grown in this nutrient-rich ash. Once soil fertility decreases, the patch is abandoned, and the farmers move to a new forest area. This is also known as Slash and Burn agriculture.

B. Extensive Farming

In extensive farming, a small number of agricultural workers farm a large plot of land. It relies heavily on machinery and capital investment. Crop yield is very high, and the surplus is grown entirely for sale in the market.

C. Commercial Farming

In this system, crops are grown, and animals are reared specifically for the market to obtain high profit margins. It involves extensive usage of machinery, High Yielding Variety (HYV) seeds, and chemical fertilizers. Examples include Commercial Grain Farming and Mixed Farming.

D. Plantation Farming

A specialized type of commercial farming where a single cash crop is grown on a large estate (plantation). It requires vast capital, skilled management, and a huge labour force. The produce is processed on the farm itself or in nearby factories. Common plantation crops include Tea, Coffee, and Rubber.

4. Major Crops

Crops can be broadly classified based on their use:

Important

Food Crops are grown primarily for human consumption (e.g., Rice, Wheat, Maize), whereas Cash Crops are grown specifically for commercial purposes and to earn profit (e.g., Cotton, Jute, Sugarcane).

  • Major Food Crops:
    • Wheat: Requires moderate temperature and rainfall during growing, and bright sunshine at the time of harvest.
    • Rice: Needs high temperature, high humidity, and high rainfall. It grows best in clayey soils that retain water.
  • Major Cash Crops:
    • Cotton: Requires high temperature, light rainfall, and 210 frost-free days with bright sunshine. It grows well in black soil.
    • Jute: Also known as the 'Golden Fibre'. Grows well on alluvial soil and requires high temperatures and heavy rainfall.
    • Sugarcane: Requires hot and humid climate with a long growing season.
  • Beverage Crops: Tea and Coffee are major beverage crops grown on hill slopes with well-drained soils.

5. The Green Revolution

The Green Revolution refers to the massive increase in food grain production resulting from the introduction of high-yielding varieties (HYV) of seeds, chemical fertilizers, and modern agricultural techniques. It originated in the 1960s to solve food shortage issues worldwide.

Key Strategies of the Green Revolution:

  • Use of High Yielding Variety (HYV) seeds for greater output.
  • Application of chemical fertilizers and pesticides to protect crops.
  • Expansion of irrigation facilities like canals and tube wells.
  • Mechanization of farming using tractors, harvesters, and modern tools.
  • Provision of agricultural credit (loans) for farmers to buy inputs.

While the Green Revolution brought immense success in making countries self-sufficient in food production (especially India with wheat), it also led to long-term soil degradation due to the excessive use of harsh chemicals.
