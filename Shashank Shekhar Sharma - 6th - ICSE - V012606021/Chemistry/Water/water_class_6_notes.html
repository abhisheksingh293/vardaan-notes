WATER

1. Introduction

Water is the second most important substance for the existence of life on earth, right after air. It is absolutely essential for the sustenance of all living beings.

  • We require water for daily activities like bathing, washing clothes, cooking food, and watering plants.
  • Water is considered the liquid of life because no living organism can survive without it.

2. Water - A Compound

Historically, water was considered an element. However, in 1781, Henry Cavendish proved that water is a compound made up of two elements: hydrogen and oxygen.

Concept

Molecular Formula: The chemical formula of water is $H_2O$. This means one molecule of water is composed of two atoms of hydrogen and one atom of oxygen combined in a fixed proportion.

3. Occurrence and Sources of Water

Water is one of the most widely distributed substances on Earth, covering nearly 70% of the Earth's surface. It is also found in the atmosphere and in living organisms.

Fact

Water Distribution: Out of the total water available on Earth:

  • 97% is in oceans and seas.
  • 2% is frozen in the form of glaciers and polar ice caps.
  • Only about 1% is available as freshwater (rivers, lakes, groundwater) suitable for use by living beings.

Classification of Sources

  • Surface Water: Water flowing on the surface of the earth (Oceans, Seas, Rivers, and Lakes).
    • Sea Water: The largest source, but heavily saline (contains dissolved impurities and common salt), making it unfit for direct consumption.
    • River/Lake Water: Comes from rain and melting snow. Often contains suspended impurities and requires purification.
  • Underground Water: Rainwater that seeps through porous rocks and collects on non-porous rocks. Extracted via wells and handpumps. Usually pure and safe to drink.
  • Atmospheric Water: Water present as vapour, clouds, mist, and rain. Rainwater is considered the purest form of natural water.

4. The Three States of Water

Chemically, water is identical in all its physical forms. It exists in nature in three distinct states:

  1. Solid State: Ice, snow, frost, glaciers, and hail.
  2. Liquid State: Water in oceans, seas, rivers, lakes, ponds, and rain.
  3. Gaseous State: Water vapour or steam present in the air.
Important

Interconversion of States: Water changes its state upon heating or cooling. Under normal conditions, pure water freezes into ice at $0^\circ C$ and boils into steam at $100^\circ C$.

5. The Water Cycle

The continuous circulation of water from the Earth's surface to the atmosphere, and from the atmosphere back to the Earth's surface, is called the water cycle. It is crucial for restoring lost water and maintaining groundwater balance.

  • Evaporation: The heat of the Sun turns water from oceans and rivers into water vapour.
  • Condensation: Vapour rises, cools, and turns into tiny droplets to form clouds.
  • Precipitation: Droplets combine and fall back to earth as rain or snow.

Other Atmospheric Forms of Water

  • Fog: Water vapour condensing into tiny droplets near the ground.
  • Mist: Tiny droplets of water remaining suspended in the air.
  • Dew: Water vapour condensing into tiny droplets on cold objects (like grass).
  • Frost: Frozen dew.
  • Hail: Water drops freezing together in upper cold regions and falling as solid balls of ice.

6. Physical Properties of Water

  • Pure water is a transparent, colourless, odourless, and tasteless liquid.
  • The freezing point of pure water is $0^\circ C$ and its boiling point is $100^\circ C$.
  • The density of water is $1 \text{ g/cc}$ at exactly $4^\circ C$.

7. Water: A Universal Solvent

Water has the unique ability to dissolve a vast number of solid, liquid, and gaseous substances in it. Because of this high dissolving capacity, it is termed the universal solvent.

Concept

Solutions Terminology:

  • Solvent: A medium (usually liquid) in which a substance is dissolved. Water is the most common solvent.
  • Solute: The solid or substance that gets dissolved.
  • Solution: The homogeneous mixture formed when a solute dissolves entirely in a solvent.
