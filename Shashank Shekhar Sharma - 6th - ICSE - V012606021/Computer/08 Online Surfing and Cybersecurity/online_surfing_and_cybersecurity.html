ONLINE SURFING AND CYBERSECURITY

1. Introduction to the Internet

The Internet is a global network of millions of computers and computer networks all over the world. It allows us to access and share information seamlessly. The Internet contains billions of websites, which are located using a unique address called a Uniform Resource Locator (URL).

2. Internet Services

Internet services allow us to perform different types of operations and access various data formats like text, images, audio, and software over the network.

Communication & E-mail

Communication with other people has become much easier with the Internet. The most popular means is E-mail (Electronic mail), which is the process of exchanging messages electronically. It is considered a highly reliable and legal method of communication.

Video Conferencing

A Video conference is a live virtual meeting held between two or more geographically separated people. It requires a web camera, microphone, speakers, and high-speed Internet. It is widely used in businesses, education (online classes), and healthcare. Examples include Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, and Zoom.

Voice-over-Internet Protocol (VoIP)

VoIP is a technology that provides a calling facility over the Internet instead of using a traditional phone line. It is primarily used for voice communications, transferring voice, fax, and SMS content.

Fact

The first VoIP service was developed in 1973 by Danny Cohen. He named this service the Network Voice Protocol (NVP).

  • Advantages: Low cost of operations, reduced infrastructure cost, available on various devices, generally free VoIP-to-VoIP calls, and clearer voice quality.
  • Disadvantages: Loss of data, less reliable, delays due to Internet traffic, requires a continuous power supply and strong connection, and may lack connectivity in rural areas.

Chatting & Social Networking

  • Chatting: Refers to communication in the form of small messages among computers over the Internet in real-time. Popular chat programs include WhatsApp, Telegram, and Discord.
  • Social Networking: Platforms where people with common interests can connect, share views, ideas, photos, and videos. Examples include Facebook, Instagram, and X.
  • Newsgroup: A discussion group focusing on a particular topic in an online community, managed by a News Admin.
  • E-Greetings: Digital greeting cards containing digital text, animations, and music sent via email.

E-Banking and Cloud Storage

  • E-Banking: Also known as online banking. It is a digital service that allows customers to manage finances online, transfer funds, and pay bills. It reduces transaction costs, minimizes human error, and provides 24x7 access.
  • Cloud Storage: A service where users can store and manage data on a remote server instead of their local computer. This allows access from any device anywhere. Examples: Google Drive, Dropbox, and OneDrive.

3. Netiquettes

To avoid misunderstandings in online communication, a set of rules has been designed. These are known as Netiquettes.

Concept

Netiquette (derived from Network Etiquettes) describes the rules of conduct and manners expected from a user during online communication.

  • The subject of an email must be a short summary of its contents.
  • Signatures should be short and simple.
  • Avoid typing in ALL CAPITAL LETTERS, as it is considered equivalent to shouting.
  • Use the Bcc (Blind Carbon Copy) field to hide email addresses and respect privacy.
  • Avoid using the "Reply to All" option when it is not necessary.
  • Try to overlook minor typing mistakes made by the sender.

4. Cyber Threats

A Cyber threat is a criminal activity in which computers are used to damage data, steal data, or disrupt the digital life of a person for personal profit or to demonstrate malicious skills.

  • Data Diddling: The process of changing data going into or out of a computer (e.g., a student hacking a system to change their grades).
  • Phreaking: Attacking telephone companies by using computers to make free calls billed to another owner, or tapping phone lines.
  • Carding: The process of stealing credit card numbers online to be resold or used to make unauthorized purchases.
  • Hacking: Modifying computer hardware or software to accomplish a goal outside its original purpose. Some companies legally employ hackers to find flaws in their own security systems.
  • Cracking: Stealing personal information, changing financial data, and breaking security systems to gain unauthorized access. Software cracking leads to pirated software.

5. Cybersecurity & Safety Measures

Cybersecurity is the process of protecting computer resources such as networks, devices, programs, and data from unauthorized access, damage, or attack. The rise in cybercrimes is often due to untrained users, lack of awareness, and software loopholes.

Important

Safety Measures While Surfing:

  • Never provide personal information online (address, phone number, school).
  • Keep your browser's privacy settings turned ON.
  • Do not share passwords with anyone other than your parents or teachers.
  • Never meet with anyone you met online without parental supervision.
  • Don't open email attachments from unknown sources or respond to messages from strangers.
  • Use strong, unique passwords for your accounts.
