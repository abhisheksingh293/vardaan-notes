E-MAIL: AN INTRODUCTION

1. Introduction to E-Mail

The Internet provides many communication services, and one of the most remarkable ones that resembles the traditional postal system is the E-mail service.

Concept

An Electronic mail (E-mail) is defined as the process of exchanging messages electronically through a communication network using a computer.

2. Features of E-mail

E-mail has transformed how we communicate by offering powerful features, including:

  • Attachments: Ability to attach files along with the text message.
  • Storage: Ability to store information such as past messages and contact lists.
  • Organization: Ability to organize inbox messages into folders and subfolders.
  • Search: Ability to quickly locate specific emails using keywords, sender name, or dates.
  • Security: Ability to filter out spam and junk emails.
Fact

E-mail can send MIME (Multipurpose Internet Mail Extension) type files. MIME was invented by Bell Communications in 1991 to expand email capabilities, allowing the transfer of audio, video, images, and application programs as attachments.

3. Advantages of Using E-Mail

E-mail holds significant advantages over traditional mail:

  • Global Reach: An e-mail can be sent anytime and from anywhere in the world.
  • Mass Communication: It can be sent to multiple people at the same time.
  • Speed: Sending an e-mail is incredibly fast compared to traditional mail.
  • Reliability: If an e-mail is not delivered on the first attempt, the mail server automatically tries again. If it completely fails, the sender receives an error notification explaining why.
  • Cost-Effective: Sending emails is typically free.
  • Record Keeping: Emails create a written record of communication.
Important

Although typically associated with the Internet, it is not always necessary to have an Internet connection to send and receive e-mails. Many organizations have internal e-mail facilities that operate strictly within their own local networks.

4. Understanding E-Mail Addresses

To send or receive an e-mail, you must have an E-mail address. This address is unique for every user. An e-mail address consists of two main parts separated by the @ (at) symbol:

  • Username: The portion before the '@' symbol (e.g., orangeeducation123). This is unique to the individual.
  • Domain Name: The portion after the '@' symbol (e.g., gmail.com). This represents the server or network providing the email service.
Fact

Ray Tomlinson was the first person to send an e-mail across a network. He is also the one who introduced the use of the "@" sign to separate the user's name from the domain name.

5. Standard E-Mail Folders

When you log into an e-mail service provider (like Gmail, Yahoo, or Outlook), you will see several standard folders used to manage messages:

  • Inbox: The main folder where all incoming, received e-mails are stored.
  • Sent: The folder where copies of e-mails you have successfully sent to others are kept.
  • Outbox: A temporary holding folder. E-mails stay here while they are in the process of being sent. Once sent, the Outbox becomes empty.
  • Spam: The folder where unwanted, junk, or suspicious e-mails are automatically stored to keep your Inbox safe and clean.
  • Trash: The folder where deleted e-mails are temporarily stored before being permanently erased.

6. Composing and Sending an E-Mail

To write a new e-mail, you click on the Compose button. The "New Message" window will open, containing several important fields:

  • To: Type the primary receiver's e-mail address here.
  • Subject: A brief introduction about the purpose of the e-mail.
  • Message Body: The main large white box where you type your actual message.
Concept

Understanding Cc and Bcc

  • Cc (Carbon Copy): Used when you want to send a copy of the e-mail to someone for their information. Everyone in the 'To' and 'Cc' fields can see each other's email addresses.
  • Bcc (Blind Carbon Copy): Used when you want to send a copy to someone, but you want to hide their email address from all other recipients.
Important

You can attach files using the Attach files button (usually a paperclip icon). However, if your attachments exceed 25 MB, Gmail will not send them directly. Instead, it will upload them to Google Drive and send a download link to the recipient.

7. Emoticons and Acronyms

Because e-mail is digital and you aren't face-to-face with the receiver, two tools are commonly used to save typing time and convey tone:

Emoticons

The word emoticon is an abbreviation for emotion icon. They represent facial expressions and help convey your mood (e.g., Happy, Sad, Surprised).

Fact

The first two emoticons used were :-) for a smiling face and :-( for a sad face, introduced by Scott Fahlman.

Acronyms

An Acronym is a word formed from the initial letters of a multi-word phrase. They work like shorthand to save typing time. Common examples include:

  • ASAP: As Soon As Possible
  • BTW: By The Way
  • FAQ: Frequently Asked Questions
  • LOL: Laughing Out Loud
