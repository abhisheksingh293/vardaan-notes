Health and Diseases

1. Introduction to Health

Keeping ourselves fit and healthy involves much more than simply not falling ill. It is a state of complete harmony encompassing our physical body, our mental state, and our interactions with society.

Concept

Definition of Health

According to the World Health Organization (WHO), health is defined as a state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being, and not merely the absence of disease.

Similarly, according to Ayurveda, true health is a balance of body, mind, and surroundings. It allows a person to perform tasks efficiently, cope with stressful situations, and adjust well with peers and society.

Ways to Keep in Health

  • Eat a balanced diet: Consume meals that contain all nutrients and adequate roughage and water.
  • Maintain personal hygiene: By bathing daily, washing hands properly, and keeping surroundings clean.
  • Exercise regularly: Includes outdoor games and physical activities to keep the body healthy.
  • Get enough rest: Skipping meals and not getting adequate sleep late at night can cause diseases. Meditate and relax on a regular basis.

2. Understanding Diseases

When we are unwell, a specific organ or system in our body may not be working as it should. A disease is a condition that affects the normal working of the body and mind.

Some diseases last for a short time and are called acute diseases, while others continue for a long time and are called chronic diseases.

Concept

What is a Pathogen?

A pathogen is a disease-causing microorganism. These include harmful bacteria, viruses, fungi, worms, or protozoa that invade the body and disrupt normal functions.

Categories of Diseases

Based on their causes and mode of spread, diseases can be broadly grouped into two main types:

A. Communicable Diseases

These are diseases that are caused by pathogens and can spread from one person to another. Examples include Typhoid, COVID-19, flu, dengue, and chickenpox.

Modes of Transmission:

  • Direct Transmission: Direct skin-to-skin contact, or mucus-to-mucus contact. Examples: Leprosy and eczema.
  • Indirect Transmission (Droplet): An infected person releases droplets carrying the disease-causing microorganisms while coughing or sneezing. Examples: Tuberculosis, whooping cough.
  • Infected needle or syringe: Infections transmitted through blood transfusion (e.g., AIDS).
  • Through Vectors (Insects): Mosquitoes can act as vectors for many viruses and parasites.
    • Malaria: Caused by the Plasmodium protozoan, spread by the female Anopheles mosquito.
    • Dengue: Caused by the Dengue virus, spread by the Aedes aegypti mosquito.

B. Non-Communicable Diseases (NCDs)

These diseases are not caused by pathogens and do not spread from one person to another. They are often linked to poor lifestyle, unhealthy diet, lack of physical exercise, or environmental factors.

  • Obesity: Caused by excess body weight and lack of physical exercise. Regular exercise and portion control help manage it.
  • Diabetes: Linked to hormonal imbalance and unhealthy eating habits. Symptoms include frequent urination, excessive thirst, and fatigue.
  • High Blood Pressure: Causes headaches and dizziness. Managing stress and reducing salt intake is crucial.
  • Heart Disease: Causes chest pain and irregular heartbeat. A low-fat, low-cholesterol diet is highly recommended.

3. Common Diseases and Their Causes

Diseases spread through the Air

Disease Causal Agent Symptoms
Common Cold & Influenza Viruses (Rhinovirus, Influenza virus) Runny or blocked nose, sneezing, mild fever, headache.
Chickenpox Varicella-zoster virus Itchy skin rash with red spots, fever, tiredness.
Tuberculosis (TB) Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Bacteria) Persistent cough, chest pain, blood in sputum, weight loss.
COVID-19 SARS-CoV-2 virus Fever, cough, loss of taste or smell, breathing difficulty.

Disease Causal Agent Symptoms
Hepatitis A Hepatitis A virus Fever, fatigue, yellowing of skin/eyes (Jaundice), loss of appetite.

4. Prevention and Control

Immunity and Vaccines

Immunity is the natural ability of our body to fight diseases. This immunity can be boosted by introducing dead or weakened microorganisms in the form of vaccines into the body.

The body reacts to the vaccine by producing antibodies that fight against the specific disease and develop lifelong protection. This act of introducing a vaccine is called Vaccination.

Extraordinary Mind

Edward Jenner discovered the first vaccine for smallpox. He observed that milkmaids who got cowpox (a milder disease) did not get smallpox, leading to the invention of vaccines.

Antibiotics

If our immune system is weak, doctors prescribe antibiotics. These are medicines that kill or stop the growth of disease-causing microorganisms (primarily bacteria).

Extraordinary Mind

Alexander Fleming discovered penicillin in 1928, which was the first antibiotic used to treat bacterial infections. He discovered it by chance while studying harmful bacteria on a discarded Petri dish where mould had stopped bacteria from growing.

Important

Antibiotic Resistance

While antibiotics are effective, their indiscriminate and excessive use has led to Antibiotic Resistance. This is a dangerous phenomenon where bacteria that were earlier killed by a given antibiotic mutate and survive. This makes it much harder to treat common infections in the future.

Community-led Campaigns

Prevention is always better than cure. Public hygiene plays a massive role in fighting both communicable and non-communicable diseases.

Fact File

Odisha – Community-led campaign: A community sanitation campaign in the Bhadrak district of Odisha helped people build and use toilets. This significantly helped reduce the cases of infections and diarrhoea, proving the importance of public sanitation efforts.
