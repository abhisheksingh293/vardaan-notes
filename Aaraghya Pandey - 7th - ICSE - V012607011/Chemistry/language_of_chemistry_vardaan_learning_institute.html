LANGUAGE OF CHEMISTRY

1. Introduction to Chemical Reactions

Concept

A chemical reaction is a process in which one or more substances undergo a chemical change to produce new substances with entirely different properties.

  • Reactants: The starting materials or substances that take part in a chemical reaction. They are written on the left-hand side of a chemical equation.
  • Products: The new substances formed as a result of the chemical reaction. They are written on the right-hand side of a chemical equation.
Fact

Example: When a magnesium ribbon burns in the presence of oxygen, it forms magnesium oxide.
Magnesium + Oxygen → Magnesium Oxide
Here, Magnesium and Oxygen are the reactants, and Magnesium Oxide is the product.

2. Characteristics of Chemical Reactions

The occurrence of a chemical reaction can be identified by observing specific changes. These characteristics include:

a) Change in Physical State

The physical state of the products may differ from that of the reactants. A reaction can result in a change from solid (s), liquid (l), gas (g), or aqueous (aq) states.

b) Evolution of a Gas

Many chemical reactions are accompanied by the release of a gas, often observed as bubbles or effervescence.

Example: When dilute hydrochloric acid reacts with zinc granules, hydrogen gas is evolved rapidly.

c) Formation of a Precipitate

A precipitate is an insoluble solid substance that separates from a solution during a chemical reaction. It is typically denoted by a downward-pointing arrow (↓).

Example: When silver nitrate solution is mixed with sodium chloride solution, a white precipitate of silver chloride is formed.

d) Evolution or Absorption of Heat

Chemical reactions often involve energy changes, usually in the form of heat.

  • Exothermic Reactions: Reactions in which heat is given out or evolved to the surroundings.
  • Endothermic Reactions: Reactions in which heat is absorbed from the surroundings.

e) Change in Colour

Some chemical reactions are characterised by a visible change in colour.

3. Chemical Equations

A chemical equation is the symbolic representation of a chemical reaction using the symbols and formulae of the substances involved.

Important

Law of Conservation of Mass: Matter can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction. Therefore, a chemical equation must always be balanced. The number of atoms of each element on the reactant side must equal the number of atoms on the product side.

Steps to Balance a Chemical Equation:

  • Write the skeletal (unbalanced) equation.
  • Count the number of atoms of each element on both the left-hand side (reactants) and right-hand side (products).
  • Use coefficients (whole numbers placed in front of formulas) to equalize the number of atoms of each element on both sides.
  • Never change the subscripts within a chemical formula to balance an equation.

Example of Balancing:
Unbalanced: H₂ + O₂ → H₂O
Balanced: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

4. Conditions Affecting Chemical Reactions

Certain conditions are often necessary for a chemical reaction to occur or to proceed at a faster rate:

  • Close Contact: Reactants must be in physical contact and properly mixed.
  • Solution Form: Some substances react only when they are dissolved in water (aqueous solution).
  • Heat: Supplying heat energy is often required to initiate a reaction.
  • Light: Photochemical reactions occur only in the presence of light.
  • Pressure: Specific pressure conditions are required for certain reactions, particularly those involving gases.
  • Catalyst: A substance that changes the rate of a chemical reaction without being consumed or permanently changed itself. A positive catalyst increases the rate, while a negative catalyst decreases it.
