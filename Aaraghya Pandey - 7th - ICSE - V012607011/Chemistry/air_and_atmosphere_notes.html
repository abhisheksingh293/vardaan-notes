AIR AND ATMOSPHERE

1. Introduction: Air is a Mixture

Concept

Air is not a single chemical element or compound. It is a homogeneous mixture of various gases present in the Earth's atmosphere, primarily consisting of nitrogen and oxygen, along with small traces of other gases, water vapour, and dust particles.

2. Composition of Air

Through various experiments (such as those conducted by Lavoisier), scientists deduced that air consists of an active component and an inactive component.

Fact

The composition of air is roughly:
• Nitrogen (Inactive component) makes up about 78% of the air by volume.
• Oxygen (Active component) makes up about 21% of the air.
• The remaining 1% includes Noble Gases (like Argon), Carbon Dioxide (about 0.03%), Water Vapour, and Dust particles.

3. Major Components and Their Properties

a) Nitrogen Gas

  • Properties: It is a colourless, odourless, and generally inactive gas. It neither burns nor supports combustion.
  • Uses:
    • It is essential for plant growth and is a major component of fertilizers.
    • Because it is unreactive, it is used in food packaging (like potato chips) to preserve freshness and prevent spoiling.

b) Oxygen Gas

  • Properties: It is a colourless and odourless gas. It is slightly heavier than air and is slightly soluble in water, which allows aquatic life to breathe.
  • Chemical Properties: Oxygen is a strong supporter of combustion (burning). It reacts with most metals to form basic metallic oxides and with non-metals to form acidic oxides.
Important

Rusting: When iron is exposed to both oxygen and moisture over time, it reacts to form a reddish-brown, flaky layer known as rust (iron oxide). This process weakens the metal.

c) Carbon Dioxide Gas

  • Properties: It is a colourless, odourless gas that is heavier than air. It does not support combustion.
  • Uses:
    • Vital for plants during the process of photosynthesis to make food.
    • Because it extinguishes fire and is heavy, it is widely used in fire extinguishers.
    • Used in aerated (fizzy) drinks.

d) Noble Gases

Gases such as Helium, Neon, and Argon are collectively known as noble gases or inert gases. Their main property is that they are completely unreactive under normal conditions.

4. Other Components in the Atmosphere

  • Water Vapour: The amount of water vapour in the air varies depending on the location and climate. It plays a critical role in the water cycle (evaporation, condensation, precipitation).
  • Dust and Smoke: Fine solid particles and unburnt carbon particles are suspended in the air. High amounts of these lead to poor air quality and respiratory issues.

5. Air Purity, Pollution, and Acid Rain

Concept

Air Pollution is defined as the contamination of the atmosphere by harmful substances (pollutants) such as excess carbon dioxide, carbon monoxide, sulphur dioxide, and nitrogen oxides. These pollutants primarily arise from burning fossil fuels in vehicles and industries.

One of the most dangerous effects of air pollution is the occurrence of Acid Rain.

  • When oxides of sulphur (SO₂) and nitrogen (NO₂) are released into the air by factories and vehicles, they mix with rainwater in the clouds.
  • This chemical reaction forms dangerous acids, specifically sulphuric acid and nitric acid.
  • Effects: When this acid rain falls to the ground, it damages crops, changes soil acidity, harms aquatic life in rivers and lakes, and corrodes marble and stone monuments (like the Taj Mahal).
