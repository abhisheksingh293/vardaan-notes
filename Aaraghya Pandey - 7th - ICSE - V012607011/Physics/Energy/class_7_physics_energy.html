Energy

1. The Concept of Work

In common language, any physical or mental effort is considered work. However, in science, work has a highly specific meaning. A person standing with a heavy load or pushing against an immovable wall does no work scientifically.

Concept

Work is said to be done only when a force acting on an object produces a displacement in the object in the direction of the force.

Essential Conditions for Work

  • A force should act on the object.
  • The object must be displaced (it must move in the direction of the force or opposite to it). If there is no displacement, no work is done.

Mathematical Formula and Unit

Work done is equal to the product of the force and the displacement of the object.

  • Work = Force × Displacement
  • W = F × s

The SI unit of work is the joule (J). Work is a scalar quantity.

Fact

1 Joule of work is done if a force of 1 Newton causes a displacement of 1 Metre in the direction of the applied force.

2. Understanding Energy

The term 'energy' is deeply associated with work. When we do work, we consume our energy. No work can be done without energy.

Concept

Energy is defined as the capacity to do work. The energy of an object is measured by the total amount of work that the object can do.

Work-Energy Relationship

There is a direct relationship between work and energy. The object that does the work loses energy, while the object on which the work is done gains energy.

Units of Energy

Because energy is the capacity to do work, it is measured in the same SI unit as work: the joule (J).

  • Calorie (cal): The amount of heat energy required to raise the temperature of 1 gram of water by 1°C.
  • Kilocalorie (kcal): A larger unit of heat energy. 1 kcal = 1000 calories.
Important

1 calorie = 4.186 joules (approximately 4.2 J).

3. Various Forms of Energy

Energy exists in our environment in several different forms.

Mechanical Energy

The energy stored in an object on account of its position, configuration, or state of motion is called mechanical energy. It exists in two main types:

  • Potential Energy (P.E.): The energy stored in an object due to its rest position or special configuration.
    • Gravitational Potential Energy: Possessed due to height (e.g., water stored in an overhead tank). Formula: P.E. = mgh
    • Elastic Potential Energy: Possessed due to a change in shape or configuration (e.g., a stretched rubber catapult, a compressed spring).
  • Kinetic Energy (K.E.): The energy possessed by an object by virtue of its motion (e.g., a flying bird, moving car). Formula: K.E. = ½ mv².
Important

The Kinetic Energy of an object depends on two factors: the mass of the object and the speed of the object. A heavier object moving at a high speed possesses immense kinetic energy.

Other Important Forms of Energy

  • Chemical Energy: The energy stored in the chemical composition of a matter. It becomes available during chemical reactions (e.g., coal, wood, batteries, food).
  • Heat (Thermal) Energy: Energy utilized to keep us warm, cook food, and run automobile engines.
  • Light Energy: The form of energy that enables us to see objects. The Sun is the ultimate source.
  • Electrical Energy: Produced by electric cells, batteries, and generators. Used to run household appliances and heavy machinery.
  • Magnetic Energy: Energy possessed by a magnet. Used in electromagnets, electric motors, and microphones.
  • Sound Energy: Produced when an object vibrates (moves to and fro).
  • Nuclear Energy: The massive energy released due to the loss in mass when a heavy nucleus (like Uranium) splits.
  • Solar Energy: Energy emitted continuously by the Sun in the form of light and heat radiations.
  • Muscular Energy: The energy utilized by living organisms (humans and animals) to perform physical tasks.

4. Interconversion of Energy

Under suitable conditions, one form of energy can be converted into another form. This process is called the transformation or interconversion of energy.

  • Hydroelectric Plants: Potential Energy of stored water ➔ Kinetic Energy ➔ Electrical Energy.
  • Electric Motor / Fan: Electrical Energy ➔ Mechanical Energy (Kinetic).
  • Microphone: Sound Energy ➔ Electrical Energy.
  • Dry Cell Battery: Chemical Energy ➔ Electrical Energy.
  • Electric Bulb / LED: Electrical Energy ➔ Light and Heat Energy.
  • Solar Cell: Solar Energy ➔ Electrical Energy.
  • Automobile Engine: Chemical Energy of fuel ➔ Mechanical Energy.
Fact

During photosynthesis, green plants transform sunlight (Solar Energy) into food (Chemical Energy). The Sun is considered the ultimate source of energy for all living things.

5. Law of Conservation of Energy

The interconversion of energy is governed by a universal law.

Concept

Law of Conservation of Energy: Energy can neither be created nor destroyed. It can only be transformed from one form to another. The total sum of energy in an isolated system remains unchanged.

Applications of the Conservation Law

  • Motion of a Roller Coaster: At the top of a slope, the car has maximum potential energy and zero kinetic energy. As it moves down, potential energy transforms into kinetic energy, speeding up the car. The total mechanical energy remains constant.
  • Oscillating Pendulum: At the extreme highest points of the swing, the bob has maximum potential energy and zero kinetic energy. At the lowest center point, it has maximum kinetic energy and zero potential energy. Over time, air friction eventually converts this mechanical energy into heat energy, stopping the pendulum, but total energy remains conserved.
  • Thermal Power Plant: Chemical energy (coal) ➔ Heat energy (steam) ➔ Kinetic energy (turbine) ➔ Electrical energy (generator).
