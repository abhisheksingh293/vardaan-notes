THE CONSTITUTION OF INDIA

1. The Foundation of India's Governance

The Constitution of India serves as a rule book for the country, outlining how it should be governed and safeguarding the rights of all citizens. It defines the structure of the government, the rights of the people, and the duties of citizens, ensuring justice, liberty, equality, and fraternity for all.

Concept

Constitution: A set of basic rules and regulations according to which a country is governed. It is the supreme law of the land.

2. The Constituent Assembly

Before independence, India was under British rule. The demand for a Constitution formed by Indians was a key part of the freedom struggle. The body created to draft this Constitution was called the Constituent Assembly.

  • It was formed in 1946 based on the Cabinet Mission Plan.
  • It consisted of 389 members, later reduced to 299 after partition.
  • Key members included Dr. Rajendra Prasad, Jawaharlal Nehru, Vallabhbhai Patel, Maulana Abul Kalam Azad, and Sarojini Naidu.
  • Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected as the President of the Constituent Assembly.
Fact

The Constitution of India was adopted on November 26, 1949, but it came into full effect on January 26, 1950. This day is celebrated as Republic Day.

Role of Dr. B.R. Ambedkar

The drafting of the Constitution was an immense task. A Drafting Committee was set up on August 29, 1947, entrusted with this responsibility.

  • Dr. B.R. Ambedkar was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee.
  • Due to his pivotal role in framing the Constitution, he is widely regarded as the "Father of the Indian Constitution".
  • He gave our country a sovereign, democratic, and republic constitution based on universal adult franchise.

3. The Preamble

Our Constitution begins with a short introduction called the Preamble. It highlights the fundamental values and philosophy on which the Constitution is based.

Important

The Preamble is often referred to as the "Soul of the Constitution" as it provides the key to understanding the intent of the framers.

Key Ideals of the Preamble

  • Sovereign: India is an independent country, free from external control.
  • Socialist: Aims to achieve social and economic equality, reducing poverty and discrimination. (Added by the 42nd Amendment in 1976).
  • Secular: The state protects all religions equally and does not uphold any religion as the state religion. (Added by the 42nd Amendment in 1976).
  • Democratic: The government is chosen by the people, for the people. People have the power to elect their leaders.
  • Republic: The head of the state (the President) is elected, not hereditary (like a king or queen).

Objectives of the Constitution

  • Justice: Ensures political, social, and economic justice for all citizens, removing discrimination.
  • Liberty: Provides freedom of thought, expression, belief, faith, and worship.
  • Equality: Guarantees equal status and opportunities to everyone, regardless of background.
  • Fraternity: Promotes a feeling of brotherhood and unity among all Indians.

4. Key Features of Our Constitution

Federal System with Unitary Bias

India has a federal system, meaning power is divided between the Central Government and the State Governments. However, the Central government is more powerful, especially during emergencies, giving it a unitary bias.

Parliamentary Form of Government

India follows a parliamentary system where the executive (Council of Ministers) is drawn from and responsible to the legislature (Parliament). The President is the nominal head, while the Prime Minister is the real head of the government.

Universal Adult Franchise

This principle grants the right to vote to all adult citizens of India, regardless of their caste, religion, sex, literacy, or wealth. Currently, the voting age is 18 years.

Concept

Single Citizenship: Unlike some federal countries (like the USA), India provides only one citizenship—Indian citizenship—to all its people, irrespective of the state they live in.

Independent Judiciary

The Constitution establishes an independent judiciary system to ensure justice. The Supreme Court is the highest court in India, followed by High Courts in states and lower courts at the district level. It acts as the guardian of the Constitution.

5. Fundamental Rights and Duties

Fundamental Rights

The Constitution guarantees six basic rights to all its citizens, essential for their well-being and development. These rights cannot be taken away by any law and help maintain the democratic nature of our government. If these rights are violated, citizens can approach the courts.

Fundamental Duties

Along with rights, the Constitution also expects citizens to perform certain duties towards the country. These were added by the 42nd Amendment Act in 1976. There are 11 Fundamental Duties, such as respecting the National Flag and Anthem, protecting the environment, and promoting harmony.

Fact

The Constitution of India is the longest written constitution in the world. Originally, it had 395 Articles, 22 Parts, and 8 Schedules. It has been amended over 100 times to adapt to changing needs.
