ATMOSPHERE

1. Introduction to the Atmosphere

The land and water are significant components for the survival of life on earth, but we cannot live in the absence of air. Air is a mixture of various gases that surround the earth. The thick layer of gases surrounding the earth is known as the Atmosphere. It is held to the earth by the gravitational force.

Concept

Biosphere: Derived from the Greek word 'bios' which means 'life'. It refers to the part of the earth where land, water, and air interact, allowing life to exist. The atmosphere is a vital component of the biosphere.

2. Composition of the Atmosphere

The atmosphere is made up of various gases, water vapour, and dust particles. The composition of clean, dry air in the lower atmosphere is relatively constant.

  • Nitrogen (78.08%): The most abundant gas. It is essential for plant growth and dilutes oxygen to prevent rapid burning.
  • Oxygen (20.95%): The life-supporting gas necessary for respiration by humans and animals. It is also essential for combustion (burning).
  • Carbon Dioxide (0.03%): The heaviest gas. It is essential for plants to prepare their food through photosynthesis. It also absorbs heat, keeping the earth warm.
  • Argon (0.93%): An inert gas present in small quantities.
Fact

Ozone (O3) is a highly reactive form of oxygen present in the atmosphere in very small amounts (0.00006%). However, it plays a critical role by absorbing harmful ultraviolet (UV) rays from the sun.

3. Structure of the Atmosphere

Based on temperature variations, the atmosphere is divided into five distinct layers.

I. Troposphere

The term 'tropos' means 'change'. This is the lowest and most important layer of the atmosphere. It extends to an average height of 13 km.

  • All weather phenomena like rain, fog, and hailstorm take place here due to the presence of dust particles and water vapour.
  • Temperature generally decreases with altitude at the rate of 1°C for every 165 meters (known as the Normal Lapse Rate).

II. Stratosphere

This layer lies above the troposphere and extends up to 50 km in height.

  • It is almost free from clouds and weather phenomena, making it ideal for flying aeroplanes.
  • Contains the Ozone Layer, which protects the living forms on earth from the sun's destructive ultraviolet rays.

III. Mesosphere

This thermal layer lies above the stratosphere and extends from 50 km to 80 km. It is the coldest layer of the atmosphere, where temperatures can drop to -90°C.

  • Meteorites burn up in this layer when they enter the earth's atmosphere from space, protecting us from falling debris.

IV. Thermosphere (Ionosphere)

Extends from 80 km to 400 km. In this layer, temperature rises rapidly with height.

  • The chief characteristic of this sphere is the presence of electrically charged particles known as ions.
  • These charged particles reflect outgoing radio waves back to the earth, making wireless communication possible.

V. Exosphere

This is the uppermost layer of the atmosphere, which gradually merges into space. It has extremely thin air containing very light gases like helium and hydrogen.

4. The Greenhouse Effect and Global Warming

The atmosphere acts as a protective blanket. Gases such as carbon dioxide trap the heat of the sun, preventing it from escaping back into space. This natural heating up of the earth is known as the Greenhouse Effect, and the gases that cause it are called greenhouse gases.

Important

Global Warming: When the amount of greenhouse gases increases abnormally due to pollution, the earth gets dangerously warm. This causes polar glaciers to melt, resulting in a rise in sea levels which can flood coastal areas.

Ways to Reduce Global Warming

  • Planting more trees to improve oxygen levels and decrease carbon dioxide in the atmosphere.
  • Using non-conventional fuels (like CNG) and renewable energy (solar, wind) to reduce emissions.
  • Using energy-efficient appliances like Compact Fluorescent Lights (CFLs).
  • Practicing carpooling and using public transport to reduce vehicular smoke emissions.
