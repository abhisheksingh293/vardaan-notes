Europe: Political Division and Physical Features

1. Location and Boundaries

Europe is the world's second-smallest continent in area, after Australia. It is surrounded by water on three sides, making it a "Peninsula of Peninsulas".

  • North: Bordered by the Arctic Ocean.
  • West: Bordered by the Atlantic Ocean.
  • South: Bordered by the Mediterranean Sea.
  • East: Separated from Asia by the Ural Mountains, the Ural River, the Caspian Sea, the Caucasus Mountains, and the Black Sea.
  • South-West: Separated from Africa by the Strait of Gibraltar.
Important

Latitudinal and Longitudinal Extent:

  • Latitudes: $35^\circ \text{N}$ to $71^\circ \text{N}$ (Mostly in the Northern temperate zone).
  • Longitudes: $25^\circ \text{W}$ to $65^\circ \text{E}$.
  • The Prime Meridian ($0^\circ$ longitude) passes through Greenwich, near London, in Europe.

2. Key Facts and Discoveries

It is believed that the name 'Europe' is derived from 'Europa' (a figure in Greek mythology). Europe has been the birthplace of western culture, with ancient Greeks and Romans heavily contributing to philosophy, literature, and science.

Fact

Continental Extremes:

  • Largest Country: Russia (which is also the largest in the world).
  • Smallest Country: Vatican City.
  • Highest Point: Mt. Elbrus ($5,642 \text{ m}$ above sea level, located in the Caucasus Mountains).
  • Lowest Point: Russian shores of the Caspian Sea ($28 \text{ m}$ below sea level).
  • Longest River: The Volga ($3,692 \text{ km}$).
  • Largest Lake: Lake Ladoga.
Concept

Eurasia: Because Europe and Asia share a continuous landmass separated only by natural features like the Ural mountains, the two continents together are often referred to as Eurasia (Europe + Asia).

3. Political Divisions

New Europe consists of 50 countries. Countries are often grouped based on their location, physiography, and cultural ties:

  • Scandinavian Countries: Norway, Sweden, Iceland, and Denmark. (Denmark is included due to historical and linguistic relations).
  • Baltic Countries: Lithuania, Latvia, and Estonia. They lie on the banks of the Baltic Sea and originated after the collapse of the USSR in 1991.
  • Lowland Countries (Benelux): Belgium, the Netherlands, and Luxembourg. A large part of the Netherlands lies below sea level (Nether means "low"). The Netherlands is also called Holland.
  • Balkan Peninsula: Includes Albania, Bulgaria, Kosovo, Greece, Macedonia, Montenegro, and others.
  • Iberian Peninsula: Consists of Spain and Portugal.
  • British Isles: Includes the United Kingdom (UK) and Ireland. The English Channel separates the mainland of Europe from the British Isles.

4. Physical Features

Europe boasts a vast variety of landforms and is divided into four major physical divisions:

I. The North-Western Highlands

Extends from Norway and Sweden to the highlands of Scotland, Wales, and Ireland. Also known as the Fennoscandian Shield.

  • These are the oldest mountains in Europe. Their height is decreasing due to weathering and glacial erosion.
  • The region contains the Kjolen Mountains.
  • These highlands are hard and rocky, making them less fertile for agriculture but very rich in minerals like copper and aluminum.
Concept

Fiord Coastline: Norway is famous for its fiords. Fiords are coastlines formed due to the submergence of glacial valleys when the sea level rose following the Ice Age, creating deep, narrow, and rugged water inlets.

II. The Great European Plains

Vast, flat, and gently rolling plains extending from the Atlantic Coast in the west to the Ural Mountains in the east.

  • They are narrow in the west and become broader towards the east (widest in Russia).
  • Part of the world's most fertile plains, known as the Steppes, making them highly suitable for agriculture due to loess soil (glacial deposits).
  • Rich in mineral deposits such as coal and iron ore.
  • Major rivers like the Rhine, Elbe, and Weser provide an excellent inland transport network.

III. The Central Plateau

Lying south of the Great European Plains, this area consists of belts of mountains, hills, and plateaus made of hard and old rocks.

  • Densely covered by forests and less populated compared to the plains.
  • Prominent features include the Bohemian Massif, Vosges (France), Black Forest Mountains (Germany), and the Spanish Meseta.

IV. The Alpine System / Southern Mountains

A chain of young fold mountains stretching from the Atlantic Ocean to the Caspian Sea. They form part of the Mid-world Mountain Belt.

  • The Alps: The center of the system. Spread across France, Italy, Switzerland, and Germany. Mt. Blanc ($4,810 \text{ m}$) is the highest peak in the Alps.
  • The Pyrenees: Form the natural border between Spain and France.
  • The Apennines: Run down the length of Italy, forming the "spine" of the peninsula.
  • The Caucasus Mountains: Lie between the Black Sea and the Caspian Sea, separating Asia from Europe. Contains Mt. Elbrus, an extinct volcano.
  • The Carpathian Mountains: Spread over Romania, Ukraine, and Poland.

5. Major Waterbodies (Rivers & Lakes)

Rivers in Europe provide excellent inland waterways and can be categorized by their flow directions and origins:

  • Rivers in the West: Originate in the Alpine System. Includes the Rhone, Rhine, Danube, and Po.
    • The Rhine is the busiest river in Europe. It flows north into the North Sea.
    • The Danube is the only east-flowing major river, draining into the Black Sea.
  • Rivers in the East: Russian rivers like the Volga (longest), Don, and Dnieper. These rivers tend to freeze during winters.
  • Lakes: Europe has numerous lakes. Finland is popularly known as the "Land of Thousand Lakes". Lake Ladoga in Russia is the largest freshwater lake in Europe.
