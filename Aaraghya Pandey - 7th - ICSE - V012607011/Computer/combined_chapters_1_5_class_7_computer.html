CHAPTERS 1 TO 5

Chapter 1: Hardware Components

Computer hardware refers to the physical components of a computer which we can touch and feel. Depending on their location and how they interact with the computer, hardware is categorized into internal and external components. They work together to process input and deliver output.

Concept

Internal Hardware Components are located inside the CPU cabinet. This computer case houses and protects all the internal components. It also provides ports and connections for external devices.

1. Internal Hardware Components

  • CPU and its Parts: The Central Processing Unit (CPU), also called the microprocessor, is the primary hardware component. It executes instructions and acts like the brain of a computer. It has three main parts:
    • Arithmetic Logic Unit (ALU): Responsible for performing a wide range of arithmetic operations (+, -, *, /) and logical operations (AND, OR, NOT).
    • Control Unit (CU): Manages the execution of instructions and controls the flow of data within the computer, ensuring correct execution order.
    • Registers: High-speed, small-capacity storage locations within the CPU used for holding data, operands, and intermediate results during processing.
  • Motherboard: The primary circuit board acting as the central hub or backbone connecting all internal components (CPU, RAM, storage, etc.). It includes a clock generator that sets the system's clock speed and timing to synchronize components. (Also known as 'mobo' or 'mainboard').
  • SMPS (Switched Mode Power Supply): A power supply unit used to convert the incoming AC (Alternating Current) voltage to the DC (Direct Current) voltage required by the computer's electronic components.
  • Computer Fan: A crucial component of a computer's cooling system, designed to release heat generated by various internal parts to prevent overheating.
  • Modem: Short for modulator-demodulator. It modulates digital signals into analog signals for transmission over telephone lines, and demodulates incoming analog signals back into digital form.
  • Ports: Physical connectors that enable communication and data transfer.
    • USB Port: Universal Serial Bus, used to connect keyboards, mice, printers, etc.
    • HDMI Port: High-Definition Multimedia Interface, used for high-quality audio and video signals (monitors, TVs, projectors).
    • Thunderbolt Port: Offers extremely fast data transfer speeds, ideal for large file backups.
    • Ethernet Port: Also known as RJ45 ports, used to connect devices to wired networks (LANs) and the Internet.

2. External Hardware Components (Peripherals)

External components refer to physical devices connected from the outside, commonly called peripherals. They facilitate human-computer interaction.

  • Input Devices: Enable users to enter data and commands.
    • Keyboard: A standard keyboard has 104 keys divided into: Typing keys (Alphabets, Numbers, Symbols), Navigation keys (Arrows, Home, End), Control keys (Ctrl, Alt, Esc), Function keys (F1 to F12), and the Numeric keypad (right side, for fast math).
    • Mouse: A hand-held device with left and right buttons and a scroll wheel (for moving through documents vertically). Moving it moves the mouse pointer on the screen.
    • Scanner: Converts physical documents into digital formats (PDF, JPEG, PNG). Types include Flatbed (most common), Sheet-fed, Drum, Handheld, and 3D Scanners.
    • Microphone: A transducer device that converts sound waves into an electrical signal to be processed, amplified, or recorded.
  • Output Devices: Provide information in a human-readable form.
    • Monitor (VDU): Displays visual output known as a soft copy. Types include CRT (large, heavy, obsolete), LCD (flat, less power), and LED (most common, 40% less power than LCD, thinner, and more durable).
    • Printer: Produces printed output known as a hard copy. Inkjet printers use small ink droplets (great for photos/colour), while Laser printers use a laser beam (fast, high-volume text printing).
    • Projector: An optical device that projects images or videos onto a larger screen or wall.
Fact

Cloud Storage: Today, data isn't just stored on physical drives. The Cloud allows you to store data over the Internet using services like OneDrive, Google Drive, iCloud, and Dropbox, making it accessible from any device.

3. Storage Devices

  • Hard Disk Drive (HDD): The primary internal data storage device for operating systems, software, and files.
  • External Hard Drives: Portable HDDs connected via USB/Thunderbolt for backups and transferring large files.
  • CDs (Compact Discs): An early optical disc format for music and data, storing up to 700 MB.
  • DVDs (Digital Versatile Discs): Optical discs with a much larger storage capacity, used for high-quality video and large software installations.

Chapter 2: Number System

A Number System is a systematic way of representing numbers using symbols and rules. The Base or Radix indicates the total number of unique digits or symbols used in that specific system.

1. Types of Number Systems

  • Decimal (Base-10): Uses 10 symbols (0-9). The positional value is based on powers of 10.
  • Binary (Base-2): Uses 2 symbols (0, 1). Each digit is called a bit. 8 bits make 1 byte. It represents the ON (1) and OFF (0) electrical states of a computer.
  • Octal (Base-8): Uses 8 symbols (0-7). Positional values are based on powers of 8.
  • Hexadecimal (Base-16): Uses 16 symbols. Numbers 0-9 represent themselves, while letters A-F represent values 10-15 (A=10, B=11, C=12, D=13, E=14, F=15). Commonly used in computer memory addresses and MAC addresses.

2. Binary Operations & Conversion

  • Decimal to Binary: Use the "Divide by 2" method. Repeatedly divide the decimal number by 2 and record the remainders. Read the remainders from bottom to top to get the binary equivalent.
  • Binary Addition Rules: 0+0=0, 0+1=1, 1+0=1, and 1+1=10 (Write 0, carry 1).

Chapter 3: Computer Viruses

A Computer Virus (Vital Information Resources Under Siege) is malicious software (malware) written intentionally to alter the way a computer operates, replicating itself and spreading from one system to another.

Important

Ransomware is a highly dangerous type of malware that encrypts a user's files or locks them out of their system, demanding a financial ransom (often in cryptocurrency) to restore access.

1. Categories of Malware

  • Boot Sector Virus: Infects the Master Boot Record (MBR) of a hard disk. It loads into the memory every time the computer is turned on, before the OS even loads.
  • Macro Virus: Infects files created by applications that use macro languages, such as Microsoft Word or Excel documents.
  • Worm: Replicates itself over a network to consume bandwidth and system resources. Unlike a virus, it does not need to attach to a host file.
  • Trojan Horse: A deceptive program that appears useful but harbours malicious code (e.g., creating backdoors for hackers).
  • Spyware & Adware: Spyware secretly monitors user activity and keystrokes, while Adware floods the user with unwanted pop-up advertisements.

2. Prevention (Antivirus Software)

Antivirus software scans, detects, and removes malware. When a virus is found, the software will either clean the file, delete it, or quarantine it (isolate it in a safe folder so it cannot run or spread).

Chapter 4: Ethics and Safety Measures with Internet

Cyber ethics refers to the code of responsible behavior on the Internet. Just as we follow rules in physical society, we must follow Netiquette (Network Etiquette) online.

1. Unethical Practices

  • Plagiarism: Stealing someone's intellectual work and presenting it as your own. Always use quotation marks and provide citations to avoid this.
  • Phishing: Cybercriminals send fake emails claiming to be from legitimate organizations (like banks) to trick users into revealing sensitive data like passwords and OTPs.
  • Cyberbullying: Using digital platforms to harass, intimidate, or humiliate others. It can have severe psychological impacts.
  • Hacking: Finding vulnerabilities in a system to gain unauthorized access. (Ethical hackers do this legally to improve security; malicious hackers do it to steal or destroy).
Concept

Digital Footprint: The trail of data you leave behind online. An Active footprint is data you share intentionally (social media posts), while a Passive footprint is collected without direct input (cookies, IP addresses).

2. Safety Measures

  • Always check for HTTPS (the 's' stands for secure) and the padlock icon in the address bar before entering personal information.
  • Enable Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) on your accounts for an extra layer of security.
  • Respect Intellectual Property Rights (Copyrights, Patents, Trademarks) and do not engage in software piracy.

Chapter 5: Introduction To Excel 2016 and 2019

Microsoft Excel is a powerful Spreadsheet software application used to organize, format, calculate, and visualize tabular data.

1. Essential Excel Components

  • Workbook vs. Worksheet: A Workbook is the entire Excel file (like a notebook). A Worksheet is a single page within that file, consisting of a grid of rows and columns.
  • Cell: The intersection of a row and a column. The Active Cell is currently selected and has a thick green border.
  • Cell Address: Defines a cell's location (e.g., Column C, Row 5 is C5). This is displayed in the Name Box.
  • Formula Bar: Located above the grid, it displays the actual data or underlying formula of the active cell.
Fact

An Excel worksheet contains an incredible 1,048,576 rows and 16,384 columns (ranging from A to XFD), allowing for massive data analysis.

2. Formulas and Cell Referencing

  • Basic Rules: Every formula in Excel MUST begin with an equal sign (=). Excel follows the BODMAS/PEMDAS rule for calculation order.
  • Functions: Pre-defined formulas in Excel. Examples include =SUM(A1:A5), =AVERAGE(B1:B10), =MAX(), and =MIN().
  • Cell Referencing Types:
    • Relative Reference: When a formula is copied, the cell addresses change relative to the destination (e.g., A1 becomes A2).
    • Absolute Reference: Using a dollar sign ($) locks the row or column so it doesn't change when copied (e.g., $A$1).
    • Mixed Reference: Only the row or the column is locked (e.g., $A1 or A$1).
