Elements, Compounds and Mixtures

1. Elements

Concept

Elements are pure substances that are made up of only one type of atom and cannot be broken down into simpler substances by physical or chemical methods.

Elements act as the fundamental building blocks of all matter. Atoms of each element are unique. Currently, there are about 118 known elements, and most of them exist in a solid state at room temperature.

Types of Elements

  • Metals: Generally hard and shiny. Examples include gold, silver, iron, and aluminium.
  • Non-metals: Generally soft and dull. Examples include oxygen and hydrogen (which are gases at room temperature).
  • Metalloids: Elements that exhibit intermediate properties of both metals and non-metals. Examples include silicon and boron.
Fact

Graphene aerogel is a wonder material developed by scientists. It is considered the lightest material on Earth. Made from carbon, it is highly porous and possesses high absorbing capacity, making it an excellent environmental cleaner.

2. Compounds

Concept

A compound is a pure substance formed when atoms of two or more elements combine chemically in a definite proportion.

The properties of a compound are entirely different from the properties of its constituent elements. The elements within a compound cannot be separated by physical methods; they require chemical processes for separation.

Classic Examples of Compounds

  • Water ($H_2O$): Consists of hydrogen and oxygen. Water extinguishes fire, whereas its components (hydrogen is combustible, and oxygen supports combustion) behave completely differently.
  • Sodium Chloride (Common Salt): Formed from sodium (a highly reactive soft metal) and chlorine (a hazardous green gas) combined in a 1:1 ratio. The resulting compound is a safe, essential part of our daily diet.
  • Carbon Dioxide ($CO_2$): Essential for plants during the process of photosynthesis.

3. Mixtures

Concept

A mixture consists of two or more substances that are mixed in varying proportions but retain their individual properties. The constituents do not react chemically with each other.

Mixtures can be separated into their constituent parts using physical methods (like filtration, evaporation, or magnetic separation). They are broadly classified into two types based on visibility and interaction:

Types of Mixtures

  • Uniform (Homogeneous) Mixtures: The components cannot be seen separately, even with a microscope. The composition is uniform throughout. Example: Sugar dissolved in water, Aerated water, Alloys.
  • Non-uniform (Heterogeneous) Mixtures: The components are visible with the naked eye or a magnifying glass. The composition varies from one region to another. Example: Sprout salad, Sand and water.

Common Examples of Mixtures by State

Mixture Type Example Uniform / Non-Uniform
Gas and gas Air Uniform
Gas and liquid Aerated water (soda water) Uniform
Solid and gas Carbon particles in air (smoke) Non-uniform
Liquid and liquid Acetic acid in water (vinegar) Uniform
Solid and liquid Sand and water Non-uniform
Solid and solid Alloys (e.g., Stainless steel) Uniform
Important

Mixture vs. Compound Experiment:
If you mix iron filings and sulfur powder, you get a mixture. The iron retains its magnetic property and can be separated using a magnet. However, if you heat this mixture, a chemical reaction occurs to form Iron Sulfide (a black solid). This is a compound, and it will no longer be attracted to a magnet because its original properties have completely changed.

4. Minerals

Minerals are naturally occurring uniform substances with a definite chemical composition and internal structure. Most rocks are composed of minerals. While native minerals are pure elements (like gold, silver, copper, or sulfur), most minerals exist as compounds (like quartz, calcite, and mica).

Uses of Elements, Compounds & Mixtures in Daily Life

  • Elements: Oxygen is vital for respiration and combustion. Iron and aluminium are widely used to construct bridges, vehicles, and buildings. Gold and silver are used for ornaments.
  • Compounds: Used to formulate life-saving medicines and vaccines. Baking soda is utilized in cooking and cleaning. Minerals like talc (talcum powder) and silica (glass, toothpaste) are chemical compounds we rely on daily.
  • Mixtures: Building materials like concrete and wood. Air is an essential gaseous mixture for all living beings. Stainless steel (an alloy/mixture) is heavily used for making utensils, cutlery, and surgical instruments.
