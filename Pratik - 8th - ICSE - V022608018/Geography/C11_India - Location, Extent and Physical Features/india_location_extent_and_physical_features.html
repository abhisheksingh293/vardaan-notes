India: Location, Extent and Physical Features

1. The Indian Subcontinent and Location

India is a country of vast geographical expanse located in South Asia. It is surrounded by the lofty Himalayas in the north and large water bodies on three sides in the south, making it a peninsula. It is the seventh-largest country in the world by area and the second-most populous (after China).

The Indian Subcontinent

India shares common land boundaries with seven countries:

  • North-West: Afghanistan and Pakistan.
  • North: China, Nepal, and Bhutan.
  • East: Bangladesh and Myanmar.
  • South (across the sea): Sri Lanka (separated by the Palk Strait) and the Maldives.

The landmass comprising India, Pakistan, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, and Sri Lanka forms a unique geographical entity known as the Indian Subcontinent.

Fact

India in a Nutshell:

  • Latitudinal Extent: 8°4'N to 37°6'N
  • Longitudinal Extent: 68°7'E to 97°25'E
  • Area: Approx. 3.28 million sq. km (2.4% of total world area)
  • North-South Distance: 3200 km (Kashmir to Kanyakumari)
  • East-West Distance: 2933 km (Arunachal Pradesh to Kutch)
  • Length of Coastline: 7516.5 km

2. Indian Standard Time and Political Divisions

Indian Standard Time (IST)

Due to India's vast longitudinal extent, there is a local time difference of almost 2 hours between its eastern and western limits. To avoid confusion, the centrally located longitude of 82°30'E (passing through Prayagraj) is taken as the Standard Meridian of India. The local time here is the Indian Standard Time (IST), which is 5½ hours ahead of Greenwich Mean Time (GMT).

Political and Administrative Divisions

For administrative convenience, India is divided into 28 States and 8 Union Territories (including the National Capital Region of Delhi). The present division is mostly organized on the basis of the languages spoken by the people.

3. Physical Divisions of India

India presents a great diversity of landforms. It is broadly divided into six major physical divisions:

A. The Northern Mountains

They form a continuous shield in the north from Kashmir to Assam. They primarily consist of the Karakoram Mountains (home to Mt. K2, the second-highest peak in the world) and the Himalayas, which are young fold mountains consisting of three parallel ranges:

  • The Himadri (Greater Himalayas): High snow-capped peaks over 6000m. Contains Mount Everest, Kanchenjunga, and important glaciers like Gangotri and Yamunotri.
  • The Himachal (Middle Himalayas): Covered with evergreen, oak, and coniferous forests. Famous for beautiful hill stations like Shimla, Nainital, and Darjeeling.
  • The Shiwalik (Outer Himalayas): The lowest foothills. Broad valleys between Himachal and Shiwalik are known as duns (e.g., Dehradun).
Concept

Influence of the Northern Mountains:

  • Act as a climatic barrier preventing cold central Asian winds from entering India and blocking rain-bearing monsoon winds from leaving.
  • Provide perennial water to rivers from massive glaciers.
  • Forests serve as valuable resources for timber and wildlife habitats.

B. The Northern Plains

Also known as the Indo-Gangetic Plains, they stretch for about 2500 km. They are formed by three main river basins: the Indus, the Ganga, and the Brahmaputra. They are highly fertile due to alluvial soil deposits, supporting dense populations, extensive agriculture, and a wide network of roads and railways.

C. The Peninsular Plateau

Located south of the Northern Plains, it is an ancient, hard crystalline landmass shaped like a triangle. It consists mainly of three sub-plateaus:

  • The Malwa Plateau: In the west, flanked by the Aravallis and Vindhyas.
  • The Chota Nagpur Plateau: In the east, covering parts of Jharkhand, Odisha, and Chhattisgarh. Highly rich in mineral reserves (coal, iron, mica).
  • The Deccan Plateau: A large triangular area bounded by the Satpuras in the north and the Eastern/Western Ghats on the sides. Rich in fertile black soil (Regur) excellent for cotton cultivation.
Important

Western Ghats vs. Eastern Ghats:

  • Western Ghats: Continuous, higher in elevation (avg. 1200m), steep slopes rising sharply from the western coast.
  • Eastern Ghats: Broken by river valleys (Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri), much lower in height (avg. 600m), with gentle slopes.

D. The Thar Desert

A large arid region in north-western India (mostly Rajasthan). It is bordered by the Aravalli range in the east. It is irrigated by the Indira Gandhi Canal. The only major river in this region is the Luni.

E. The Coastal Plains

Narrow stretches of plain land along the coasts:

  • Western Coastal Plains: Stretch from Kutch to Kanyakumari. Divided into the Gujarat Coast, Konkan Coast, and Malabar Coast. Contains the largest seaport, Mumbai, and numerous lagoons (e.g., Lake Vembanad).
  • Eastern Coastal Plains: Wider and more level, stretching from the Mahanadi to Kanyakumari. Divided into the Northern Circars and the Coromandel Coast. Known for fertile river deltas and large lakes (Chilika, Pulicat).

F. India's Island Groups

  • Lakshadweep Islands: Located in the Arabian Sea. They are coral islands forming the smallest Union Territory.
  • Andaman and Nicobar Islands: Located in the Bay of Bengal. They are larger, more numerous, and are remnants of submerged volcanic mountains (including Barren Island, India's only active volcano). Separated by the Ten Degree Channel.

4. The Drainage System

The river systems of India can be classified into two major groups:

  • The Himalayan Rivers: Originating from snow-capped mountains (e.g., Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra). These are perennial (flow year-round) as they are fed by both rainfall and melting snow. They are long, slow-flowing over plains, and highly useful for irrigation and navigation.
  • The Peninsular Rivers: Originating in the plateaus and highlands. These are seasonal and rain-fed, drying up in summers. They flow through deep valleys and are less navigable.
    • West-flowing: Narmada and Tapi (drain into the Arabian Sea).
    • East-flowing: Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, and Kaveri (drain into the Bay of Bengal and form large deltas).
