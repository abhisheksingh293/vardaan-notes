Laws of Motion

1. Contact and Non-Contact Forces

A force when applied on a body can produce two main effects: it can change the state of rest or motion of the body, and it can change the size or shape of the body.

Concept

Force: Force is that physical cause which changes (or tends to change) either the size, shape, or the state of rest or motion of a body. It is a vector quantity.

Contact Forces

Forces which are applied on bodies by making a physical contact with them are called contact forces.

  • Frictional Force: When a body slides or rolls over a rough surface, a force starts acting opposite to the motion.
  • Normal Reaction Force: A force exerted by a surface perpendicularly (normal) on the object placed on it, balancing the object's weight.
  • Tension Force: The restoring force developed in a string when a body is suspended from it.
  • Force by a Spring: The restoring force exerted by a stretched or compressed spring opposite to the displacement.
  • Force during Collision: When two bodies collide, they push each other with equal and opposite action-reaction forces.

Non-Contact Forces (Forces at a Distance)

The forces experienced by bodies even without being physically in touch are called non-contact forces.

  • Gravitational Force: The force of attraction between two bodies because of their masses. Always attractive in nature.
  • Electrostatic Force: The force acting between two charged objects. Like charges repel, unlike charges attract.
  • Magnetic Force: The force exerted between magnetic poles. Like poles repel, unlike poles attract.
Important

General Character of Non-Contact Forces: The magnitude of a non-contact force varies inversely as the square of the distance of separation. If distance is doubled, the force becomes one-fourth.

2. Newton's First Law of Motion and Inertia

Before Galileo, it was believed a continuous force was needed to keep a body moving. Galileo disproved this through his law of inertia.

Definition

Newton's First Law: If a body is in a state of rest, it will remain in the state of rest, and if it is in the state of motion, it will remain moving in the same direction with the same speed unless an external force is applied on it.

Mass and Inertia

Inertia is the inherent property of an object to resist changes to its state of rest or motion. Mass is the measure of inertia. The greater the mass, the greater the inertia. It is harder to move a heavy truck than a bicycle.

Kinds of Inertia

  • Inertia of Rest: The tendency of a body to remain at rest unless an external force acts on it.
  • Inertia of Motion: The tendency of a body to continue in its state of uniform motion unless an external force acts on it.
Examples

Inertia of Rest: Dust falling from a beaten carpet, passengers falling backward when a train starts suddenly.

Inertia of Motion: An athlete running before taking a long jump, passengers leaning forward when a bus stops suddenly.

3. Linear Momentum & Newton's Second Law

Linear Momentum

The force required to stop a moving body depends on its mass and velocity.

Linear Momentum ($p$): It is the product of a body's mass ($m$) and velocity ($v$).

Formula: $p = mv$

SI Unit: $\text{kg m/s}$. It is a vector quantity having the direction of motion.

Newton's Second Law of Motion

The first law defines force qualitatively, while the second law provides the quantitative measurement.

Definition

Newton's Second Law: The rate of change of momentum of a body is directly proportional to the force applied on it, and the change in momentum takes place in the direction of the applied force.

Derivation ($F=ma$):

Rate of change of momentum = $\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t} = \frac{\Delta(mv)}{\Delta t}$

If mass ($m$) is constant, $\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t} = m\frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$

Since Acceleration ($a$) = $\frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$, then Force ($F$) $\propto ma$. Taking the constant of proportionality as 1, we get:

Important

Mathematical Form: $F = ma$

SI Unit of Force: Newton (N). One Newton is the force which acts on a mass of $1\text{ kg}$ to produce an acceleration of $1\text{ m/s}^2$.

C.G.S Unit of Force: Dyne. ($1\text{ Newton} = 10^5\text{ dyne}$).

4. Newton's Third Law of Motion

The first and second laws describe the magnitude and acceleration of force, but the third law explains how forces interact between two bodies.

Definition

Newton's Third Law: To every action, there is always an equal and opposite reaction.

Forces always exist in pairs. The action and reaction forces are equal in magnitude but act in opposite directions. Crucially, action and reaction never act on the same body; they act simultaneously on two different interacting bodies.

Examples

1. Swimming: A swimmer pushes water backward (action), and the water pushes the swimmer forward (reaction).

2. Rocket Motion: Exhaust gases are pushed out downward (action), propelling the rocket upward (reaction).

3. Firing a Gun: The bullet is pushed forward (action), causing the gun to recoil backward (reaction).

5. Universal Law of Gravitation

Given by Sir Isaac Newton, this law explains the attractive force existing between any two masses in the universe.

Statement: The force of attraction acting between two bodies is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.

Formula: $F = G\frac{M_{1}M_{2}}{r^2}$

Where $G$ is the Universal Gravitational Constant ($6.67 \times 10^{-11} \text{ N m}^2/\text{kg}^2$).

Acceleration Due to Gravity ($g$)

The rate at which the velocity of a freely falling body increases due to the gravitational force of the earth is called acceleration due to gravity ($g$).

Average value of $g$ on Earth: $9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$.

Relationship between $g$ and $G$: $g = \frac{GM}{R^2}$ (Where $M$ is the mass of the planet and $R$ is its radius).

Mass vs Weight

Important
  • Mass: The quantity of matter contained in a body. It is a scalar quantity, measured in kg, and remains constant everywhere.
  • Weight ($W$): The force with which the earth attracts a body towards its centre. It is a vector quantity, measured in Newtons (N), and changes from place to place depending on $g$. ($W = mg$).
