Chinese Civilisation

1. Extent of the Civilisation

The history of the Chinese Civilisation dates back over 4,500 years. It is one of the oldest and most long-lasting civilisations in the world. The unique culture that developed in ancient China forms the basis of the culture that exists in China today.

  • The civilisation flourished on the banks of the river Hwang Ho.
  • Hwang Ho literally means Yellow River because the large amount of silt and sediment in the water gives it a yellow colour.
  • The fertile soil, abundant water, a temperate climate, and waterways helped agriculture and civilisation flourish.
Important

The Sorrow of China: The river Hwang Ho often changed its course after floods. This led to the massive destruction of crops and canals. For this reason, the Hwang Ho river is also called the "Sorrow of China." To protect themselves, people built dykes and embankments to control the floods.

2. Chinese Dynasties

Throughout most of its history, China was ruled by powerful families of kings known as Dynasties. They ruled for over 3,000 years. The Chinese Civilisation is often classified according to these ruling dynasties:

  • Shang Dynasty: 1600 BCE to 1046 BCE.
  • Chou or Zhou Dynasty: 1046 BCE to 256 BCE.
  • Qin Dynasty: 221 BCE to 206 BCE.
  • Han Dynasty: 206 BCE to 220 CE.
Fact

The Chou (Zhou) period is known as the classical period of China because culture developed to great heights during this era. Despite changing dynasties, the basic character of Chinese society remained largely the same.

3. Society

The society in ancient China was highly structured with a clear hierarchy:

  • The King: He was all-powerful and ruled the territory.
  • Noblemen: They were next in importance and helped the King during wars and conquests.
  • Commoners: Under the noblemen were the merchants, craftsmen, and farmers.
  • Slaves: The lowest class in the social hierarchy.

The Chinese greatly respected their elders and ancestors. They formed clans (large groups sharing a common ancestor). The family was considered a sacred unit, and the head of the family was given immense respect. Women traditionally worked within the house.

4. Religion

Like other river valley civilisations, the Chinese worshipped the forces of nature. The earth was worshipped in the form of an earthen mound.

  • Shang Ti: They worshipped a supreme god called Shang Ti, which means the "Lord of Heaven".
  • Ancestor Worship: This was highly prevalent. From childhood, children were taught to respect the wisdom and achievements of their ancestors.
Concept

Philosophical Systems: Later in history, two major philosophical and religious systems emerged:
1. Taoism: Founded by Lao-Tze, emphasising a natural and simple way of life.
2. Confucianism: Founded by Confucius, which was a system of philosophical and ethical teachings.

5. Occupations and Trade

Agriculture

Agriculture was the main occupation. The Chinese developed a highly organised network of irrigation to increase agricultural produce. Main crops included rice, wheat, barley, sorghum, millets, and soyabeans.

Tea Cultivation: The Chinese were the first to cultivate tea. They initially grew tea for medicinal purposes, which later became a popular beverage worldwide.

Sericulture (Silk Production)

The Chinese were the first to produce silk from silkworms. This process is called Sericulture. Silkworms were specially reared, and weaving high-quality silk threads became an important tradition. Silk was their chief item of export.

Trade

The Chinese maintained trade relations with countries like Egypt, Mesopotamia, Iran, and India. Their chief exports were silk, chinaware, tea, and paper.

6. Script

The Chinese script was pictographic or ideographic (representing ideas by graphic symbols). They wrote on silk cloth with a fine brush. Interestingly, the earliest Chinese writings were found on animal bones. The script is remarkable because it has changed very little since earliest times.

7. Contributions and Achievements

The ancient Chinese Civilisation was highly advanced and made tremendous progress in science, technology, and architecture.

Architecture

  • The Great Wall of China: A marvel of engineering skills, it is the highest and longest wall in the world. It was built with watchtowers for soldiers to keep watch and ward off enemy attacks. It is recognised as one of the Seven Wonders of the World.
  • The Pagodas: These are stupa-style Buddhist temples made of wood with decorated roofs at each level, showcasing fine traditional Chinese architecture.
  • The Tomb of Emperor Qin Shi Huang: One of the most spectacular archaeological discoveries, this mausoleum contains a terracotta army of 8,000 soldiers standing in full military dress according to division and rank.

Inventions and Science

  • Paper and Silk: The greatest contributions of the Chinese to the world.
  • Porcelain (Chinaware): They were the first to make a hard, shiny glazed pottery decorated with beautiful designs.
  • Calendar: They developed a combined solar and lunar calendar. It had 12 months of 30 days (360 days) and was adjusted periodically by adding 5 days.
  • Seismograph: They invented an instrument to measure and record the waves of an earthquake.
  • Other Inventions: The Chinese are credited with inventing the mariner's compass, gunpowder, the umbrella, kites, and the astronomical clock.
