AGRICULTURE

Concept

Agriculture refers to the practice of cultivating land to grow crops and rearing animals. It is one of the oldest economic activities and provides food for humans and fodder for animals, alongside raw materials for industries.

1. Factors Influencing Agriculture

The growth and development of agriculture depend heavily on several environmental and human-made factors:

  • Arable Land: Land on which crops can be grown, such as plains, river basins, and deltas. (Coffee/tea grow better on hill slopes).
  • Fertile Soil: Soil provides nutrients and moisture to plants. Without natural fertility, large amounts of artificial fertilizers are required.
  • Climatic Conditions: Rainfall, moisture, and temperature dictate what crops can grow. (e.g., Rice needs wet tropical areas, wheat needs cooler, drier areas).
  • Other Factors: Availability of labor, manure, fertilizers, good quality seeds, machinery, and transport facilities.

2. Traditional vs. Modern Farming

Farming has evolved significantly over time. Below is a comparison between ancient methods and modern techniques:

  • Farm Size: Traditional holdings are small and scattered, whereas modern holdings are usually large.
  • Labor & Tools: Traditional relies heavily on family labor, simple ploughs, and sickles. Modern relies on machines (tractors, harvesters) and minimal human labor.
  • Water Supply: Traditional is heavily dependent on seasonal rains. Modern utilizes advanced irrigation facilities to grow crops even in dry seasons.
  • Inputs: Traditional uses natural manure. Modern uses chemical fertilizers, pesticides, and High-Yielding Variety (HYV) seeds.
  • Yield & Health: Modern farming gives a much higher yield due to chemicals and HYV seeds, though traditional farming often retains higher natural food value.

3. Types of Agricultural Practices

Subsistence Farming

Practiced mainly to fulfill the needs of the farmer's family with very little surplus left for the market.

  • Intensive Subsistence Farming: Small farms cultivated by many people using abundant labor, manure, and fertilizers. Common in densely populated countries like India and China. Often more than one crop is grown annually.
  • Shifting Farming: A primitive "slash and burn" method. A patch of land is cleared, trees are felled, stumps are burned, and ash is mixed with the soil. Once fertility drops, the farmer moves on.
Important

Shifting farming is highly discouraged worldwide because it accelerates soil erosion, causes deforestation, and leads to severe air pollution due to the burning of stumps.

Commercial Farming

Farming where crops are grown primarily for sale in the market to earn profit.

  • Extensive Farming: A small number of people work on massive farms using heavy machinery. It is highly capital-intensive and common in sparsely populated countries like the USA and Canada.
  • Mixed Farming: Crops are grown and animals are reared on the same farm. Animal dung acts as manure, and crop residue serves as fodder, providing supplementary income.
  • Plantation Farming: Large estates cultivating a single cash crop (like tea, coffee, or rubber) using modern scientific methods, huge capital, and skilled labor.

4. Major Crops of the World

Food Crops (Cereals)

Crops grown primarily to feed human populations.

  • Wheat: Requires a cool, moist climate (10°C - 15°C) during sowing, and warm, dry climate (20°C - 25°C) during harvesting. Needs moderate rainfall (75cm) and loamy soil. Grown extensively in the USA, Russia, and northern India.
  • Rice (Paddy): Staple food for half the world. Requires hot and humid climate (25°C), high rainfall (150cm - 300cm), and fertile alluvial soil that can retain standing water.

Cash Crops

Crops grown specifically for sale and to provide raw materials to industries.

  • Cotton: A fiber crop that needs well-drained clayey Black Cotton Soil (Deccan Plateau). Requires high temperature (27°C), moderate rainfall, and at least 200 frost-free days.
  • Jute (The Golden Fibre): Needs a hot, humid climate (24°C - 35°C), heavy rainfall (150cm), and rich deltaic alluvial soil renewed by floods. India and Bangladesh are leading producers.
  • Sugarcane: Requires well-drained rich alluvial/lava soil, 20°C - 26°C temperature, and 100-150cm of rainfall. Must be processed within 48 hours of cutting.
  • Tea: A beverage crop needing well-drained loamy soil on sloping land to prevent water-logging. It is a shade-loving plant needing 18°C - 24°C and heavy rainfall (150cm - 250cm).
  • Coffee: Needs warm climate (15°C - 28°C) and a dry spell to ripen the beans. Brazil is the largest producer and is known as the "Coffee Pot" of the world.
  • Rubber: Derived from latex. Requires hot, wet climate (25°C - 35°C) and heavy rainfall (200cm - 300cm) distributed evenly throughout the year.

5. The Green Revolution

The term refers to the drastic increase in the production of food crops (especially wheat and rice) worldwide between the 1940s and late 1960s.

Fact

Dr. Norman Borlaug, an American agronomist, developed the high-yielding variety of wheat seed in Mexico. For his remarkable achievements, he was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 1970 and is known globally as the Father of the Green Revolution.

Key Strategies of the Green Revolution:

  • Use of High-Yielding Variety (HYV) seeds developed by cross-breeding.
  • Heavy application of chemical fertilizers, pesticides, and weedicides.
  • Consolidation of small land holdings and introduction of modern machinery (tractors).
  • Multiple cropping (growing two or more crops a year) made possible by quick-maturing seeds and improved irrigation.

Impact: It transformed India from a food-scarce nation to a self-sufficient one, boosted agro-based industries, and created massive rural employment opportunities.
