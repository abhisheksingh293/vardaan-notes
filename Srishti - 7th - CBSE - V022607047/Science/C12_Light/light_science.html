Section D: Answer the following questions in brief.

Q1. Explain clearly the difference between a real image and a virtual image. Give one example of a virtual image.

Answer:

  • Real Image: An image that can be obtained or projected on a screen is called a real image. It is formed when light rays actually meet or intersect after reflection or refraction. Real images are always inverted.
  • Virtual Image: An image that cannot be obtained on a screen is called a virtual image. It is formed when light rays appear to meet or diverge from a point after reflection or refraction. Virtual images are always erect.
Example

Example of a virtual image: The image formed by a plane mirror, such as the mirror we use to see ourselves, is a virtual image.

Q2. How does changing the distance of the object, from a convex mirror, affect the size and nature of the image formed by it?

Answer: Changing the distance of the object from a convex mirror does not change the nature of the image. The image formed by a convex mirror is always virtual, erect, and diminished (smaller than the object), regardless of the object's distance from the mirror. However, as the object moves closer to the mirror, the image becomes slightly larger (but still smaller than the object) and moves closer to the mirror's pole.

Q4. Where should we keep an object, in front of a concave mirror, so as to get a virtual and magnified image of it?

Answer: To get a virtual and magnified image using a concave mirror, the object should be placed very close to the mirror, specifically between the pole (P) of the mirror and its principal focus (F).

Q5. The magnified image, of an object, appears to be formed, behind the mirror, by an apparent intersection of the reflected rays. What can you say about (a) nature of the image (b) the type of mirror used?

Answer:

  1. Nature of the image: Since the image is formed behind the mirror by an apparent intersection of rays and is magnified, it is a virtual and erect image.
  2. Type of mirror used: A magnified virtual image can only be formed by a concave mirror (when the object is very close to it). Therefore, the mirror used is a concave mirror.

Q6. Rohit suggested to his father to fix a 'security mirror' which would provide them with a wider view of the surrounding of their shop. Name the type of mirror that Rohit must have suggested to his father. Also mention the basic characteristics of the image formed by this type of mirrors.

Answer:

  • Type of mirror: Rohit must have suggested a convex mirror. Convex mirrors bulge outwards and thus offer a wider field of view compared to plane mirrors.
  • Basic characteristics of the image: The image formed by a convex mirror is always virtual, erect, and diminished (smaller in size).

Q7. State the type of mirror that the following items/objects would correspond to:

Answer:

  • (a) inside of a stainless steel bowl: Acts like a concave mirror.
  • (b) reflector of a torch: Uses a concave mirror to produce a strong, parallel beam of light.
  • (c) a polished shining wooden surface: Acts like a plane mirror (if flat).
  • (d) the shiny outer surface of the bicycle bell: Acts like a convex mirror.

Section E: Answer the following questions.

Q1. State the laws of reflection. Describe an activity to show that incident ray, reflected ray and the normal, at the point of incidence, all lie in same plane.

Answer:

Laws of Reflection:

  1. The angle of incidence is always equal to the angle of reflection (∠i = ∠r).
  2. The incident ray, the reflected ray, and the normal at the point of incidence, all lie in the same plane.
Activity

Activity description: Fix a white sheet of paper on a drawing board. Take a comb and close all its openings except one in the middle. Hold the comb perpendicular to the paper. Shine a torch through the opening of the comb to get a straight beam of light on the paper. Place a strip of plane mirror in the path of the light beam. Let the sheet of paper project a little beyond the edge of the board. Ensure the reflected ray extends to the projecting part of the paper. You will see the incident ray, normal (imaginary line perpendicular to mirror), and reflected ray on the paper. Now, cut or fold the projecting part of the paper where the reflected ray falls. The reflected ray is no longer visible on the folded part. When you bring the paper back to the same plane, the reflected ray reappears. This proves that the incident ray, reflected ray, and normal all lie in the same plane (the plane of the original sheet of paper).

Q3. Draw a ray diagram to show the formation of the image of a point object by (i) a plane mirror and (ii) a convex mirror.

Answer: [Please refer to your textbook for accurate ray diagrams.]

  • (i) Plane Mirror: The diagram should show rays from the point object striking the mirror, reflecting according to laws of reflection, and appearing to meet behind the mirror at a distance equal to object distance.
  • (ii) Convex Mirror: The diagram should show parallel rays diverging after reflection, and appearing to meet at the virtual focus behind the mirror.

Q4. Three persons are standing at points P, Q and R in front of a plane mirror. Can 'P' see himself in the mirror? Also can he see the image of Q and R in the mirror?

Answer:

  • Can 'P' see himself? No, 'P' cannot see his own image. This is because rays of light from 'P' falling on the mirror will be reflected away and will not reach 'P's eyes, given his position relative to the mirror's edges shown in the diagram.
  • Can 'P' see Q and R? 'P' can see the image of 'Q' because the light rays from 'Q' strike the mirror and can reflect towards 'P'. Similarly, 'P' can see the image of 'R' because rays from 'R' can reflect from the mirror to 'P's position. You can verify this by drawing normal lines from the mirror and checking if reflected rays can reach P.

Q5. With the help of a suitable diagram, explain the meaning of the following terms for a concave mirror. (a) centre of curvature (b) pole (c) principal axis (d) focus

Answer: [Please draw the diagram from your textbook showing these parts on a concave mirror.]

  • (a) Centre of Curvature (C): It is the center of the hollow glass sphere of which the spherical mirror is a part.
  • (b) Pole (P): The central point of the reflecting surface of a spherical mirror is called its pole. It lies on the surface of the mirror.
  • (c) Principal Axis: The straight line passing through the pole (P) and the centre of curvature (C) of a spherical mirror is called the principal axis.
  • (d) Focus (Principal Focus) (F): The point on the principal axis where all rays incident parallel to the principal axis converge (in a concave mirror) or appear to diverge from (in a convex mirror) after reflection is called the principal focus.

Q6. What type of mirror is used in the following cases? Is the image formed virtual and erect in each case?

Answer:

  • (a) 'make up' mirror: A concave mirror is used. Yes, when the face is held close to it, it forms a virtual and erect (and magnified) image.
  • (b) 'rear view' mirror of vehicles: A convex mirror is used. Yes, it always forms a virtual and erect (and diminished) image, providing a wider field of view.
  • (c) reflecting type telescope: A large concave mirror is generally used as the primary objective. The final image observed through the eyepiece is often virtual.
  • (d) shaving mirror: A concave mirror is used. Yes, when the face is held close, it forms a virtual and erect (and magnified) image to see details clearly.
