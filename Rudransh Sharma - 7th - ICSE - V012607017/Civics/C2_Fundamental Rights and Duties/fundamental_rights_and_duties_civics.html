Fundamental Rights and Duties

1. Citizenship & The Concerned Citizen

Citizenship is a legal relationship between an individual and the State. An Indian citizen belongs solely to India and cannot simultaneously hold the citizenship of another country (India follows a system of single citizenship).

A concerned citizen is someone who is socially aware, committed, responsible, and actively participates in public life. In a democracy, the right to freedom of speech and expression is very important for functioning as a concerned citizen, allowing them to express views publicly and hold the government accountable.

2. Understanding Fundamental Rights

Concept Fundamental Rights are the basic rights absolutely essential for the all-round development of an individual, making life worth living. They cannot be altered or removed by any simple process.

These rights are essential for the smooth functioning of a democratic republic and lead to the development of the nation. In a diverse country like India—where caste, gender, and class-based inequalities still persist—the effective enforcement of Fundamental Rights empowers weaker sections and ensures social dignity.

3. The Six Fundamental Rights

The Constitution of India guarantees six fundamental rights to its citizens:

I. Right to Equality

This right guarantees that all citizens living in India are equal before the law.

  • The State cannot discriminate against anyone on the basis of gender, religion, caste, race, or place of birth.
  • Everyone has equal access to shops, restaurants, public places, wells, tanks, and public transport.
  • All citizens have equal opportunities for employment in government offices based on merit and qualification.
  • The heinous practice of untouchability is abolished and made a punishable offence.
  • Titles conferred by the British (like Rai Bahadur, Maharaja) are abolished to promote social equality. Only military and academic titles (like Major or Doctor) can be awarded.

II. Right to Freedom

This right protects individuals from oppressive government actions and guarantees civil liberties. It encompasses six specific types of freedom:

  • Freedom of Profession: To practise any lawful profession, trade, or business.
  • Freedom of Speech & Expression: To express views via press, books, public speeches, etc. (Cannot be misused to incite violence or threaten national security).
  • Freedom to Assemble: To hold peaceful public meetings without arms.
  • Freedom to Form Associations: To form unions to voice concerns or safeguard interests.
  • Freedom of Movement: To travel freely across the country.
  • Freedom of Residence: To reside and settle in any part of India.
Important Right to Education (Article 21-A): Added by the Right to Education Act of 2009. Every child between 6 and 14 years of age is entitled to free education. Private schools must reserve seats for children from economically weaker sections.
Important Right to Property: Till 1978, we had a seventh Fundamental Right—the Right to Property. It was removed from the list by the 44th Amendment Act.

III. Right Against Exploitation

This protects citizens from being forced to work against their will or without proper payment.

  • Children under the age of 14 cannot be employed in any form of labour.
  • Adolescents (ages 15 to 18) cannot be employed in hazardous occupations, such as mines and factories.

IV. Right to Freedom of Religion

India is a multi-religious society. The government maintains a secular approach, meaning the State does not treat any religion as an official state religion.

This right allows individuals to freely profess, practice, and propagate any religion of their choice.

V. Cultural and Educational Rights

To safeguard India’s vast cultural diversity, minorities are given the right to preserve and promote their unique language, script, and traditions. They can also establish and administer their own educational institutions.

VI. Right to Constitutional Remedies

Fundamental Rights are meaningless unless there is a mechanism to enforce them. This right grants citizens the power to move directly to the court, even the Supreme Court, to seek protection if any of their Fundamental Rights are violated by a state, group, or person.

4. Limitations to Exercising Our Rights

While Fundamental Rights are essential, they are not absolute. They must be exercised responsibly without harming others.

  • Rights can be restricted during emergencies (e.g., the right to form associations) for public safety.
  • Special laws like the Essential Services Maintenance Act (ESMA) may allow the temporary detention of individuals to maintain public order.

5. Fundamental Duties

Rights and duties go hand-in-hand—they are two sides of the same coin. While enjoying our rights, we have corresponding obligations towards society and the state.

Fact Initially, the Constitution only listed Rights. A list of 10 Fundamental Duties (Article 51A) was added by the 42nd Amendment in 1976. The 11th duty was added by the 86th Amendment Act in 2002.

Under Article 51A, it is the duty of every citizen of India to:

  • Abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals, the National Flag, and the National Anthem.
  • Cherish and follow the noble ideals of the freedom struggle.
  • Uphold and protect the sovereignty, unity, and integrity of India.
  • Defend the country and render national service when called upon.
  • Promote harmony and common brotherhood, and renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women.
  • Value and preserve our rich heritage and composite culture.
  • Protect the natural environment (forests, lakes, rivers, wildlife) and have compassion for living creatures.
  • Develop a scientific temper, humanism, and the spirit of inquiry and reform.
  • Safeguard public property and abjure (reject) violence.
  • Strive towards excellence in all individual and collective activities.
  • Provide opportunities for education to their child/ward between the age of 6 and 14 years (added in 2002).

Though these duties are not legally enforceable by courts, it is the moral obligation of every responsible citizen to perform them sincerely.
