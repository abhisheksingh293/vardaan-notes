Classification of Animals

1. Introduction to the Animal Kingdom

Earth is home to thousands of different animal species living across varied environments. From tiny ants to giant whales, they exhibit enormous diversity in shape and size. Despite these differences, all animals share key fundamental characteristics:

  • Heterotrophic Nutrition: Animals cannot prepare their own food. They must feed on plants or other animals for survival.
  • Locomotion: The majority of animals move from one place to another primarily in search of food, shelter, or to escape predators.
Concept

The Core Classification: The simplest and broadest way to classify the animal kingdom is based on the presence or absence of a backbone (vertebral column). Animals are divided into two primary categories: Invertebrates (without a backbone) and Vertebrates (with a backbone).

2. Invertebrates (Animals Without a Backbone)

Invertebrates lack a vertebral column. Based on their distinct biological features, they are divided into eight major groups (phyla):

2.1 Porifera (Pore-bearing animals)

  • They have a porous body bearing many tiny pores to draw water into their body cavity and one large pore to pass it out.
  • They are primarily aquatic, mostly found in the sea, while a few occur in fresh water.
  • They are sessile, meaning they are fixed to some object or to the bottom of ponds/oceans.
  • Common Example: Sponge.

2.2 Cnidaria or Coelenterata (Hollow sac-like animals)

  • These are aquatic animals with a sac-like body and only one opening, the mouth.
  • The mouth is surrounded by finger-like projections called tentacles, used for swimming and catching food.
  • Their bodies are radially symmetrical (can be divided into two identical halves along any plane).
  • Common Examples: Jellyfish, Hydra, Sea-anemone.

2.3 Platyhelminthes (Flatworms)

  • Their bodies are thin, flat, and ribbon-like.
  • Most of them live as parasites inside the bodies of other animals (hosts) where they absorb pre-digested food.
  • Common Examples: Tapeworm and Liver fluke.
Important

Parasitic Warning! Tapeworms occur in the human intestine and can grow to a massive length of about six metres! Similarly, the roundworm (Ascaris) commonly infects children who eat with unwashed hands or consume unwashed fruits and vegetables. Always maintain proper hygiene!

2.4 Nematoda (Roundworms)

  • Their body is rounded and unsegmented.
  • Except for a few species, most are minute organisms.
  • They mostly live as parasites in the bodies of animals, including humans.
  • Common Example: Ascaris (Roundworm in humans).

2.5 Annelida (Segmented worms)

  • Their body is composed of rings or segments.
  • They breathe through a soft, moist body wall.
  • They have special excretory organs known as nephridia.
  • Common Examples: Earthworm and Leech.

2.6 Arthropoda (Animals with jointed legs)

This is the largest phylum. Their bodies are divided into three regions: head, thorax, and abdomen. They possess jointed legs and their body is covered by a tough covering of chitin. Arthropods are further divided into four classes:

  • Crustacea: Head and thorax are fused. Many jointed legs. Mainly aquatic. (e.g., Crab, prawn, lobster).
  • Myriapoda: Body divided into many segments with 1-2 pairs of legs per segment. (e.g., Centipede, millipede).
  • Insecta: Head bears antennae/feelers. Have 3 pairs of legs. (e.g., Ant, housefly, butterfly).
  • Arachnida: Head and thorax fused. Have 4 pairs of legs and no antennae. (e.g., Spider, scorpion).

2.7 Mollusca (Soft-bodied shelled animals)

  • They possess a soft, unsegmented body.
  • The body is generally enclosed in a hard shell for protection.
  • They move with the help of a muscular foot.
  • Common Examples: Snail, slug, pearl oyster, octopus.

2.8 Echinodermata (Spiny-skinned animals)

  • Their bodies are rough, spiny, and radially symmetrical.
  • They move using special structures called tube feet.
  • They are mainly marine animals.
  • Common Examples: Starfish, Sea urchin.

3. Vertebrates (Phylum Chordata)

Animals containing a backbone (vertebral column) are classified as vertebrates. They are highly developed and are divided into five distinct classes:

3.1 Class Pisces (Fishes)

  • They are strictly aquatic animals featuring a streamlined body (narrow at both ends) for easy movement in water.
  • They possess scales covering their body.
  • They swim using fins and breathe dissolved oxygen in water through gills.
  • Common Examples: Rohu, Catla, Shark, Electric ray fish.

3.2 Class Amphibia (Frogs and Toads)

  • Amphibians are unique as they can live in water as well as on land.
  • Their bodies are covered by a slimy and slippery skin.
  • They breathe through lungs (on land) and through their moist skin (in water). They lay their eggs only in water.
  • Common Examples: Frog and Toad.

3.3 Class Reptilia (Lizards and Snakes)

  • They mostly live on land (though turtles and crocodiles live in water).
  • Their skin is completely dry and scaly.
  • They breathe exclusively through lungs.
  • Females lay soft-shelled eggs on land. Most have four short legs for crawling (snakes lack legs entirely).
  • Common Examples: Lizard, snake, crocodile, tortoise.

3.4 Class Aves (Birds)

  • Their bodies are completely covered with feathers, and they possess wings to fly.
  • Their bones are hollow and light in weight, an adaptation for flight.
  • Their jaws are provided with a horny beak and they have no teeth.
  • Females lay hard-shelled eggs. Legs bear scales and toes have claws.
  • Common Examples: Peacock, parrot, pigeon, sparrow.
Fact

Flightless Birds: While birds are characterized by having wings, not all birds can fly! The Ostrich, which happens to be the largest bird on Earth, along with the Penguin and Kiwi, are examples of flightless birds.

3.5 Class Mammalia (Milk-nourishing animals)

  • Mammals are the most advanced animals. Their body is covered with hair.
  • They possess projecting external ears and usually have a tail (with exceptions like humans).
  • Instead of laying eggs, they give birth to young ones (babies).
  • The young ones suckle milk from mammary glands present in the mothers.
  • Common Examples: Human, cow, dog, tiger, elephant, whale.
