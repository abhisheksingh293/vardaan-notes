Chapter 4: Light Energy

1. Introduction to Light

Light is an invisible form of energy that gives us the sensation of sight. We cannot see light itself, but objects around us are seen only in the presence of light.

Concept Rectilinear Propagation of Light: The property of light traveling in a straight line is called rectilinear propagation. This is the reason why opaque objects cast shadows.
Fact Light does not require a material medium to travel. It can travel through a vacuum at a staggering speed of 3 × 10⁸ m/s (or 300,000 km/s).

2. Reflection of Light

When light falls on the surface of a highly polished object (like a mirror), it bounces back into the same medium. This phenomenon is called the reflection of light.

Terms Related to Reflection

  • Incident Ray: The ray of light that strikes the surface.
  • Point of Incidence: The specific point on the surface where the incident ray strikes.
  • Reflected Ray: The ray of light that bounces back in the same medium after striking the surface.
  • Normal: A perpendicular line drawn at the point of incidence.
  • Angle of Incidence (i): The angle between the incident ray and the normal.
  • Angle of Reflection (r): The angle between the reflected ray and the normal.
Important Laws of Reflection:
1. The angle of incidence is always equal to the angle of reflection (∠i = ∠r).
2. The incident ray, the reflected ray, and the normal at the point of incidence, all lie in the same plane.

Real vs. Virtual Images

A real image is an image that can be obtained on a screen. A virtual image (like the one formed by a plane mirror) cannot be obtained on a screen because the reflected rays only appear to meet.

3. Image Formation by a Plane Mirror

When you stand in front of a plane mirror, you see an image that looks exactly like you, but with a specific inversion.

Characteristics of the Image

  1. The image is upright or erect.
  2. The image is virtual.
  3. The image is of the same size as the object.
  4. The image is formed as far behind the mirror as the object is in front of it.
  5. The image is laterally inverted.
Concept Lateral Inversion: The interchange of the left and right sides in the object and image formed by a plane mirror. This is why the word AMBULANCE is written laterally inverted on the front of vehicles, so drivers ahead can read it correctly in their rearview mirrors.

Uses of a Plane Mirror

  • As a dressing mirror.
  • In a barber's shop to see the back of the head.
  • By opticians to increase the effective length of a small room.
  • In instruments like the periscope and kaleidoscope.

4. Types of Reflection

Depending on the nature of the surface, reflection can be categorized into two types:

  • Regular Reflection: Occurs on smooth, highly polished surfaces (like a plane mirror). A parallel beam of incident light remains parallel after reflection.
  • Irregular (Diffused) Reflection: Occurs on rough surfaces. A parallel beam of incident light gets scattered in different directions. This is how we are able to see objects around us from any angle.

5. Colours of Light

The white light from the sun is actually made up of several colours.

Speed of Light in Different Media

While light travels at 3 × 10⁸ m/s in air or vacuum, it slows down in denser transparent media:

  • Speed in Water: 2.25 × 10⁸ m/s
  • Speed in Glass: 2 × 10⁸ m/s

Constituent Colours of White Light

When white light passes through a glass prism, it splits into a band of seven prominent colours. This order can be remembered using the acronym VIBGYOR (Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red).

Primary and Secondary Colours

  • Primary Colours: Red, Green, and Blue. These cannot be obtained by mixing other colours. Mixing all three yields white light.
  • Secondary Colours: Obtained by mixing two primary colours.
    • Red + Green = Yellow
    • Green + Blue = Cyan
    • Blue + Red = Magenta
Important Colour of Opaque Objects: An opaque object appears to have a certain colour because it reflects that specific colour of light and absorbs the rest.

Example: A red rose appears red in white light because it reflects red and absorbs other colours. If placed in green light, it absorbs the green light, reflects nothing, and thus appears black.
