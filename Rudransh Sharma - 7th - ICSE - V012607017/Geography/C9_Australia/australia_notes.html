Australia

1. Introduction and Location

Australia is a unique landmass as it is the world's only continent that is also a country. Because it is surrounded by water bodies on all sides, it is frequently referred to as an island continent. In terms of area, it is the smallest continent and the 6th largest country in the world.

The continent lies entirely in the eastern and southern hemispheres. The Tropic of Capricorn passes directly through its centre, dividing it almost in half.

Fact

English captain James Cook landed in Australia in 1770 and found the land suitable for habitation, paving the way for the first English settlers, though Portuguese, Dutch, and Spanish sailors already knew of its western and northern coasts.

Concept

Australasia or Oceania: This is a collective geographical term that refers to the regions comprising Australia, New Zealand, and the adjoining island groups in the Pacific Ocean.

Boundaries of Australia

  • North: Separated from the New Guinea Island by the Torres Strait.
  • South: Separated from the island of Tasmania by the Bass Strait.
  • West & South: Bordered by the vast expanse of the Indian Ocean.
  • East & North: Bordered by the Pacific Ocean.

2. Political Divisions

Australia is a federation composed of six states and two mainland territories. The national capital is Canberra, located in the Australian Capital Territory.

  • New South Wales: The oldest and most populous state. Its capital, Sydney, is the largest city and most important port in Australia.
  • Victoria: A major industrial hub with its capital at Melbourne.
  • Queensland: Located in the north-east, its capital is Brisbane.
  • South Australia: Known for its famous seaport capital, Adelaide.
  • Western Australia: A major industrial region with its capital at Perth.
  • Tasmania: The island state located to the south, with its capital at Hobart.
  • Northern Territory: A mainland territory with its capital at Darwin.

3. Major Physical Features

Australia is the flattest and lowest-lying continent in the world, with a mean elevation of about 300 m above sea level. Only about 5% of the continent exceeds 600 m in height. It is divided into six main physical divisions:

I. The Western Plateau

  • Covers one-third of the continent and is made of very old, hard rocks, similar to India's Deccan Plateau.
  • It features mountain ranges like Hamersley, Musgrave, and Barkly Tableland.
  • Due to scanty rainfall, this region contains vast desert areas covering 18% of the mainland, including the Great Victoria Desert, the Great Sandy Desert, and the Gibson Desert.

II. The Central Lowland

  • Extends from the Gulf of Carpentaria in the north down to the southern coast.
  • Contains the Murray-Darling Basin, which drains the southern part and forms Australia's largest river system.
  • Contains the Eyre Basin, a region of inland drainage where rivers flow into lakes instead of the sea. Lake Eyre is the lowest point on the continent at 15 m below sea level.

III. The Eastern Highlands

  • Features the Great Dividing Range, which runs parallel to the eastern Pacific coast from the Cape York Peninsula down to Tasmania.
  • The southern portion forms the Australian Alps, home to Mt. Kosciuszko, the highest peak in Australia.

IV. The Great Artesian Basin

  • Lying between the Eastern Highlands and Central Lowland, it is one of the largest and deepest artesian basins in the world.
  • The basin consists of a sandstone base covered by sedimentary rock.
Concept

Artesian Well: In the Great Artesian Basin, wells are bored into the ground from which groundwater rushes out automatically. Because of the high natural water pressure, the water does not need to be pumped out, making it an invaluable resource for cattle rearers in dry areas.

V. The Great Barrier Reef

Located off the north-eastern coast, this is the world's largest coral reef. It stretches over 2,500 km and is even visible from outer space.

Important

The Great Barrier Reef is formed from the skeletal remains of millions of tiny marine organisms called coral polyps. It acts as a massive natural barrier, restricting the movement of ships nearing the coast.

VI. The Rift Valley of Australia

  • Located in the southern part of the continent, it is formed by deep cracks or fissures in the Earth's crust.
  • This faulting created deep valleys between raised land sections, containing salt lakes such as Lake Torrens.

4. Sheep Rearing in Australia: A Case Study

Sheep rearing is a major form of animal husbandry in Australia. The country boasts one-seventh of the world's sheep population but produces an astonishing one-fourth of the world's wool, making it the largest exporter of wool globally.

Rearing Regions and Breeds

  • The Dry Interiors: Regions with temperate grasslands (like parts of the Murray-Darling Basin) are primarily used to rear sheep for wool. The Merino breed makes up 70% of the flocks here because they produce high-grade wool. Farming in these dry areas is extensive.
  • The Humid East Coast: In areas like New South Wales where the climate is humid and pastures are richer, sheep such as the Lincoln and Romney Marsh breeds are raised primarily for meat. Farms here are smaller and mixed with arable farming.

Life on a Sheep Station

Sheep are reared on massive farms that cover thousands of hectares, known as sheep stations.

  • Paddocks: The station is divided by fences into smaller fields called paddocks, each holding between 2,000 to 3,000 sheep.
  • Workers: The owner of the station is called a grazier or pastoralist. They hire paid workers called jackaroos. Jackaroos move the sheep between paddocks, treat injuries, mend fences, and protect the flocks from wild animals and rabbits.
  • Facilities: A station is a self-sufficient unit. It includes homesteads for families, sheds for shearing, silos for grain, windmills for energy, and even landing strips for light aircraft used by the flying doctors.
Fact

The shearing season begins in early summer. Professional sheep shearers use electrical machines to remove the fleece rapidly. They travel from station to station during this busy period. The fleece is then cleaned, sorted, and pressed into bales for export from ports like Sydney and Melbourne.
