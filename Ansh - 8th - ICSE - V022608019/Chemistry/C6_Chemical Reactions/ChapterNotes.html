Chemical Reactions

A Chemical reaction is the process which transforms one or more substances into new substances with new properties. The substances which take part in a chemical reaction are called reactants and the substances which are formed due to a chemical reaction are called products.

For example, in the chemical reaction of metallic zinc with dilute sulphuric acid, zinc and sulphuric acid are reactants, whereas zinc sulphate and hydrogen gas are products:

Zn + H2SO4 → ZnSO4 + H2

1. Necessary Conditions for Chemical Reactions

A chemical reaction takes place when one or more of the following conditions are satisfied:

  • Close physical contact: It takes place only when the reactants are in contact with each other.
  • Solution form: Some substances react with each other only when they are mixed in the solution form.
  • Heat: Most of the chemical reactions take place by heating the reactants together.
  • Light: Some chemical reactions take place in the presence of sunlight.
  • Catalyst: The speed of the chemical reactions can be increased or decreased by using a catalyst.
  • Electricity: Some chemical reactions take place during the passage of electric current.
  • Pressure: Some chemical reactions take place at high pressure.
Fact

A chemical reaction is normally accompanied by any one of the following changes:

  • Change of state
  • Change of colour
  • Evolution of a gas
  • Formation of a precipitate
  • Change of temperature

2. Exothermic and Endothermic Processes

Whenever a chemical reaction takes place, heat is either absorbed or evolved. Accordingly, the reactions are termed as endothermic or exothermic.

Exothermic Reactions

A reaction is called exothermic when heat is given out during a chemical reaction. In such cases, the temperature of the surrounding increases.

  • Example 1: When hydrogen combines with oxygen to form water.
    2H2(g) + O2(g) → 2H2O(l) + Heat
  • Example 2: Burning of coke in air to form carbon dioxide gas.
    C(s) + O2(g) → CO2(g) + Heat
  • Example 3: In our body, glucose undergoes combustion to form CO2 and H2O, releasing energy.
    C6H12O6(aq) + 6O2(g) → 6CO2(g) + 6H2O(l) + Heat
  • Example 4: Neutralisation reaction between an acid and a base is always an exothermic reaction.
    HCl(aq) + NaOH(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H2O(l) + Heat

Endothermic Reactions

A chemical reaction is said to be endothermic if the reaction involves absorption of heat.

  • Example 1: When nitrogen and oxygen combine to form nitric oxide (NO).
    N2(g) + O2(g) + Heat → 2NO(g)
  • Example 2: The reaction between hot coke (C) and steam (H2O).
    C(s) + H2O(g) + Heat → CO(g) + H2(g)
  • Example 3: Decomposition of limestone (CaCO3).
    CaCO3(s) + Heat → CaO(s) + CO2(g)
Concept

Bond Energy: During a chemical reaction, the bonds between the atoms of reactants are broken and new bonds are formed to give the products. Energy is required to break the bonds and when new bonds are formed, energy is released.

  • If energy absorbed during the breaking up of bonds is greater than the energy released during formation of new bonds, the reaction is endothermic.
  • If the energy released during formation of new bonds is greater than the energy absorbed during breaking up of bonds, the reaction is exothermic.

3. Types of Chemical Reactions

1. Combination Reaction (Synthesis)

The reactions in which two or more substances combine to form a single substance under suitable conditions are called combination reactions. A + B → AB

  • Between Two Elements:
    N2(g) + 3H2(g) → 2NH3(g) + Heat
  • Between Two Compounds:
    CaO(s) + H2O(l) → Ca(OH)2(aq) + Heat
  • Between Compound and Element:
    2CO(g) + O2(g) → 2CO2(g)

2. Decomposition Reaction

The reactions in which a single substance decomposes into two or more simple substances under suitable conditions are called decomposition reactions. AB → A + B. They are mostly endothermic.

  • Thermal Decomposition: A single substance breaks down when heated.
    CaCO3(s) → CaO(s) + CO2(g)
  • Electrolytic Decomposition: Electric current is passed through an aqueous solution.
    2H2O(l) → 2H2(g) + O2(g)
  • Photo Decomposition: Takes place in the presence of sunlight (e.g. used in black and white photography).
    2AgCl(s) → 2Ag(s) + Cl2(g)
Important

Reactivity Series of Metals (Decreasing Order):
Potassium (K) > Sodium (Na) > Calcium (Ca) > Magnesium (Mg) > Aluminium (Al) > Zinc (Zn) > Iron (Fe) > Nickel (Ni) > Tin (Sn) > Lead (Pb) > Hydrogen (H) > Copper (Cu) > Mercury (Hg) > Silver (Ag) > Gold (Au) > Platinum (Pt)

3. Displacement Reactions (Single-displacement)

The chemical reactions in which one element of a reactant takes the place of another element present in another reactant. A + BC → AC + B

A more reactive metal displaces the less reactive metal from its salt solution. Highly reactive metals can also displace hydrogen from dilute acids.

  • Zn(s) + CuSO4(aq) → ZnSO4(aq) + Cu(s)
  • Fe(s) + CuSO4(aq) → FeSO4(aq) + Cu(s) (Iron replaces copper)
  • Zn(s) + H2SO4(aq) → ZnSO4(aq) + H2(g) (Zinc replaces hydrogen)

4. Double-displacement Reaction

A chemical reaction in which one component each of both the reacting molecules get exchanged to form new products. AB + CD → AD + CB

These generally involve reactions between ions of compounds in aqueous solutions. Any reaction that produces a precipitate is called a precipitation reaction.

  • AgNO3(aq) + NaCl(aq) → AgCl(s) + NaNO3(aq) (White precipitate of AgCl forms)
  • BaCl2(aq) + Na2SO4(aq) → BaSO4(s) + 2NaCl(aq) (White precipitate of BaSO4 forms)

4. Oxidation and Reduction Reactions (Redox)

These reactions are very common and invariably involve both oxidation and reduction simultaneously.

  • Oxidation: The addition of oxygen to a substance OR the removal of hydrogen from a substance.
  • Reduction: The addition of hydrogen to a substance OR the removal of oxygen from a substance.

The substance which gives oxygen or removes hydrogen is an oxidising agent. The substance which gives hydrogen or removes oxygen is a reducing agent. In a reaction, the oxidising agent undergoes reduction, and the reducing agent undergoes oxidation.

Concept

Modern Definition of Redox: The modern definition involves the transfer of electrons.
Oxidation is the loss of electrons.
Reduction is the gain of electrons.

5. Oxides

Oxides are binary compounds formed by the reaction of oxygen with other elements. Based on acid-base characteristics, oxides are classified as:

  • Acidic Oxides (Acid Anhydrides): Non-metals react with oxygen to form acidic oxides. When combined with water, they produce acids. They react with bases to produce salts.
    Example: SO2 + H2O → H2SO3
  • Basic Oxides (Basic Anhydrides): Metals react with oxygen to give basic oxides. They react with water to produce hydroxides and with acids to produce salts.
    Example: CaO + H2O → Ca(OH)2
  • Amphoteric Oxides: Metallic oxides which show both basic as well as acidic properties. They react with acids to show basic properties and with alkalis to show acidic properties.
    Example: ZnO + 2HCl → ZnCl2 + H2O
  • Neutral Oxides: Oxides which show neither basic nor acidic properties, meaning they do not form salts when reacted with acids or bases.
    Examples: Carbon monoxide (CO), Nitrous oxide (N2O), Nitric oxide (NO).
