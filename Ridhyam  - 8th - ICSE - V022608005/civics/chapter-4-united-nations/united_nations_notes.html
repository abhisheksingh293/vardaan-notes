United Nations

1. Background and The Need for an Organization

The League of Nations

After the First World War, an organization called the League of Nations was established in Geneva (Switzerland) in 1920.

Its primary aim was to establish peace among the nations of the globe and prevent future wars. However, it ultimately failed in its mission, leading to the outbreak of the Second World War.

Concept International Organization: An entity created by treaty, involving multiple nations, meant to ensure global security, resolve conflicts diplomatically, and prevent catastrophic wars between sovereign states.

The Second World War and its Impact

The Second World War proved to be far more disastrous than the First World War, primarily due to the introduction of highly destructive weapons.

  • For the first time, atomic bombs were utilized in warfare.
  • The United States of America dropped two atomic bombs on the Japanese cities of Hiroshima and Nagasaki in August 1945.
  • The untold devastation brought about by these bombings forced global leaders to think of setting up a new, much more powerful organization to protect mankind from future wars.

2. Key Declarations and the Formation of the UNO

During the Second World War, Allied leaders, notably US President Franklin D. Roosevelt and British Prime Minister Winston Churchill, held sincere discussions that laid the groundwork for the United Nations Organisation (UNO).

London Declaration (June 1941)

Representatives of Great Britain, Canada, New Zealand, Australia, and South Africa met in London. They discussed establishing a world organization that would be significantly more effective than the previous League of Nations.

Atlantic Charter (August 1941)

Issued by US President Franklin D. Roosevelt and British PM Winston Churchill, the Atlantic Charter recognized the principle of sovereign equality for all states, regardless of whether they were big or small.

The Washington Declaration (January 1, 1942)

The representatives of 26 Allied States met at Washington. They pledged to continue the war collectively against the Axis Powers and promised not to conclude peace separately.

Fact It was during the Washington Declaration that the term "United Nations" was used for the very first time! The declaration also firmly recognized the principles previously laid out in the Atlantic Charter.

The Moscow Declaration (October 1943)

The Foreign Ministers of Great Britain, the USA, the erstwhile USSR, and China met in Moscow. They issued a Four Nations Declaration which formally recognized the extreme urgency of establishing an international organization for global peace.

Tehran Conference

The heads of the governments of Britain (Winston Churchill), the USA (Franklin Roosevelt), and the erstwhile USSR (Joseph Stalin) met to solidify the foundational plans for the UNO.

3. Objectives and Principles

Based on the discussions from these historical declarations, the United Nations was established with the following primary aims:

  • To maintain international peace and security across the globe.
  • To develop friendly relations among nations based on the respect for the principle of equal rights and self-determination of peoples.
  • To achieve international cooperation in solving global economic, social, cultural, or humanitarian problems.
  • To be a center for harmonizing the actions of nations in the attainment of these common ends.
Important The UN Charter was officially signed on June 26, 1945, by 50 nations. The UNO formally came into existence on October 24, 1945. This is why October 24 is celebrated globally every year as UN Day.
