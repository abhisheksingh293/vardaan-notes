CHEMICAL REACTIONS

1. Introduction

In our surroundings, we see different types of changes taking place. These changes may be physical or chemical.

Concept A Chemical Reaction is a process in which matter changes into a new substance or substances with entirely different properties.

A chemical reaction takes place due to the breaking of chemical bonds existing between the atoms of reactants and the formation of new bonds with a rearrangement of atoms to make new substances.

Important In a chemical change, no new atoms are formed. One element does not change into another element; only the rearrangement of atoms takes place in a chemical reaction.

2. Reactants and Products

  • Reactants: The substances which take part in a chemical reaction.
  • Products: The new substances which are produced during the chemical reaction.

3. Characteristics of Chemical Reactions

Generally, a chemical reaction takes place with certain characteristics or observable changes.

Fact Key Characteristics:
  • Change of Colour: e.g., heating Red Mercuric Oxide turns it silvery. (2HgO → 2Hg + O2)
  • Evolution of a Gas (Effervescence): e.g., Zinc reacting with dilute Sulphuric acid releases Hydrogen gas. (Zn + H2SO4 → ZnSO4 + H2↑)
  • Evolution or Absorption of Heat: Temperature changes during the reaction (Exothermic and Endothermic).

4. Types of Chemical Reactions

I. Combination Reactions (Synthesis)

A reaction in which two or more substances combine to form a single new product.

General Form: A + B → AB

  • Example: Carbon burning in oxygen to form Carbon dioxide (C + O2 → CO2).

II. Decomposition Reactions

A reaction in which a single compound breaks down into two or more simpler substances.

General Form: AB → A + B

Important Thermal Decomposition: A decomposition reaction brought about by heat. (e.g., Calcium carbonate decomposing into Calcium oxide and Carbon dioxide on heating: CaCO3 → CaO + CO2).

III. Displacement Reactions

A reaction in which a more reactive element displaces a less reactive element from its compound.

General Form: A + BC → AC + B

  • This is governed by the Activity Series of Metals, where metals are arranged in decreasing order of their reactivity.
  • Example: Iron displaces Copper from Copper sulphate solution (Fe + CuSO4 → FeSO4 + Cu).

IV. Double Displacement Reactions

A reaction in which two compounds react by exchanging their radicals to form two new compounds.

General Form: AB + CD → AD + CB

Types of Double Displacement:

  • Precipitation Reactions: Where an insoluble solid (precipitate) is formed.
  • Neutralization Reactions: Reaction between an acid and a base to form salt and water.

5. Catalytic Reactions

Concept A Catalyst is a substance which alters the rate of a chemical reaction without itself undergoing any permanent chemical change.

Reactions involving a catalyst are known as Catalytic Reactions.

  • Positive Catalyst: Increases the rate of reaction.
  • Negative Catalyst: Decreases the rate of reaction.

6. Energy Changes in Chemical Reactions

Exothermic Reactions

Chemical reactions in which heat is evolved (released) to the surroundings. The temperature of the mixture rises.

  • Example: Burning of carbon, respiration, and neutralization reactions.

Endothermic Reactions

Chemical reactions in which heat is absorbed from the surroundings. The temperature of the mixture falls.

  • Example: Photosynthesis, decomposition of calcium carbonate.

7. Oxides and their Classification

An oxide is a binary compound formed when oxygen combines with another element (metal or non-metal).

Fact Classification of Oxides:
  1. Acidic Oxides: Formed by non-metals. They dissolve in water to give acids (e.g., CO2, SO2).
  2. Basic Oxides: Formed by metals. They react with acids to form salt and water (e.g., Na2O, MgO).
  3. Amphoteric Oxides: React with both acids and bases to form salt and water (e.g., ZnO, Al2O3).
  4. Neutral Oxides: Formed by non-metals but show neither acidic nor basic properties (e.g., H2O, CO).

8. Indicators

Concept Indicators are complex organic compounds which indicate whether a substance is acidic or basic by changing their colour.

Common Indicators and their colour changes:

Indicator Colour in Acidic Solution Colour in Basic Solution Neutral Colour
Litmus (Paper/Solution) Red Blue Purple
Methyl Orange Red/Pink Yellow Orange
Phenolphthalein Colourless Pink Colourless
