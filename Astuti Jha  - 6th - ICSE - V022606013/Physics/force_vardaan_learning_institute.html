Force

1. Introduction to Force

Concept A force is that cause which changes the state of a body (either the state of rest or the state of motion or the direction of motion) or changes the size or shape of the body. Force is a push or pull.

It is important to remember that a force cannot be seen; we can only see or feel the effect of a force. A force does not change the mass of the body on which it is applied.

Fact The S.I. unit of force is Newton (N). Its gravitational units are kgf and gf.

1 kgf = 1000 gf and 1 kgf = 10 N (nearly).

2. Effects of a Force

When a force is applied on a body, it can have the following main effects:

  • Can move a body originally at rest: A stationary ball on the ground moves when it is kicked.
  • Can stop a moving body: A moving bicycle is stopped by applying the brakes, which act in a direction opposite to the motion.
  • Can make a moving body move faster: The speed of a bicycle increases when more force is applied to the pedals.
  • Can slow down a moving body: A moving vehicle slows down when a force is applied in the opposite direction.
  • Can change the direction of motion: A hockey player applies force with a stick to change the direction of the ball.
  • Can change the shape or size: Stretching a rubber string increases its length (force as a stretch). Squeezing an inflated balloon changes its shape (force as a squeeze).

3. Kinds of Forces

Depending upon the application of forces, they are classified into two broad categories:

A. Contact Forces

The force which acts on bodies by making actual physical contact is called a contact force. Examples include:

  • Muscular Force: Force applied manually as a push or a pull.
  • Frictional Force: Force exerted by a surface on a body in contact with it, acting in the direction opposite to motion.
  • Tension Force: Force in a string when a load is suspended from it.
  • Force of Reaction: The upward force normal to the surface when a body is placed on a table.

B. Non-Contact Forces (Forces from a distance)

Forces which act on bodies with absolutely no physical contact are called non-contact forces. Examples include:

  • Gravitational Force: The attractive force between two bodies at a distance. For instance, the Earth attracts all objects. The weight of a body is the force by which the Earth attracts it.
  • Electrostatic Force: The force between two charged bodies (like charges repel, unlike charges attract).
  • Magnetic Force: The force acting between the poles of two magnets.

4. Force of Friction

Concept Friction is the force that opposes the relative motion between the two surfaces in contact with each other. The word friction is derived from the Latin word "fricare" which means "to rub".

Key Characteristics and Effects of Friction

  • Friction opposes motion: It makes it difficult to push heavy objects over rough surfaces.
  • Acts in the opposite direction: Friction always acts in a direction opposite to the direction of motion.
  • Produces heat: When you rub your palms together or when machine parts move against each other, heat is produced.
  • Causes wear and tear: The soles of our shoes and tyres of vehicles wear out over time due to friction.

Factors Affecting Friction

  • Smoothness of the Surface: Friction is higher on rough surfaces and lower on smooth surfaces.
  • Nature of Medium: The friction between two solids is maximum. It is less between a solid and liquid, and least between a solid and a gas.
  • Weight of the moving body: The greater the weight of the moving body on a surface, the more the force of friction.

5. Kinds of Friction

  • Static Friction: The maximum opposing force exerted by a surface on a stationary body when an external force is applied, just before the body begins to slide.
  • Sliding Friction: The force exerted by a surface when a body is actively sliding over it.
  • Rolling Friction: The force that opposes the rolling motion of an object over a surface.
Important Always remember the relationship between the magnitudes of these frictions:
Static Friction > Sliding Friction > Rolling Friction.

6. Advantages and Disadvantages of Friction

Disadvantages

  • It opposes the motion of a body and decreases efficiency.
  • It causes wear and tear in the moving parts of machinery.
  • It produces unnecessary heat, wasting energy.

Advantages (Why friction is necessary)

  • Without friction, we would not be able to walk on a road.
  • Vehicles would fail to move as wheels would spin in place without moving ahead.
  • Friction helps in supporting a ladder against a wall.
  • Brakes in vehicles function solely because of friction.
  • It is essential for simple tasks like lighting a matchstick or writing on a blackboard with chalk.

7. Methods to Change Friction

Ways of Reducing Friction

  • Making surfaces smooth: By polishing or cementing surfaces.
  • Using lubricants: Oil, grease, or fine powder fill the fine pores on a surface to decrease interlocking.
  • Using ball bearings: Replaces sliding friction with rolling friction (which is much lower) in axles and hubs.
  • Streamlining: Designing bodies (like airplanes, boats, birds) with sharp or pointed front ends to easily cut through air or water.

Ways of Increasing Friction

  • Making surfaces rough: Sand is often sprinkled on slippery roads.
  • Using dry surfaces: Wet surfaces reduce friction; keeping them dry maintains a better grip.
  • Increasing weight: A heavier body develops a stronger grip with the surface it moves on.
