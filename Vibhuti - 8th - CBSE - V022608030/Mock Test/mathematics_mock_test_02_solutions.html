SECTION A
Q1-Q3: Square & Square Roots
[1 Mark Each]
1. Which of the following numbers would end with digit 1?
Answer: (d) \( 109^2 \)
Explanation: A number ends in 1 if its unit digit is 1 or 9. The unit digit of 109 is 9, so \( 9^2 = 81 \), ending in 1.
2. The square root of 12.96 is:
Answer: (a) 3.6
Explanation: \( \sqrt{1296} = 36 \). Since there are 2 decimal places in the number, there will be 1 decimal place in its square root. Thus, 3.6.
3. How many non-square natural numbers lie between \( 99^2 \) and \( 100^2 \)?
Answer: (b) 198
Explanation: The number of natural numbers between squares of two consecutive numbers \( n \) and \( n+1 \) is \( 2n \). Here \( n=99 \), so \( 2 \times 99 = 198 \).
Q4-Q6: Cube & Cube Roots
[1 Mark Each]
4. The ones digit of the cube of 23 is:
Answer: (c) 7
Explanation: The unit digit is determined by \( 3^3 = 27 \). Hence, it ends in 7.
5. The value of \( \sqrt[3]{-1000} \) is:
Answer: (b) -10
Explanation: \( (-10) \times (-10) \times (-10) = -1000 \). The cube root of a negative number is negative.
6. Which of the following numbers is a perfect cube?
Answer: (c) 8000
Explanation: \( 8000 = 20 \times 20 \times 20 \). The others do not have prime factors in perfect triplets.
Q7-Q9: Exponents & Radicals
[1 Mark Each]
7. The value of \( (2^0 + 3^0) \times 4^0 \) is:
Answer: (c) 2
Explanation: Any non-zero number to the power of 0 is 1. \( (1 + 1) \times 1 = 2 \times 1 = 2 \).
8. The value of \( (64)^{-1/3} \) is:
Answer: (b) \( 1/4 \)
Explanation: \( (64)^{-1/3} = (4^3)^{-1/3} = 4^{-1} = \frac{1}{4} \).
9. In standard form, the number 0.0000564 is:
Answer: (b) \( 5.64 \times 10^{-5} \)
Explanation: Moving the decimal point 5 places to the right gives a negative exponent of -5.
Q10-Q11: Direct & Indirect Variation
[1 Mark Each]
10. If x and y vary directly, and x = 10 when y = 15. What is x when y = 30?
Answer: (a) 20
Explanation: Direct variation: \( \frac{x_1}{y_1} = \frac{x_2}{y_2} \Rightarrow \frac{10}{15} = \frac{x_2}{30} \Rightarrow x_2 = \frac{10 \times 30}{15} = 20 \).
11. If 12 workers can build a wall in 50 hours, how many hours will 15 workers take?
Answer: (a) 40
Explanation: Inverse variation: \( 12 \times 50 = 15 \times t \Rightarrow t = \frac{600}{15} = 40 \text{ hours} \).
Q12-Q13: Algebraic Identities
[1 Mark Each]
12. The value of \( (x+5)(x-5) \) is:
Answer: (a) \( x^2 - 25 \)
Explanation: Using the identity \( (a+b)(a-b) = a^2 - b^2 \Rightarrow x^2 - 5^2 = x^2 - 25 \).
13. The coefficient of x in the expansion of \( (x-3)^2 \) is:
Answer: (c) -6
Explanation: \( (x-3)^2 = x^2 - 6x + 9 \). The coefficient of x is -6.

SECTION B (Very Short Answer)
21. Least number to multiply 252 for a perfect square.
[2 Marks]
Prime factorization of 252:
\( 252 = 2 \times 126 = 2 \times 2 \times 63 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 21 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 7 \)
\( 252 = 2^2 \times 3^2 \times 7 \).
Here, 7 does not have a pair. Therefore, the least number by which 252 must be multiplied to make it a perfect square is 7.

[2 Marks]
Speed and time are inversely proportional.
\( S_1 \times T_1 = S_2 \times T_2 \)
\( 80 \times 1.5 = 60 \times T_2 \)
\( 120 = 60 \times T_2 \Rightarrow T_2 = \frac{120}{60} = \mathbf{2 \text{ hours}} \).

[2 Marks]
\( 104^2 = (100 + 4)^2 \)
Using identity \( (a+b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2 \), where a=100, b=4.
\( = (100)^2 + 2(100)(4) + (4)^2 \)
\( = 10000 + 800 + 16 = \mathbf{10816} \).

27. Simplify exponents.
[3 Marks]
\[ \frac{3^{-5} \times 10^{-5} \times 125}{5^{-7} \times 6^{-5}} \] Express bases as primes: \( 10 = 2 \times 5 \), \( 125 = 5^3 \), \( 6 = 2 \times 3 \).
\[ = \frac{3^{-5} \times (2 \times 5)^{-5} \times 5^3}{5^{-7} \times (2 \times 3)^{-5}} \] \[ = \frac{3^{-5} \times 2^{-5} \times 5^{-5} \times 5^3}{5^{-7} \times 2^{-5} \times 3^{-5}} \] Cancel out common terms (\( 3^{-5} \) and \( 2^{-5} \)):
\[ = \frac{5^{-5+3}}{5^{-7}} = \frac{5^{-2}}{5^{-7}} \] \[ = 5^{-2 - (-7)} = 5^{-2+7} = \mathbf{5^5} \text{ (or 3125)} \]
28. Profit Loss: Marked Price and Discount.
[3 Marks]
Cost Price (CP) = ₹ 1200.
Marked Price (MP) = CP + 25% of CP = \( 1200 + (\frac{25}{100} \times 1200) = 1200 + 300 = \mathbf{₹ 1500} \). [1 Mark]
Discount = 10% of MP = \( \frac{10}{100} \times 1500 = ₹ 150 \).
Selling Price (SP) = MP - Discount = \( 1500 - 150 = \mathbf{₹ 1350} \). [1 Mark]
Profit = SP - CP = 1350 - 1200 = ₹ 150.
Profit % = \( \frac{\text{Profit}}{\text{CP}} \times 100 = \frac{150}{1200} \times 100 = \mathbf{12.5\%} \). [1 Mark]
29. Simplify Polynomial expression.
[3 Marks]
Expression: \( (3x - 4y)(2x + 5y) - (x - y)(x + y) \)
Part 1: \( (3x)(2x) + (3x)(5y) - (4y)(2x) - (4y)(5y) = 6x^2 + 15xy - 8xy - 20y^2 = 6x^2 + 7xy - 20y^2 \) [1.5 Marks]
Part 2: \( (x - y)(x + y) = x^2 - y^2 \) (Using identity)
Subtracting Part 2 from Part 1:
\( = (6x^2 + 7xy - 20y^2) - (x^2 - y^2) \)
\( = 6x^2 + 7xy - 20y^2 - x^2 + y^2 \)
\( = \mathbf{5x^2 + 7xy - 19y^2} \). [1.5 Marks]
30. Rational Number word problem.
[3 Marks]
Let the numerator be \( x \). Then, denominator is \( x + 8 \).
Original Number = \( \frac{x}{x+8} \).
According to the question:
\( \frac{x + 17}{(x + 8) - 1} = \frac{3}{2} \)
\( \frac{x + 17}{x + 7} = \frac{3}{2} \) [1 Mark]
Cross multiplying:
\( 2(x + 17) = 3(x + 7) \)
\( 2x + 34 = 3x + 21 \) [1 Mark]
\( 34 - 21 = 3x - 2x \Rightarrow x = 13 \).
Numerator = 13, Denominator = 13+8 = 21.
The rational number is \( \frac{13}{21} \). [1 Mark]

SECTION D (Long Answer)
32. Greatest 4-digit perfect square.
[5 Marks]
The largest 4-digit number is 9999. [1 Mark]
Let's find the square root of 9999 by long division to find the remainder.
Grouping: \( \overline{99}\overline{99} \)
- \( 9 \times 9 = 81 \). Remainder = 18. Bring down 99. New dividend = 1899.
- Divisor becomes 18_. The largest digit is 9.
- \( 189 \times 9 = 1701 \). [2 Marks]
Remainder = \( 1899 - 1701 = 198 \).
This shows \( 99^2 \) is less than 9999 by 198.
Required number = \( 9999 - 198 = \mathbf{9801} \). [1 Mark]
Its square root is 99. [1 Mark]
33. Food provision (Inverse Variation).
[5 Marks]
Original students (\( S_1 \)) = 125, Days (\( D_1 \)) = 24.
Total provision = \( 125 \times 24 = 3000 \) student-days.

(i) If 25 more join:
New number of students (\( S_2 \)) = 125 + 25 = 150.
Inverse variation: \( S_1 \times D_1 = S_2 \times D_2 \)
\( 3000 = 150 \times D_2 \Rightarrow D_2 = \frac{3000}{150} = \mathbf{20 \text{ days}} \). [2.5 Marks]

(ii) If 25 leave:
New number of students (\( S_3 \)) = 125 - 25 = 100.
\( S_1 \times D_1 = S_3 \times D_3 \)
\( 3000 = 100 \times D_3 \Rightarrow D_3 = \frac{3000}{100} = \mathbf{30 \text{ days}} \). [2.5 Marks]
34. Algebraic Identities.
[5 Marks]
Given \( a - \frac{1}{a} = 7 \)
(i) Find \( a^2 + \frac{1}{a^2} \):
Squaring both sides: \( (a - \frac{1}{a})^2 = 7^2 \)
\( a^2 + \frac{1}{a^2} - 2(a)(\frac{1}{a}) = 49 \)
\( a^2 + \frac{1}{a^2} - 2 = 49 \Rightarrow a^2 + \frac{1}{a^2} = 49 + 2 = \mathbf{51} \). [2.5 Marks]

(ii) Find \( a^4 + \frac{1}{a^4} \):
Squaring the result from (i): \( (a^2 + \frac{1}{a^2})^2 = 51^2 \)
\( a^4 + \frac{1}{a^4} + 2(a^2)(\frac{1}{a^2}) = 2601 \)
\( a^4 + \frac{1}{a^4} + 2 = 2601 \Rightarrow a^4 + \frac{1}{a^4} = 2601 - 2 = \mathbf{2599} \). [2.5 Marks]

\( 5(x) + 2(3x) + 1(160 - 4x) = 300 \)
\( 5x + 6x + 160 - 4x = 300 \) [1 Mark]

(iii) Solving the equation:
