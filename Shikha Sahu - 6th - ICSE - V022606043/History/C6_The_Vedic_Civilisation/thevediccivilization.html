The Vedic Civilisation

1. Introduction & Origin of the Aryans

The Vedic Civilisation flourished in India from 1500 BCE to 600 BCE. It is the earliest civilisation in Indian history for which written records are available. The people who developed this civilisation were known as the Aryans.

Origin of the Aryans

  • There are multiple theories about their origin (India, Arctic region, Central Europe).
  • The most widely accepted theory by historians is that they migrated from Central Asia around 1500 BCE.
  • They first settled in the region of seven rivers known as Sapta Sindhu (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Satluj, and Saraswati). They called this land Brahmavarta (land of gods).
  • Later, they moved towards the Gangetic valley and southwards to the Vindhya mountains. They called this new settled land Aryavarta.

2. Sources of Information

We learn about the Vedic Age through two main sources: Literary and Archaeological.

Literary Sources

  • The Four Vedas: Rig Veda (the oldest, composed ~1500 BCE), Sama Veda, Yajur Veda, and Atharva Veda.
  • Other Texts: Upanishads, Brahmanas, Puranas, and Aranyakas.
  • The Epics: The Ramayana (written by Sage Valmiki) and the Mahabharata (written by Sage Ved Vyas).
Concept

What does "Veda" mean? The word 'Veda' means knowledge. Initially, hymns were composed in praise of gods and passed on orally from generation to generation before being written down.

Archaeological Sources

  • Excavations at sites like Hastinapura and Atranjikhera.
Fact

Painted Grey Ware (PGW): A highly distinctive type of pottery found during archaeological excavations that provides major evidence about the lifestyle of the Vedic Age people.

3. Early Vedic Period (1500 BCE - 1000 BCE)

Political Organisation

  • The Aryans were divided into tribes.
  • The head of the tribe was a king called the Rajan, selected for his bravery and wisdom.
  • The Rajan was assisted by the Senani (commander of the army) and the Purohit (chief priest).
  • The village head was known as the Gramini.
  • Assemblies: The King's power was kept in check by two assemblies: the Sabha (senior citizens and elders) and the Samiti (general assembly of all village people).

Social Life & Status of Women

  • The society was patriarchal. The eldest male member was the head of the family, known as the Grihapati or Gahapati. A family was known as a Kul.
  • Status of Women: Women were highly respected, educated, and treated on par with men.
  • They actively participated in assemblies and debates. Famous women scholars of this time include Gargi, Maitreyi, Lopamudra, and Ghosha.
  • Practices like Sati and the purdah system did not exist. Widow remarriage was accepted.
Important

The Flexible Varna System: In the Early Vedic Period, society was divided into four varnas based on occupation, not birth. Brahmins (teachers/priests), Kshatriyas (rulers/warriors), Vaishyas (farmers/merchants), and Shudras (artisans/labourers). It was highly flexible.

Religion

  • Aryans worshipped forces of nature (Sun, Earth, Sky, Wind, Rain, Fire).
  • Important Gods: Indra (God of Rain/Storm/War - the most important god), Agni (Fire), Varun (Water), and Surya (Sun).
  • There were no temples or idols. Yajnas (sacrifices) were performed in the open with simple offerings and chanting of hymns.

Economic Life

  • Initially, they were pastoral (cattle herders). The cow was considered a symbol of wealth and status. A person owning many cows was called a Gopati.
  • Agriculture, weaving, carpentry, and chariot making were major occupations.
  • Transport: The Aryans introduced the horse to India, using them to draw chariots.

4. Later Vedic Period (1000 BCE - 600 BCE)

Political Changes

  • Small tribes expanded into large kingdoms like Kuru, Kosala, Magadha, and Kashi.
  • The Rajan gained absolute power and his position became hereditary. He assumed titles like Samrat and performed grand sacrifices like Ashwamedha and Rajasuya.
  • The assemblies (Sabha and Samiti) lost their importance and were dominated by the rich aristocracy.
  • A new officer, the Sangrihitri (tax collector), gained importance.

Social Changes

  • Decline in Women's Status: Women were considered inferior to men. They were denied education, restricted to household duties, and widow remarriage was looked down upon.
  • Rigid Varna System: The Varna system became strictly based on birth (hereditary). Brahmins and Kshatriyas became privileged classes (paying no taxes), while Shudras faced severe discrimination and untouchability.

Religious & Economic Changes

  • Religion: Early gods lost importance. Brahma (Creator), Vishnu (Preserver), and Shiva (Destroyer) became the supreme deities. Religion became complex, and priests gained immense power.
  • Economy: Iron was discovered (around 1000 BCE), leading to better agricultural tools and weapons. Trade flourished, and cities began to grow.

5. Education: The Gurukul System

Education was transmitted orally through the Guru-Shishya tradition. A student (Brahmachari) lived with his Guru in a Gurukul.

Subjects Taught & The 4 Stages of Life

Students learned Vedic literature, mathematics (Anka ganita, Rekha ganita, Beej ganita), astronomy, medicine, and martial arts (archery, sword fighting). Human life was divided into four distinct stages (Ashramas):

  • 1. Brahmacharya: The first stage dedicated to receiving education and attaining knowledge.
  • 2. Grihastha: The second stage focusing on marriage and fulfilling family responsibilities.
  • 3. Vanprastha: The third stage of retiring to the forest for meditation, giving up materialistic life.
  • 4. Sanyasa: The final stage of giving up all worldly ties to preach moral values.
