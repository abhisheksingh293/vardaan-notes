Section D: Answer the following questions in brief.

1. Difference between a real image and a virtual image. Give one example of a virtual image.

Answer:
Real Image: It is formed when reflected light rays actually meet at a point. It can be obtained on a screen (e.g., image formed on a cinema screen).
Virtual Image: It is formed when reflected light rays appear to meet at a point but do not actually meet. It cannot be obtained on a screen.
Example of a virtual image: The image of our face formed by a plane mirror.

2. How does changing the distance of the object, from a convex mirror, affect the size and nature of the image formed by it?

Answer: Changing the distance of an object from a convex mirror does not change the nature of the image. The image formed by a convex mirror is always virtual, erect, and diminished. However, as the object is brought closer to the mirror, the size of the image increases slightly, but it always remains smaller than the object.

4. Where should we keep an object, in front of a concave mirror, so as to get a virtual and magnified image of it?

Answer: To get a virtual and magnified image, the object should be placed very close to the concave mirror, specifically between the pole (P) and the principal focus (F) of the mirror.

5. The magnified image, of an object, appears to be formed, behind the mirror, by an apparent intersection of the reflected rays. What can you say about (a) nature of the image (b) the type of mirror used?

Answer:
(a) Nature of the image: The image is Virtual and Erect.
(b) Type of mirror used: It is a Concave mirror. (A convex mirror also forms virtual images behind the mirror, but they are always diminished, never magnified).

6. Rohit suggested to his father to fix a 'security mirror' which would provide them with a wider view of the surrounding of their shop. Name the type of mirror that Rohit must have suggested to his father. Also mention the basic characteristics of the image formed by this type of mirrors.

Answer:
Type of mirror: Convex mirror.
Basic characteristics of the image: The image formed by a convex mirror is always virtual, erect, and diminished (smaller than the object). It provides a wider field of view.

7. State the type of mirror that the following items/objects would correspond to:

  • (a) inside of a stainless steel bowl: Concave mirror
  • (b) reflector of a torch: Concave mirror
  • (c) a polished shining wooden surface: Plane mirror
  • (d) the shiny outer surface of the bicycle bell: Convex mirror

Section E: Answer the following questions.

1. State the laws of reflection. Describe an activity to show that incident ray, reflected ray and the normal, at the point of incidence, all lie in same plane.

Answer:
Laws of Reflection:
1. The angle of incidence is always equal to the angle of reflection ($\angle i = \angle r$).
2. The incident ray, the reflected ray, and the normal at the point of incidence, all lie in the same plane.

Activity: Fix a white sheet of paper on a drawing board. Draw a straight line and place a plane mirror vertically on it. Direct a narrow beam of light from a laser torch towards the mirror at an angle along the paper. You will observe the reflected ray also travelling along the paper. Draw a normal (perpendicular line) at the point where the light hits the mirror. Since the incident ray, reflected ray, and the drawn normal are all flat against the paper sheet, this proves they all lie in the same plane.

4. Three persons are standing at points P, Q and R in front of a plane mirror. Can 'P' see himself in the mirror? Also can he see the image of Q and R in the mirror?

Answer: No, person 'P' cannot see himself in the mirror. This is because light rays from 'P' falling on the limited length of the mirror will reflect far to the right, and will not reach 'P's own eyes.
Yes, 'P' can see the images of 'Q' and 'R'. Light rays starting from 'Q' and 'R' will strike the mirror and reflect outwards towards the left side where 'P' is standing, allowing those reflected rays to reach 'P's eyes.

5. With the help of a suitable diagram, explain the meaning of the following terms for a concave mirror. (a) centre of curvature (b) pole (c) principal axis (d) focus.

Answer:
(a) Centre of curvature (C): It is the centre of the hollow glass sphere of which the spherical mirror is a part.
(b) Pole (P): It is the geometric centre of the reflecting surface of the spherical mirror.
(c) Principal axis: It is the straight imaginary line passing through the pole (P) and the centre of curvature (C) of the mirror.
(d) Focus (F): It is a point on the principal axis where a beam of light rays, initially parallel to the principal axis, actually meet after reflection from the concave mirror.

6. What type of mirror is used in the following cases? Is the image formed virtual and erect in each case?

  • (a) 'make up' mirror: Concave mirror. Yes, the image is virtual and erect (when the face is held very close to the mirror).
  • (b) 'rear view' mirror of vehicles: Convex mirror. Yes, the image is always virtual and erect.
  • (c) reflecting type telescope: Concave mirror. No, the image formed inside the telescope tube is real.
  • (d) shaving mirror: Concave mirror. Yes, the image is virtual and erect (when the face is held close).
