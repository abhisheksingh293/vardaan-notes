Chemical Substances and Processes

D. Answer the following questions in brief.

1. What are elements?

An element is a pure substance made up of only one kind of atom. It cannot be broken down into simpler substances by chemical means.

2. Give the chemical symbol of iron and chlorine.
  • Iron: Fe
  • Chlorine: Cl
3. Write the chemical formulae of the following compounds: (a) Aluminium oxide (b) Zinc acetate
  • (a) Aluminium oxide: Al₂O₃
  • (b) Zinc acetate: Zn(CH₃COO)₂
Important

Always write the valency correctly while forming chemical formulae. For Aluminium Oxide, Al is +3 and O is -2, cross-multiplying gives Al₂O₃.

4. Balance the following equation: Ca(OH)₂ + HCl ⟶ CaCl₂ + H₂O

The balanced equation is:
Ca(OH)₂ + 2HCl ⟶ CaCl₂ + 2H₂O

5. What does a chemical equation represent?

A chemical equation represents a chemical reaction using symbols and formulae of the substances involved.

6. What are reactants and products in a chemical equation?
  • Reactants: The substances that take part in a chemical reaction (written on the left side).
  • Products: The new substances formed as a result of the reaction (written on the right side).
7. Give one example of a combination reaction.

Burning of carbon in oxygen to form carbon dioxide.
C + O₂ ⟶ CO₂

E. Answer the following questions.

1. Write the steps involved in writing the chemical formula of calcium phosphate.
  1. Write the symbols of positive and negative radicals: Ca and PO₄.
  2. Write their valencies on the top right: Ca²⁺ and PO₄³⁻.
  3. Cross-multiply the valencies and write them as subscripts: Ca₃(PO₄)₂.
2. How is a chemical change different from a physical change?

In a physical change, only the physical properties change, and no new substance is formed (e.g., melting of ice). In a chemical change, a new substance with entirely different properties is formed, and it is usually irreversible (e.g., rusting of iron).

Fact

Chemical changes are permanent and involve the absorption or release of energy.

3. 'Neutralisation reaction is a chemical change.' Justify this statement with the help of an example.

Yes, it is a chemical change because new substances (salt and water) are formed with completely different properties from the reacting acid and base.
Example: NaOH (base) + HCl (acid) ⟶ NaCl (salt) + H₂O (water).

4. Define a decomposition reaction and give an example of the same.

A reaction in which a single compound breaks down into two or more simpler substances is called a decomposition reaction.
Example: Heating of Calcium Carbonate.
CaCO₃ ⟶ CaO + CO₂ (on heating)

5. Classify the following reactions into different types, giving reason.
  • (a) CaO(s) + SiO₂(g) ⟶ CaSiO₃(s)
    Combination Reaction: Two simpler substances combine to form a single product.
  • (b) KOH(aq) + HCl(aq) ⟶ KCl(aq) + H₂O(l)
    Neutralisation (Double Displacement) Reaction: An acid and a base react to form salt and water.
  • (c) Cu(s) + 2AgNO₃(aq) ⟶ Cu(NO₃)₂(aq) + 2Ag(s)
    Displacement Reaction: A more reactive metal (Cu) displaces a less reactive metal (Ag) from its salt solution.
  • (d) BaCO₃(s) (heat) ⟶ BaO(s) + CO₂(g)
    Decomposition Reaction: A single compound breaks down into simpler substances upon heating.
