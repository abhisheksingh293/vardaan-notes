Earth, Moon, and the Sun

1. The Solar System and its Motions

The sun, the planets, and their moons are all key members of our Solar System. In space, celestial bodies exhibit continuous, predictable movements.

  • Earth and the other planets revolve around the sun in fixed paths called orbits.
  • Similarly, the moon revolves around Earth in its own fixed orbit.
  • Alongside revolving, planets and moons also spin about themselves.

2. Rotation of Earth

Have you ever watched a top spin? It stays in one place but constantly turns around itself. Earth exhibits a similar spinning motion.

Key Terms of Rotation

  • Rotation: The type of motion in which an object spins on its own axis. Earth rotates from west to east.
  • Axis: An imaginary straight line running from the geographic North Pole to the South Pole, around which Earth rotates. Earth's axis is slightly tilted at an angle of 23.5°.
  • Equator: The imaginary circular line passing through the centre of Earth, dividing it into two equal halves.
  • Northern Hemisphere: The top half of Earth, from the Equator to the North Pole.
  • Southern Hemisphere: The bottom half of Earth, from the Equator to the South Pole.
Concept

Apparent Motion: When we move, objects around us appear to be moving in the opposite direction, even though they are stationary. Because Earth rotates from west to east, the sun, moon, and stars appear to move across the sky from east to west.

Effects of Rotation

Earth takes about 24 hours to complete one full rotation. This causes two primary phenomena:

  1. Occurrence of Day and Night: As Earth rotates, only one half faces the sun at any given moment. The side receiving sunlight experiences daytime, while the side in shadow experiences night.
  2. Apparent Motion of Stars: The sun, moon, and stars appear to rise in the east and set in the west.
Fact

The Pole Star (Polaris): While other stars seem to move across the sky, the Pole Star always points to the North and appears fixed. This happens because it is almost directly aligned with Earth's axis of rotation in the Northern Hemisphere.

3. Revolution of Earth

While Earth rotates on its axis, it simultaneously travels around the sun. This movement is called revolution.

  • Earth moves around the sun in a fixed, elongated (elliptical) path called an orbit.
  • It takes approximately 365 days and 6 hours (roughly one year) to complete one revolution.
  • Earth revolves in an anti-clockwise direction.

Effects of Revolution

The revolution of Earth, combined with its spherical shape and tilted axis, leads to significant changes throughout the year:

  1. Changing View of the Night Sky: Because Earth moves along its orbit, our position in space changes. Stars and constellations visible in winter (like Orion) may disappear into the sun's glare during summer.
  2. Seasons on Earth: Different parts of Earth experience different seasons at different times. Because of Earth's 23.5° tilt, one hemisphere is often leaning toward the sun while the other leans away.

How Seasons Work

  • June: The Northern Hemisphere is tilted towards the sun, receiving intense heat for a longer duration, resulting in summer. The Southern Hemisphere is tilted away, experiencing winter.
  • December: The Northern Hemisphere is tilted away from the sun, receiving less heat for a shorter duration, resulting in winter. The Southern Hemisphere is now tilted towards the sun, experiencing summer.
  • March & September: Neither hemisphere is tilted directly towards or away from the sun. Both receive moderate heat, resulting in spring or autumn.
  • The Equator: Regions near the Equator experience very little change in sunlight intensity throughout the year. Days and nights remain nearly equal (about 12 hours each) all year round, and there are no distinct seasonal differences.

4. Solstice and Equinox

Throughout Earth's yearly journey around the sun, four key positions determine the extremes of our daylight and seasons.

  • Solstice: Twice a year, the poles tilt the most towards or away from the sun. These positions mark the longest or shortest days of the year for a given hemisphere.
    • Summer Solstice (June 21): The Northern Hemisphere experiences its longest day and shortest night.
    • Winter Solstice (December 22): The Northern Hemisphere experiences its shortest day and longest night.
  • Equinox: Twice a year, neither hemisphere is tilted towards or away from the sun. Day and night are of equal length all over Earth.
    • Spring Equinox (March 21): Marks equal day and night in the Northern Hemisphere as it transitions into Spring.
    • Autumn Equinox (September 23): Marks equal day and night in the Northern Hemisphere as it transitions into Autumn.

5. Eclipses

An eclipse occurs when either Earth or the moon comes in the way of the sun and blocks the sunlight, casting a shadow on the other body. Even though the sun is massive, the moon can block its light because of apparent size—the moon is much smaller, but it is much closer to Earth than the sun.

Solar Eclipse

A solar eclipse happens when the moon comes directly between the sun and Earth, blocking sunlight from reaching our planet.

  • Total Solar Eclipse: Occurs where the moon completely blocks the sunlight, casting the area into total darkness.
  • Partial Solar Eclipse: Occurs where the moon only partially covers the sun.
Important

Safety Warning: Never look directly at a solar eclipse with the naked eye! The intense light can damage your eyes and lead to permanent blindness. Always use special eclipse-watching glasses or view it under adult supervision using indirect projection.

Lunar Eclipse

A lunar eclipse takes place when Earth comes directly between the sun and the moon. Earth blocks the sunlight, casting its shadow over the moon.

  • Total Lunar Eclipse: The entire moon enters the darkest part of Earth's shadow. The moon often appears reddish in color during this time.
  • Partial Lunar Eclipse: Only a part of the moon is in Earth's darkest shadow, leaving the rest visible.
  • Note: Unlike a solar eclipse, it is completely safe to look at a lunar eclipse with the naked eye.
