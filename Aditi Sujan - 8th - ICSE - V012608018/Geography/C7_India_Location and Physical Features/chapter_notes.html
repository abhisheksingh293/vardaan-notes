India-Location and Physical Features

1. Location and Identity

India is the seventh largest country in the world and the second most populous country after China. Despite having diverse physical features, stark climatic contrasts, and varied vegetation, India possesses a distinct geographical identity.

India and some of her neighbouring countries together form the Indian subcontinent.

Concept

Strategic Position: India is situated at the head of the Indian Ocean (the only ocean named after a country). The mainland is a large peninsula bound by the Arabian Sea in the west, the Bay of Bengal in the east, and the Indian Ocean in the south. This facilitates trade with both eastern and western countries.

2. Extent and Standard Meridian

  • Latitudinal Extent: 8°4'N to 37°6'N.
  • Longitudinal Extent: 68°7'E to 97°25'E.
  • Hemispheres: It lies entirely in the Northern and Eastern hemispheres. The Tropic of Cancer passes through the middle of the country.
  • Dimensions: North to South (Kashmir to Kanyakumari) is about 3,200 km. West to East (Gujarat to Arunachal Pradesh) is about 2,900 km.
  • Extreme Points: The southernmost point of the country is Indira Point (submerged during the 2004 tsunami), while the northernmost point is Indira Col in Kashmir.
Important

Indian Standard Time (IST): A longitudinal difference of 29° leads to a time variation of almost 2 hours between Arunachal Pradesh (east) and Gujarat (west). For uniformity, the local time of 82°30'E longitude (Standard Meridian of India) is used across the entire country.

3. Neighbouring Countries & Political Divisions

India shares its boundaries with several nations:

  • North-west: Pakistan and Afghanistan.
  • North: China, Nepal, and Bhutan.
  • East: Bangladesh and Myanmar.
  • South (across the sea): Sri Lanka and Maldives.

For administrative purposes, India is divided into 28 states and 8 union territories. The capital of India is New Delhi. States have been primarily divided based on the languages spoken.

4. Physical Divisions of India

India has six major physical divisions which shape its geography and economy.

A. The Northern Mountains

An arc-shaped chain stretching for 2,500 km, serving as the highest mountain chain in the world.

  • Karakoram Range: Northernmost range. Contains K2 (Mount Godwin Austen), the second-highest peak globally, and large glaciers like Siachen and Baltoro.
  • The Himalayas: Consist of three parallel ranges:
    • Greater Himalayas (Himadri): Average height of 6,000 m. Contains the highest peaks like Mt. Everest, Kanchenjunga, Nanda Devi. Source of rivers like Ganga and Yamuna.
    • Lesser Himalayas (Himachal): Average height of 4,500 m. Known for hill stations (Shimla, Manali, Darjeeling).
    • Outer Himalayas (Shivalik): Height less than 1,250 m. Made of loose rocks, prone to earthquakes. Contains broad valleys known as duns (e.g., Dehradun).
Fact

The Purvachal Mountains: The eastern extensions of the Himalayas in North-east India. They include the Patkai Bum, Naga, Garo, Khasi, Jaintia, Mizo, and Lushai Hills.

B. The Northern Plains

Also known as the Indo-Gangetic Plain, it lies south of the mountains. Formed by alluvium deposits, it is extremely fertile and divided into three sections:

  • Indus Plain: Drained by the Indus and its tributaries (Chenab, Jhelum, Ravi, Beas, Satluj). Mainly located in Punjab and Haryana in India.
  • Ganga Plain: Covers UP, MP, Bihar, and West Bengal. Drained by Ganga and tributaries like Yamuna, Ghaghra, Kosi, and Son.
  • Brahmaputra Plain: Located in Assam. Drained by the Brahmaputra (called Tsangpo in Tibet).

The Ganga and Brahmaputra rivers form the Sunderban delta, the largest delta in the world, before flowing into the Bay of Bengal.

C. The Peninsular Plateau

The oldest landmass of the Indian subcontinent, made of hard igneous and metamorphic rocks, rich in minerals.

  • Malwa Plateau: Bound by Aravalli (NW) and Vindhyas (S). Rivers like Chambal and Betwa originate here.
  • Deccan Plateau: South of the Narmada Valley. Bordered by the Western Ghats (continuous hills, source of Narmada/Tapi) and Eastern Ghats (broken hills, source of Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri).
  • Deccan Trap: North-western part made of lava sheets. Rich in black soil, excellent for cotton cultivation.

D. The Thar Desert

Located north-west of the Malwa Plateau and west of the Aravalli Hills in Rajasthan. It is a region of inland drainage (rivers disappear into the sand or salt lakes). The Luni is the only large river, and Lake Sambhar is an important saltwater lake.

E. The Coastal Plains

  • Western Coastal Plain: Narrow strip bordering the Arabian Sea. Divided into the Konkan Coast (north) and Malabar Coast (south). Contains estuaries and lagoons (backwaters).
  • Eastern Coastal Plain: Wider plain bordering the Bay of Bengal. Divided into Northern Circars (north) and Coromandel Coast (south). Features large river deltas.

F. The Islands

  • Lakshadweep Islands: Located in the Arabian Sea, these are a group of coral islands.
  • Andaman and Nicobar Islands: Located in the Bay of Bengal, separated by the Ten Degree Channel. These are volcanic islands. They house Barren Island, India's only active volcano.
