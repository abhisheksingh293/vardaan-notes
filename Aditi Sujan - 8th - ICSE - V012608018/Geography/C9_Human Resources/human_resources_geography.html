Human Resources

1. Humans as a Resource

Human beings engaged in useful activities are called human resources. Human resources include the population as well as the physical and mental abilities of the people like health, education, and skill.

  • Humans are the most important of all resources because natural substances acquire resource value only when used by us.
  • The quality along with the quantity of the population of a nation determines the level of utilization of natural resources.
  • The economic development of a region or a country depends heavily on the development of its human resources.
Concept

Human Resource Development: The responsibility of honing the skills of the people lies with its nation. The nation should ensure proper provision of nutritious food, healthcare facilities, and basic education. Adequate training to improve skills and employment opportunities must be provided.

Impact of Population Growth

The growth of population is directly proportional to the economic development of a country. A healthy and educated population is the wealth of a nation.

  • Developing Nations: Have a high growth rate but often face challenges like a lower standard of living, scarcity of food, and lack of infrastructural, healthcare, and educational facilities.
  • Developed Nations: Generally have a lower growth rate and a higher standard of living.
Important

The Human Development Index (HDI) is a composite index used to rank countries into four tiers of human development. It uses indicators like life expectancy, education, and per capita income.

2. Distribution of Population in India

Asia is the most populous continent, and India is highly populated. The total population of India is estimated to be 1.38 billion, constituting about 17.8% of the world's population.

Geographical Distribution

The population is not evenly distributed across the country.

  • Top 5 Most Populous States: Almost half of India's population lives in just five states: Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Bihar, West Bengal, and Madhya Pradesh.
  • Uttar Pradesh is the most populous state (243 million).
  • Sikkim has the least population among states (0.7 million).
  • Among Union Territories, Delhi and Jammu & Kashmir are highly populated, while Lakshadweep has the minimum.
Fact

Density of Population: This is the number of persons living in a unit area. According to the 2011 Census, India's population density is 382 persons per sq km. Bihar has the highest density (1102 per sq km), while Arunachal Pradesh has the lowest (17 per sq km).

Categories of Population Density

  • Densely Populated States: Northern Plains (UP, Bihar, West Bengal, Punjab, Haryana) and coastal states (Kerala, Tamil Nadu). Reasons: flat plains, fertile soil, and abundant rainfall.
  • Moderately Populated States: Gujarat, Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Jharkhand, Odisha, Assam, etc. Reasons: rocky/undulating terrain, less fertile soil, moderate/low rainfall.
  • Sparsely Populated Regions: Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Rajasthan, Chhattisgarh, and North-East states. Reasons: rugged relief and inhospitable climatic conditions.

Sex Ratio

The sex ratio is a critical demographic indicator.

  • According to the National Family Health Survey-5, India achieved a favorable sex ratio for the first time: 1020 females for every 1000 males.
  • Reasons for improvement: High literacy, increasing employment among women, and high awareness.
  • Haryana has the most unfavorable sex ratio (877 females per 1000 males), primarily due to female infanticide, female foeticide, and lack of awareness.

Rural-Urban Distribution

  • According to the 2011 Census, around 37.7% of India's population is urban.
  • Goa has the maximum percentage of urban population (62%), followed by Mizoram, Tamil Nadu, and Kerala.
  • Himachal Pradesh has the maximum percentage of rural population (89%), followed closely by Bihar, Assam, and Odisha.

3. Role of Health and Education

A country's development takes into account not only its total production or per capita income but also the health status and education levels of its people.

Healthcare

A healthy workforce is a nation's asset. The government must ensure a balanced diet, adequate sanitation, and health facilities for all.

  • In India, death rates declined from 25 per 1000 (1951) to about 7.3 per 1000 (2016).
  • Life expectancy increased from 36.7 years (1951) to 68.35 years (2015).
  • This improvement is due to better public health, hygiene, prevention of infectious diseases, and modern medical methods.

Education

Education equips people with skills, prepares them for the job market, and aids mental and spiritual development. The Right to Education provides free and compulsory education to all children below 14 years.

4. Skilled and Unskilled Human Resource

Skilled Human Resource

Refers to people who have specialized training or a specific skill-set to perform a task. They have thorough knowledge and can work independently.

  • Examples: Physicians, mechanics, electricians, carpenters, tailors, chefs, computer programmers.

Unskilled Human Resource

Refers to people who do not have any special training or skill-set. They perform simple functions requiring no previous experience or independent decision-making.

  • Examples: Agricultural laborers, sweepers, cleaners, domestic maids, peons.
Concept

Impact on Socio-Economic Development: Skilled human resources make a significant positive impact on the economy. They help in the optimum use of resources, increase productivity, generate income, and positively influence birth rates and general health.

Fact

The Skill India Programme was launched in 2015 to train youth based on their knowledge and ability. It estimated training over 40 crore people by 2022 to reduce unemployment and contribute to industrial growth.
