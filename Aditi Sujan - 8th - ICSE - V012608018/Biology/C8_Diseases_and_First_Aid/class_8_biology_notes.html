DISEASES AND FIRST AID

1. Health and Disease

Concept

Health is defined as a state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being, and not merely an absence of disease or infirmity. Physical health and mental health are inter-related.

A person is considered healthy if they are not suffering from any disease, can work hard, interact well with people, enjoy leisure, and can adapt to the stresses of life. Conversely, Disease is a departure from normal health through structural or functional disorders of the body.

2. Categories of Diseases

Diseases are broadly classified into two major categories:

  • Communicable or Infectious Diseases: These diseases spread from an infected person to a healthy one. They are caused by disease-causing microorganisms called pathogens (such as viruses, bacteria, fungi, worms, and protozoa). Examples: Cholera, viral fever, chickenpox, malaria.
  • Non-communicable or Non-infectious Diseases: These diseases are not caused by germs and cannot spread from person to person (they are non-transmissible). They usually occur due to improper functioning of body organs. Examples: Diabetes, heart attack.

3. Spread of Communicable Diseases

Pathogens try to transfer from a patient to a healthy host to survive and multiply. They spread generally through two methods:

A. Direct Method

Diseases spread through direct physical contact with an infected person. Examples include measles, chickenpox, and fungal infections.

B. Indirect Method

This method involves the transmission of germs without direct physical contact:

  • Touching and Sharing Items: Using the same towel, handkerchief, bed, or utensils handled by a patient.
  • Contaminated Food and Water: Unwashed fruits/vegetables or food washed with contaminated water carry germs to the alimentary canal.
  • Droplet Infection: Germs of respiratory diseases pass into the air during coughing or sneezing. A healthy person inhales the same air. Examples: Tuberculosis (TB), common cold, measles, diphtheria.
Fact

Vectors are organisms that carry germs from a source of infection but themselves do not get the infection. For example, mosquitoes pick up germs while feeding on an infected person and transfer them to the next person they bite. Similarly, houseflies carry germs from garbage to human food.

4. Important Communicable Diseases

Name of Disease Causative Agent Symptoms Prevention / Treatment
Influenza Virus Cold, fever, and chills. Rest and isolation of the patient.
Measles Virus Nasal discharge, red watery eyes, fever, red papules on skin. Vaccination.
Malaria Protozoan (Plasmodium). Spreads via female Anopheles mosquito. Intermittent high fever, profuse sweating, headache, shivering. Prevent breeding of mosquitoes, wear full clothes.
Dengue Virus. Spreads via Aedes aegypti mosquito. Fever, headache, muscle/joint pain, skin rash. Prevent water stagnation, use mosquito repellents.
Chikungunya Virus. Spreads via Aedes mosquito. Fever, headache, and acute pain in joints. Mosquito control, avoid visiting common disease areas.
AIDS HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus). Loss of weight, destruction of the body's normal defense system against infection. Avoid sexual contact with infected persons, always use disposable syringes.

5. Preventive Measures, Vaccination & Immunization

Public Hygiene: Crucial for preventing diseases. It involves the proper disposal of domestic waste and human excreta. Sewage must be chemically treated before release into water bodies to prevent water-borne diseases.

Healthy Environment: Keep garbage in covered bins and do not allow water to stagnate around homes to stop vectors like flies and mosquitoes from breeding.

Important

The concept of Vaccination was conceived by Edward Jenner (1749-1823). He observed that milkmaids who got cowpox never contracted the deadly smallpox. He successfully tested this by injecting a boy with cowpox, which made him immune to smallpox. The term comes from the Latin word 'vacca' meaning cow.

Vaccination is the practice of artificially introducing germs or germ substances into the body to develop resistance to particular diseases (scientifically called prophylaxis). The substance introduced is called a Vaccine.

When injected, the vaccine stimulates White Blood Cells (WBCs) to produce antibodies against specific germs. Vaccines are prepared using four methods:

  1. Using Killed Germs: TAB vaccine for typhoid, Salk's vaccine for poliomyelitis, rabies vaccine.
  2. Using Living Weakened Germs: BCG vaccine for tuberculosis, measles vaccine.
  3. Using Fully Virulent Living Germs: Cowpox virus used for smallpox vaccination (smallpox is now eradicated).
  4. Using Toxoids: Extracts of toxins secreted by bacteria made harmless by formalin (used for diphtheria and tetanus).

Immunization is the process by which an individual's body is made resistant to a specific disease by vaccination. Examples include the Pulse Polio programme aiming to eradicate polio from India.

6. First Aid Guidelines

First Aid is the immediate care or help given to a patient during a medical emergency before they are taken to a doctor.

  • Burns:
    • Superficial: Pour cold water over the area, dry, and cover with sterile dressing. Apply oily substances (ointment, vaseline).
    • Deep Burns: Never use water. Cover directly with a sterile dressing.
    • Chemical Burns: Wash with running water for 10 minutes, then cover.
  • Bleeding: Raise the affected body part to minimize blood flow. Wash the cut, press with clean cotton, and apply mild antiseptic.
  • Fractures: Lay the victim down. Loosen clothing. Do not move the fractured part. Use a sling if the arm is fractured.
  • Objects in the Eye: Do not rub. Wash gently with clean water.
  • Unconsciousness: Lay the person comfortably. Loosen clothes and ensure fresh air circulation.
  • Swallowing Poison: Make the patient drink lots of salt water and try to induce vomiting.
  • Snake Bite: Squeeze out some blood. Tie a tourniquet tightly above the spot to prevent venom from spreading in the bloodstream.
  • Insect Stings (Bee/Wasp): Pull out the sting if visible. Squeeze out some blood and apply an alkali like baking soda.

7. Harmful Effects of Bad Habits

  • Tobacco: Smoking and chewing tobacco products increase the risk of mouth and lung cancer, high blood pressure, and heart attacks. It also harms non-smokers sitting nearby (passive smoking).
  • Alcohol: A slow poison that impairs judgment, damages the nervous system, blood vessels, stomach, and causes severe liver damage.
  • Drugs: Narcotic/psychotropic drugs (like morphine, cocaine, heroin) cause a loss of sensation, severe nervous system damage, respiratory issues, and often lead to early death due to addiction.
