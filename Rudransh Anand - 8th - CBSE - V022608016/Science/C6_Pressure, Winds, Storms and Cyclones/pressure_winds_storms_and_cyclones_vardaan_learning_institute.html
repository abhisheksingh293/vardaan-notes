Pressure, Winds, Storms and Cyclones

1. Pressure

The effect a force has on an object depends on two primary factors:

  • Magnitude of Force: The greater the force applied, the greater its effect.
  • Area of Contact: The area over which the force acts. The smaller the area of contact, the greater the effect of the force.
Concept

Pressure is defined as the force exerted per unit area. It is calculated to measure and describe the effect of a force acting on a surface.

Pressure = Force (F) / Area (A)

Note: The force used here is acting perpendicular to the area.

Units of Pressure

  • SI Unit: Newton per square metre (N/m²), commonly known as Pascal (Pa).
  • Practical Units for weather: millibar (mb) where 1 mb = 100 Pa, and hectopascal (hPa) where 1 hPa = 100 Pa.
Fact

Applications of Pressure in Daily Life:

  • Camels on sand: Camels have broad flat feet, increasing the area of contact, which reduces pressure and prevents them from sinking into soft sand.
  • Heavy trucks: They use multiple tyres (e.g., eight instead of four) to increase contact area, reducing pressure on the road surface.
  • Cutting tools: Knives and needles have sharp edges. This reduces the contact area, exerting greater pressure with less force for easier cutting or piercing.
  • Battle Tanks: Use broad caterpillar tracks instead of wheels to decrease pressure and avoid sinking in marshy terrain.

2. Pressure Exerted by Liquids

Liquids exert pressure not only on the base of their container but also sideways on the walls.

Characteristics of Liquid Pressure

  • Increases with Depth: The pressure exerted by a liquid increases as the depth increases. This is why water spurts out with greater force from a hole near the bottom of a container than one near the top.
  • Equal in All Directions at Same Depth: At any given depth, a liquid exerts equal pressure in all directions.
  • Depends on Liquid Column Height: The pressure depends on the height of the liquid column above it.
Important

Dams are built broader and thicker at the bottom than at the top because water pressure increases significantly with depth. The wider base provides stability against this immense pressure.

3. Atmospheric Pressure

The earth is surrounded by a layer of air called the atmosphere. The weight of this air column pressing down due to gravity exerts a force.

Concept

Atmospheric Pressure is the pressure exerted on an object by the weight of the column of air over it. At ground level, it is roughly equal to a force of 10 N per square cm.

We are not crushed by this enormous pressure because the fluids inside our bodies exert an equal and opposite outward pressure, canceling it out.

Characteristics and Factors

  • Air exerts pressure in all directions.
  • Air flows from a region of high pressure to a region of low pressure.
  • Altitude: Pressure is maximum at sea level and decreases as altitude increases (air becomes thinner).
  • Temperature: Air expands on heating. Atmospheric pressure decreases with an increase in temperature.
  • Humidity: Humid air is lighter than dry air, so pressure decreases with increased humidity.
Fact

Applications of Atmospheric/Liquid Pressure:

  • Deep-sea divers: Wear special suits to protect against crushing pressure at ocean depths.
  • Suction hooks: Stick to smooth surfaces because pressing them expels air, creating low pressure inside. The higher external atmospheric pressure holds it firmly in place.
  • Drinking straws & Syringes: Sucking/pulling creates a low-pressure area inside; higher atmospheric pressure outside pushes the liquid up.
  • Airplanes: Cabins are pressurized at high altitudes to prevent blood vessels from bursting due to low external pressure.

4. Formation of Wind and Pressure Variations

Wind is moving air. It moves because of pressure differences in the atmosphere.

Uneven Heating Causes Winds

When a region of the earth receives more sunlight, it heats up quickly. The air above it warms, becomes lighter, and rises, creating a low-pressure area. Cooler, heavier air from a high-pressure area rushes in to fill the space. This movement is wind.

  • Sea Breeze (Day): Land heats faster than sea. Hot air over land rises (low pressure). Cooler air from the sea (high pressure) moves towards land.
  • Land Breeze (Night): Land cools faster than sea. Air over the warmer sea rises (low pressure). Cooler air from the land moves towards the sea.
Important

Bernoulli's Principle related to winds: Increased wind speed results in reduced air pressure. High-speed winds blowing over a roof create low pressure above it, and higher pressure inside can blow the roof off.

5. Storms, Thunderstorms, and Lightning

Storms

Formed due to sudden, large differences in air pressure caused by uneven heating. Rapid rising of warm moist air causes continuous violent wind circulation. When water vapor condenses and falls as rain/snow accompanied by strong winds, it is called a storm.

Thunderstorms and Lightning

A weather condition marked by heavy rain, thunder, and lightning, frequent in hot, humid conditions.

  • Upward winds cause collisions between water droplets and ice crystals in clouds, producing static electricity.
  • Lighter ice (positive charge) moves up; heavier droplets (negative charge) move down.
  • Lightning: A powerful electric discharge between oppositely charged clouds, or a cloud and the ground. Heavy charges jump through the air, producing immense heat and a flash of light.
  • Thunder: The rapid heating and expansion of air surrounding the lightning strike produces this sound.
Concept

A Lightning Conductor is a safety device for tall buildings. It's a metal rod with spikes at the top and a copper plate buried deep in the ground. It provides a harmless path for a lightning strike to travel to the earth.

Safety During Thunderstorms

  • Seek shelter in a building with a lightning conductor or inside a closed car/bus.
  • Do NOT stand under tall trees (choose a short tree in a forest).
  • Avoid open areas and metallic objects (like umbrellas).
  • Stay out of water bodies.

6. Cyclones

Also known as hurricanes or typhoons, a cyclone is a violent, spinning storm characterized by strong spiraling winds, heavy rainfall, and potential tidal waves, developing over warm seas.

Formation of a Cyclone

  1. Sun heats sea water, warming the air and adding moisture (evaporation).
  2. Warm, moist air rises, condenses into rain, releasing heat which further warms surrounding air.
  3. More heated air rises, creating a strong low-pressure center.
  4. Due to earth's rotation, incoming cooler air spirals around this center instead of flowing straight in.
  5. The system intensifies as long as it's over warm water. It weakens upon hitting land due to friction and lack of moisture.

Structure and Effects

  • The Eye: The center of the storm is a calm, cloudless, low-pressure region with light winds.
  • Effects: Cyclones cause massive destruction via strong winds, heavy rain, and storm surges (tidal waves flooding coasts), damaging property and taking lives.
Important

Cyclone Forecasting: The Indian Meteorological Department uses satellites and Doppler Weather Radars (DWRs) to issue alerts 48 hours in advance and warnings 24 hours in advance.
