INDIA'S ELECTORAL SYSTEM

1. Universal Adult Franchise

The makers of the Indian Constitution gave every adult citizen an equal voice by establishing the system of Universal Adult Franchise. India held its first general election in 1951-52, breaking the mold by rejecting the idea that only a select few should vote.

Concept

Universal Adult Franchise: It means every adult citizen (aged 18 and above) has the right to vote. This right is granted without discrimination based on gender, caste, religion, wealth, or education.

  • Initially, the voting age was 21 years.
  • It was reduced to 18 years by the 61st Constitutional Amendment (1989).
  • Each citizen gets one vote, and every vote carries equal value.

Why is it Important?

  • Equality in practice: Every adult gets one vote, reinforcing constitutional equality.
  • Legitimacy: Leaders are chosen by all citizens, giving the government wider acceptance.
  • Inclusion: The marginalized (Dalits, Adivasis, women, minorities) have an equal say.
  • Reduction of elite control: Decisions aren't limited to the rich or educated. Power is widely shared.

2. Election Commission of India (ECI)

The Election Commission of India is an independent constitutional body established on 25 January 1950 (celebrated as National Voters' Day). Under Article 324, it is responsible for conducting free and fair elections to Parliament, state legislatures, and the offices of the President and Vice President.

Structure of ECI

  • National Level: Headed by the Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) and two Election Commissioners (ECs), appointed by the President. They serve up to 6 years or till age 65.
  • State Level: Elections are supervised by the Chief Electoral Officer (CEO), appointed by the ECI from senior civil servants.
  • District Level: The District Election Officer (DEO) (usually the District Magistrate) is assisted by Electoral Registration Officers (EROs) and Returning Officers (ROs).
Important

Functions of the ECI:

  • Conducts elections and holds by-elections.
  • Prepares and updates electoral rolls (voter lists) and issues EPIC (Voter ID Cards).
  • Announces election schedules, polling, and counting dates.
  • Registers political parties and allots election symbols.
  • Enforces the Model Code of Conduct (MCC).
  • Runs voter awareness programs like SVEEP (Systematic Voters' Education and Electoral Participation).

3. The Model Code of Conduct (MCC)

The MCC is a set of rules used by the ECI to keep campaigns free, fair, and peaceful. It takes effect as soon as the election schedule is announced and remains in force until results are declared.

  • General Conduct: No bribery, hate speech, or appeals to caste/religion.
  • Party in Power: No misuse of government vehicles or staff. No new policy announcements or grants that could influence voters.
  • Silence Period: Campaigning must stop 48 hours before polling ends.

4. Tools of Voting: EVMs & VVPAT

To meet the needs of India's massive electorate, traditional paper ballots were replaced with technology.

Fact

EVM (Electronic Voting Machine): A machine with buttons for each party's symbol. Introduced nationwide in 2004, it saves tonnes of paper, speeds up counting, and prevents invalid votes.

VVPAT (Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail): Introduced in 2013 (Nagaland). It prints a small slip for a few seconds showing the chosen candidate, allowing voters to verify their vote before it drops into a sealed box.

NOTA (None Of The Above): An option introduced in 2013 allowing voters to formally reject all available candidates.

Voting Process

  1. Verify: Name checked on the voter roll using an approved ID (like the Voter ID card/EPIC).
  2. Ink & Register: Finger is inked, and the voter signs the register.
  3. Vote: Voter goes to a private compartment, presses the EVM button. A Secret Ballot system ensures privacy and freedom from fear.
  4. Verify: Voter checks the VVPAT slip through the window.

5. Election to Lok Sabha & State Assemblies

  • Direct Elections: Voters directly elect their representatives.
  • System: India uses the First-Past-The-Post (FPTP) system, where the candidate with the highest number of votes wins.
  • Lok Sabha Constituencies: Total 543 seats. Out of these, 84 seats are reserved for SCs and 47 seats for STs to ensure representation.
  • Forming Government: The party or coalition winning a majority of seats forms the government. The leader becomes the Prime Minister (at the centre) or Chief Minister (in the state).

6. Election to the Rajya Sabha

The Rajya Sabha (Council of States) represents the states and union territories. It is a Permanent House and is never dissolved; 1/3rd of its members retire every 2 years, serving a 6-year term.

Concept

How Rajya Sabha differs from Lok Sabha:

  • Composition: Total 245 members (233 elected + 12 nominated by the President for art, science, etc.).
  • Indirect Election: People do not vote directly. Instead, the elected MLAs of the state legislative assemblies vote for Rajya Sabha members.
  • Voting System: Uses the Single Transferable Vote (STV) system, where MLAs rank candidates in order of preference (proportional representation).

7. Election of the President and Vice President

Both the President and Vice President are indirectly elected using the Single Transferable Vote system, but by different Electoral Colleges.

Election of the President

The Electoral College consists ONLY of elected representatives to ensure authority comes from the people:

  • Elected Members of Parliament (Lok Sabha & Rajya Sabha).
  • Elected Members of State Legislative Assemblies (MLAs), including Delhi and Puducherry.
  • Note: Nominated members and Members of Legislative Councils (MLCs) cannot vote for the President.

Election of the Vice President

The Vice President is the acting President (in emergencies) and the Chairperson of the Rajya Sabha. Their Electoral College consists of:

  • All Members of Parliament (Both elected and nominated members of the Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha).
  • Note: MLAs do not vote in the Vice President's election.

8. Challenges and The Road Ahead

Despite being the world's largest democracy, India's electoral system faces challenges:

  • Growing influence of money in elections.
  • A significant proportion of candidates with criminal records.
  • Voter Apathy: Lack of interest in voting, especially in urban areas.

The future of democracy relies on informed and responsible voters. Education and awareness are essential to help citizens evaluate promises and protect democratic values.
