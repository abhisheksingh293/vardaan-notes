Manufacturing Industries

1. Need for Industries

Industry means the production of goods or extraction of minerals in a particular field. The manufacturing industry takes raw materials (like agricultural products or minerals) and turns them into finished goods that are more useful and have a higher economic value.

Why are industries important?

  • They generate employment, leading to a higher standard of living.
  • They satisfy the increasing demands of a large population by producing goods on a mass scale.
  • Exporting manufactured goods helps a country earn foreign exchange.
  • They lead to the overall economic development of a country.

2. Types of Industries

Industries can be classified based on their size, the raw materials they use, and their ownership.

Classification by Raw Material

  • Agro-based Industries: Use plant and animal-based agricultural produce as raw materials. Examples: Cotton textiles, jute textiles, sugar, and food processing.
  • Mineral-based Industries: Use mineral ores as raw materials. The products of these industries feed other industries. Example: Iron and steel industry (provides steel for heavy machinery and building materials).

Classification by Size

  • Large-scale Industries: Employ a large number of labourers, require huge capital investment, and use heavy machinery and power. Examples: Iron and steel, large-scale cotton textiles.
  • Small-scale Industries: Use lesser capital and fewer workers compared to large-scale industries. Often found in semi-urban or rural areas. Example: Garment manufacturing.
  • Cottage Industries: Very small industries where artisans set up manufacturing units in their own homes. Usually run by family members using simple tools. Examples: Handloom weaving, pottery, basket weaving, and leather work.
Concept

Manufacturing: The process of converting raw materials into finished, usable goods on a large scale using machines.

3. Factors Determining Industrial Location

The location of an industry is a crucial decision influenced by several geographical and economic factors:

  • Availability of raw materials: Industries tend to locate near sources of raw materials, especially if the materials are heavy and bulky (like iron ore) or perishable (like sugarcane). This reduces transportation costs.
  • Availability of labour: An adequate supply of cheap and skilled labour is necessary. Therefore, industries often attract populations, leading to the growth of towns and cities around them.
  • Proximity to markets: Access to a market is vital for selling finished goods. Industries producing perishable or fragile items prefer to locate near their markets.
  • Transport facilities: A good network of roads, railways, and ports is essential for bringing in raw materials and distributing finished products.
  • Availability of power: Regular supply of power (electricity, coal, oil) is needed to run machinery. Industries, particularly heavy ones, often locate near power sources.
  • Capital: Huge financial investment is required to set up an industry, buy land, machinery, and pay wages.
  • Government policies: Governments provide incentives like subsidized land, power, and tax holidays to encourage industries to set up in backward areas for balanced regional development.
Fact

Silicon Valley in California (USA) and Bengaluru in India are major hubs for the Information Technology (IT) industry due to the availability of skilled professionals, good infrastructure, and favorable government policies.

4. Major Industries

Cotton Textile Industry

One of the oldest industries in the world. Initially, cotton cloth was made using hand-spinning techniques (wheels and looms). The Industrial Revolution in the 18th century brought power looms, accelerating the development of the industry, first in Britain and later elsewhere.

Key locations in India: Mumbai, Ahmedabad, Kanpur, Surat, and Coimbatore. Historically, places like Dhaka (muslins) and Masulipatnam (chintzes) were famous for their high-quality cotton fabrics.

Iron and Steel Industry

Considered a basic or key industry because it provides raw materials (steel) for almost all other industries, from heavy machinery to construction and shipbuilding. It requires bulky raw materials: iron ore, coal, and limestone.

Key locations: Major steel-producing nations are China, Japan, USA, Russia, and Germany. In India, important centres are Jamshedpur, Bhilai, Durgapur, and Bokaro.

Information Technology (IT) Industry

Deals with the storage, processing, and distribution of information using computers and telecommunications. It is a rapidly growing, "footloose" industry (not strongly tied to specific raw material locations).

Sugar Industry

An agro-based industry that relies on sugarcane (which is bulky and perishable). India is a leading producer of sugar globally, with major production in Uttar Pradesh and Maharashtra.

Shipbuilding Industry

Involves the construction of large ships for cargo, passenger transport, or military purposes. It requires a specialized facility called a shipyard. In India, major shipyards are located in Vishakhapatnam, Kolkata, Kochi, and Mumbai.

Automobile Industry

Involves the design, development, manufacturing, marketing, and selling of motor vehicles. Major automobile centres in India include Gurugram, Pune, Chennai, and Jamshedpur.

Fishing Industry

Includes any industry or activity concerned with taking, culturing, processing, preserving, storing, transporting, marketing or selling fish or fish products.

Important

Footloose Industries: Industries like IT are called 'footloose' because they can be located almost anywhere. They don't depend heavily on a specific raw material source like the iron and steel industry does.

5. Industrial Pollution

While industries are essential for economic growth, their rapid expansion has led to severe environmental degradation.

Types of Industrial Pollution

  • Air Pollution: Caused by the emission of harmful gases (like sulphur dioxide and carbon monoxide) and smoke from factory chimneys. Affects human health and causes issues like acid rain.
  • Water Pollution: Dumping of untreated industrial effluents (chemical wastes) into rivers and lakes. This kills aquatic life and makes water unfit for human consumption.
  • Soil/Land Pollution: Improper disposal of solid wastes and chemicals degrades soil quality, making it barren.
  • Noise Pollution: High-intensity sounds from heavy machinery and factory operations can cause hearing impairment and stress.

Controlling Industrial Pollution

Industrial pollution needs to be checked to maintain the ecological balance.

  • Treating Effluents: Industrial waste and effluents must be treated in effluent treatment plants before being discharged into water bodies.
  • Using Cleaner Fuels: Shifting from coal and oil to cleaner energy sources like electricity or natural gas to reduce air pollution.
  • Tall Chimneys with Filters: Installing tall chimneys fitted with electrostatic precipitators and scrubbers to trap harmful particles before they are released into the air.
  • Recycling and Reuse: Encouraging industries to recycle water and raw materials to minimize waste generation.
  • Afforestation: Planting more trees around industrial areas (creating green belts) as trees act as natural air purifiers and noise absorbers.
  • Strict Laws: Governments must enforce stringent environmental laws and penalize industries that flout pollution control norms.
