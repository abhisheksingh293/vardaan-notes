RESPIRATION

1. Introduction & Basic Process

Energy is needed by living as well as non-living things to do work. Without energy, activities stop. The food we eat is broken down into simpler forms during digestion. This digested food is then absorbed by the walls of the intestine and carried by the blood to different parts of the body.

The energy stored in food is released through the oxidation of food materials. During this process, oxygen combines with the food we eat, liberating energy and releasing carbon dioxide as a waste product. This energy production takes place all through the day.

Concept

Respiration: The process by which food (glucose) is broken down by oxygen (oxidized) to release energy, carbon dioxide, and water.

Fact

ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate): The released energy produced during respiration is stored in special molecules within the cells called ATP.

2. Characteristics of Respiration

Respiration is a vital process common to all plants and animals. It is characterized by the following acts inside living cells:

  • Food is used up (oxidized).
  • Energy is produced and stored.
  • Exchange of gases occurs: Oxygen is used up, and Carbon dioxide is given out.

3. Types of Respiration

Based on whether oxygen is used in the process or not, respiration is classified into two main types:

Important

The two main types of respiration are Aerobic Respiration (requires oxygen) and Anaerobic Respiration (does not require oxygen).

A. Aerobic Respiration

  • Takes place in the presence of oxygen.
  • Food molecules (glucose) are completely broken down into carbon dioxide and water.
  • Releases a large amount of energy (38 ATP molecules).

B. Anaerobic Respiration

  • Takes place in the absence of oxygen.
  • Food molecules (glucose) are only partially broken down into ethyl alcohol and carbon dioxide. Water is not produced.
  • Releases much less energy (2 ATP molecules). Examples include yeast and some bacteria.

4. Process of Respiration

The overall process of respiration consists of two main stages:

  • Breathing (External Respiration): A physical process in which an organism inhales oxygen and exhales carbon dioxide. It occurs outside the cells and involves no enzymes or energy release.
  • Cellular Respiration (Internal Respiration): A biochemical process occurring inside living cells (mitochondria). Food molecules are oxidized to release energy, carbon dioxide, and water. Enzymes take part in this stepwise process.

5. Combustion vs. Respiration

While both burning (combustion) and respiration use oxygen and release carbon dioxide and energy, they differ significantly:

  • Combustion: A fast process occurring in non-living things at high temperatures. Energy is released in a single step as heat and light. No enzymes are involved.
  • Respiration: A slow, stepwise process occurring in living cells at normal room temperature. Energy is released gradually and stored in a special molecule (ATP) without producing heat or light. Reactions are catalyzed by enzymes.

6. Respiration in Plants

Plants, like all other living organisms, respire to produce energy for their life activities. Unlike animals, plants lack specific breathing organs. The exchange of gases (oxygen and carbon dioxide) occurs by the process of diffusion through three main sources:

  • Stomata: Minute openings present on the surface of leaves, surrounded by guard cells.
  • Lenticels: Tiny openings present on old, woody stems.
  • Root Surface: Gases diffuse through the general surface of the roots in the soil.
Fact

Anaerobic Respiration in Plants: Germinating seeds can respire in the absence of oxygen for a short time, producing carbon dioxide and ethyl alcohol.

7. Respiration vs. Photosynthesis

Respiration and photosynthesis are two important, complementary processes that take place simultaneously in green plants:

  • Photosynthesis: Occurs only in the presence of sunlight and requires chlorophyll. It uses carbon dioxide and water to produce food (glucose) and releases oxygen. This process results in weight gain.
  • Respiration: Occurs all the time (day and night) in all living cells, and does not require chlorophyll. It uses food and oxygen to produce energy, releasing carbon dioxide and water. This process results in weight loss.
Important

Lungs of the Earth: Plants are crucial for maintaining the environmental balance of oxygen and carbon dioxide. We must grow more plants, as they are the only natural source capable of converting atmospheric CO₂ back into life-sustaining oxygen!
