PHOTOSYNTHESIS

1. Introduction

Green plants prepare their own food through a process known as photosynthesis, and are thus also known as autotrophs (auto = self, troph = food). Literally, the word photosynthesis means "synthesis in the presence of light".

This process takes place mostly in the leaves, though in some cases, it also occurs in green stems. The leaves are essentially the chemical factories of the plant. They convert energy from sunlight into chemical energy by making carbohydrates (food).

Concept

Photosynthesis: The process of preparation of food by green plants in the presence of sunlight using atmospheric carbon dioxide and water from the soil.

Leaves contain a green-coloured pigment called chlorophyll, which is present inside cellular structures called chloroplasts. During photosynthesis, food is synthesised in the form of sugars, and oxygen is released as a byproduct. The presence of starch (a complex carbohydrate) in leaves is regarded as evidence that photosynthesis has taken place.

Important

Chemical Equation of Photosynthesis:

Carbon dioxide + Water (in the presence of Sunlight & Chlorophyll) ➔ Carbohydrates (Food) + Oxygen

2. Raw Materials Required

Green plants require four essential things to prepare their own food:

  • Carbon dioxide: Absorbed from the atmosphere through tiny pores on the leaf surface called stomata.
  • Water: Absorbed from the soil through the root system.
  • Chlorophyll: The green pigment present in the leaf's chloroplasts, necessary to trap solar energy.
  • Sunlight: The source of energy that drives the entire process. Without it, photosynthesis cannot take place.

3. Stomata

Stomata are minute openings present on the surface of leaves. They are generally more common on the lower surface of the leaf.

Fact

Stomatal Apparatus: A stoma consists of an opening surrounded by two kidney-shaped cells called guard cells. The guard cells contain chloroplasts and control the opening and closing of the pore. They are surrounded by subsidiary cells.

Role of Stomata: The primary function of stomata is the exchange of gases (carbon dioxide and oxygen) between the plant and the atmosphere. Stomata are wide open during the day when photosynthesis is actively occurring, and they close in the dark or at night when there is no photosynthesis.

4. Factors Affecting Photosynthesis

The rate at which a plant performs photosynthesis is influenced by several factors:

  • Light Intensity: Photosynthesis increases with an increase in light intensity, up to a certain point.
  • Availability of Carbon Dioxide: The rate of photosynthesis increases as the carbon dioxide concentration increases.
  • Temperature: Up to a certain value (generally between 20°C to 35°C), the rate of photosynthesis increases with an increase in temperature.
  • Availability of Water: Less availability of water reduces photosynthesis primarily because it causes the stomata to close, restricting carbon dioxide intake.
  • Chlorophyll: Essential for trapping light; without it, the process halts entirely.

Nutrients and Mineral Ions

Plants also absorb inorganic mineral ions from the soil water through their roots, which play various roles in growth and photosynthesis. These are classified into:

  • Macronutrients: Required in higher quantities (e.g., Carbon, Hydrogen, Oxygen, Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium, Calcium, Magnesium, Sulphur, Iron). Deficiency can lead to diseases like Chlorosis (yellowing of leaves due to lack of Nitrogen or Magnesium).
  • Micronutrients (Trace Nutrients): Required in very small quantities (e.g., Copper, Zinc, Boron, Manganese, Molybdenum).

5. Significance of Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is of monumental importance to life on Earth due to the following reasons:

  • Synthesis of Food for All: Green plants are the primary producers. All animals and non-green plants depend directly or indirectly on green plants for their food.
  • Oxygen for Respiration: Green plants release oxygen during photosynthesis. All other living organisms depend on this life-sustaining gas to survive.
  • Maintaining Atmospheric Balance: Through this process, plants consume carbon dioxide (released by animals during respiration) and release oxygen, thus maintaining a perfect balance of gases in the atmosphere.
  • Provides Fossil Fuels: The energy stored in all types of fossil fuels (like coal and petroleum) is basically ancient solar energy trapped by plants during photosynthesis millions of years ago.
  • Other Products: We derive numerous other essential products from plants, such as fibres, gum, medicines, timber, and resins.
