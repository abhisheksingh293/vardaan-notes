ELECTRICITY AND MAGNETISM

1. Laws of Magnetism and Test for a Magnet

Concept Magnetite is a natural magnet and an ore of iron. A freely suspended natural magnet was historically called a lodestone or leading stone because it points in the geographic north-south direction.

Important Properties of a Magnet

  • Attractive Property: It attracts small pieces of magnetic substances like iron, cobalt, and nickel towards itself.
  • Directive Property: When suspended freely, it always points in the geographic north-south direction.
  • Magnetic Poles: A magnet has two poles: a North Pole and a South Pole. The strength of a magnet is concentrated near these ends.
  • Magnetic Field: The space around a magnet in which its influence can be felt.
Important Laws of Magnetism:
1. Like poles of magnets repel each other.
2. Opposite (unlike) poles of magnets attract each other.
3. Repulsion is the surest test of magnetism because attraction can happen between a magnet and any unmagnetized piece of iron.

Making and Storing Magnets

A simple way to make a magnet is by using the single touch method, where a permanent bar magnet is rubbed repeatedly over a flat piece of iron in one direction without lifting it.

Fact Magnets can lose their magnetism if they are heated strongly, hammered, or handled roughly. To protect them, bar magnets should be stored in pairs with opposite poles facing each other, separated by wood, and with soft iron pieces called magnetic keepers placed across their ends.

2. Electromagnetism

Concept Electromagnetism is the branch of physics dealing with the relationship between electricity and magnetism. An electromagnet is a temporary magnet consisting of a coil of insulated copper wire wound around a soft iron core, which acts as a magnet only when an electric current flows through it.

Characteristics of Electromagnets

  • They are temporary magnets; they lose their magnetism as soon as the current is switched off.
  • Their strength can be increased by increasing the current or increasing the number of turns in the coil.
  • Permanent magnets (made from steel or ALNICO) do not lose their magnetism when the cause producing it is removed.

Oersted's Experiment

This experiment proved the magnetic effect of current. It showed that:

  • Whenever current is passed through a straight conductor, it behaves like a magnet.
  • The magnitude of the magnetic effect increases with the strength of the current.
  • The magnetic field is at right angles to the direction of the current flow.

The Electric Bell

An electric bell is a direct application of electromagnets. It consists of an electromagnet, a soft iron armature, a contact spring, an adjustable contact screw, a hammer, and a gong.

Working Principle: When the circuit is closed, current flows through the electromagnet, which attracts the armature. The hammer strikes the gong. As the armature moves, the circuit breaks at the contact point, the electromagnet loses its magnetism, and a spring pulls the armature back. The contact is re-established, the circuit completes again, and the rapid make-and-break action produces a continuous ringing sound.

3. Electric Circuit and its Components

Electric Charge and Current

  • Electric Charge (Q): The number of charges (electrons) that drift from higher to lower potential. The unit is the coulomb (C).
  • Electric Current (I): The rate of flow of charge in an electric circuit. The unit is the ampere (A).
  • Formula: Current (I) = Quantity of Charge (Q) / Time (t)
Fact By convention, the direction of electric current is considered to be from the positive terminal to the negative terminal, which is opposite to the actual flow of electrons.

Resistance and Conductors

Concept Resistance (R) is the friction or obstruction offered to the passage of electric current by a material. It is measured in ohms (Ω).
  • Good Conductors: Materials that allow electric current to flow through them easily (e.g., silver, copper, aluminum, impure water, human body).
  • Bad Conductors (Insulators): Materials that do not allow electric current to flow through them (e.g., plastic, wood, rubber, glass, pure water).

Types of Electric Circuits

An electric circuit is a path through which electric current flows. Current only flows if the circuit is closed or complete.

Series Circuit

  • Components are connected end-to-end (positive of one resistor acts as negative of the other).
  • The total resistance increases as more resistors are added.
  • If one element fails (e.g., a bulb breaks) or the circuit is broken anywhere, the entire circuit stops functioning.

Parallel Circuit

  • All components are connected across common positive and negative terminals.
  • The total resistance decreases as more resistors are added.
  • Each resistor functions independently. If one bulb breaks, the others continue to glow.
Important A cell is a single source of electricity. A combination of two or more cells connected together is called a battery.
