HEAT

1. Heat Energy and its General Effects

Concept Heat is an invisible form of energy that flows from a body at a higher temperature to a body at a lower temperature. It causes the sensation of hotness or coldness.

General Effects of Heat Energy

Heat energy produces a number of effects in material as well as living bodies:

  • Change in Temperature: When a material absorbs heat, its temperature rises. When it releases heat, its temperature falls.
  • Change in Dimensions: Most materials expand on heating and contract on cooling. This process is called thermal expansion.
  • Change in State: Heat energy can change a solid into a liquid, or a liquid into a gas without changing the temperature during the transition.
  • Chemical Changes: Heat breaks original bonds between elements and forms new bonds, leading to chemical reactions (e.g., combustion).

Processes of Change of State

  • Fusion (Melting): Change of solid state into liquid state by the absorption of heat energy.
  • Solidification (Freezing): Change of liquid state into solid state by the release of heat energy.
  • Vaporisation (Boiling): Change of liquid state into gaseous state by the absorption of heat energy.
  • Condensation: Change of gaseous state into liquid state by the release of heat energy.
  • Sublimation: Direct change of a solid into a gaseous state on heating (e.g., Camphor, Iodine, Naphthalene).
Important The freezing point of a liquid and the melting point of a solid of the same material have the exact same numerical value (e.g., 0°C for water/ice).

2. Temperature and its Measurement

Concept Temperature is the degree of hotness or coldness of an object. It dictates the direction of the flow of heat.

The Thermometer

A thermometer is an instrument used to measure temperature accurately. The most common type utilizes the thermal expansion of liquids.

Fact Mercury is an ideal thermometric liquid because it needs very little heat to expand, boils at a high 357°C, does not stick to the glass, and is easily visible (opaque and shining). Alcohol is used for extremely cold regions because it freezes at a much lower temperature than mercury.

Temperature Scales

  • Celsius Scale (°C): Lower standard point is 0°C (melting point of pure ice). Upper standard point is 100°C (boiling point of pure water).
  • Fahrenheit Scale (°F): Lower standard point is 32°F. Upper standard point is 212°F.
  • Kelvin Scale (K): The SI unit of temperature. Lower standard point is 273 K (0°C). Upper standard point is 373 K (100°C).

Formula for Conversion: Temperature in Kelvin = Temperature in °C + 273

3. Thermal Expansion and its Applications

Whenever a substance (solid, liquid, or gas) is heated, its volume increases. Gases expand the most, followed by liquids, while solids expand the least.

Practical Applications (Solids)

  • Jammed Lids: Putting a jammed metal lid in hot water expands the metal faster than the glass, making it loose.
  • Railway Tracks: Small gaps are left between rails to allow them to expand during summer, preventing the derailment of trains.
  • Electric Wires: Transmission wires are laid slightly loose during summer so they do not snap when they contract during winter.
  • Bimetallic Strip: Used in automatic fire alarms; it consists of two different metals (like brass and iron) that expand at unequal rates, causing the strip to bend and close an electrical circuit during a fire.

4. Transmission of Heat

Heat energy can travel from a hot body to a cold body through three distinct modes:

A. Conduction

The transmission of heat from one particle to another without the actual visible movement of the particles themselves. It happens primarily in solids.

  • Good Conductors: Allow heat to flow easily (e.g., Silver, Copper, Aluminium). Used in cooking vessels and radiators.
  • Bad Conductors (Insulators): Do not allow heat to flow easily (e.g., Glass, Air, Vacuum, Wood, Wool). We wear woollen clothes in winter because they trap air (a bad conductor), preventing our body heat from escaping.

B. Convection

The transmission of heat in liquids and gases where the particles actually move to the heat source, absorb energy, and move away, setting up convection currents.

Important Ventilation, Sea breezes, and Land breezes are direct natural applications of convection currents in the air.

C. Radiation

The transference of heat energy directly from a hot body to a cold body without heating the space in between. It is the fastest mode of heat transfer and can travel through a vacuum (like the sun's heat reaching Earth).

Fact Black surfaces are good absorbers and radiators of heat, which is why we wear dark clothes in winter and cooking vessels have blackened bottoms. White/shining surfaces reflect heat, keeping things cool.

The Thermos Flask (Vacuum Flask)

A device designed to minimize heat loss or gain through all three methods:

  • Conduction/Convection prevention: Minimized by the vacuum between the double glass walls and the insulating cork/stopper.
  • Radiation prevention: Minimized by the highly silvered/mirrored inner and outer walls which reflect the radiant heat back.
