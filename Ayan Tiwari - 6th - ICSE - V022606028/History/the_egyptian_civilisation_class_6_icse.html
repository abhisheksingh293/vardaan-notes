The Egyptian Civilisation

Concept

Civilisation: Ancient Egypt, a land of mystery, was one of the earliest and most flourishing civilisations in the world, developed along the banks of the River Nile.

1. Overview and Origins

The Egyptian Civilisation lasted for over 3000 years, from 3200 BCE to 30 BCE. The River Nile provided fertile soil, water, and transportation, leading to Egypt being famously called the "Gift of the Nile." By 3300 BCE, villages had grown into towns ruled by powerful kings or Pharaohs.

2. Society and Structure

The society was hierarchical, divided into three main classes:

  • Upper Class: Royal family, priests, nobles, government officials, scribes, and doctors.
  • Middle Class: Merchants, manufacturers, and artisans.
  • Lower Class: Farmers and slaves.

While the society was patriarchal, women held significant rights, including the ability to own property, participate in trade, and divorce their husbands.

Fact

Did you know? In ancient Egypt, the make-up helped protect eyes and skin from the hot sun. Wealthy people often wore wigs to signify their status.

3. Religion and Beliefs

Religion was central to Egyptian life. The Pharaoh was considered a representative of God on Earth. They worshipped many gods and goddesses, often taking animal forms:

  • Ra: The Sun God, supreme ruler.
  • Osiris: God of the Dead and underworld ruler.
  • Isis: Mother Goddess, protector.
  • Anubis: Patron god of embalmers and guardian of the dead.

They strongly believed in life after death, leading to the process of Mummification—preserving the body using spices and linen—to unite the "ka" (life force) and "ba" (soul) in the afterlife.

4. Major Achievements

Writing and Records

The Egyptians used Hieroglyphics, a complex system of "sacred writing" using thousands of symbols. They recorded their lives on Papyrus, a paper-like material made from reed plants.

Important

Solar Calendar: The Egyptians were the first to devise an accurate 365-day solar calendar, called the 'Haab', used to track time and agricultural cycles.

Medicine and Math

Due to the mummification process, they gained extensive knowledge of human anatomy, leading to advancements in surgery and medicine. They also excelled in mathematics, using fractions and the decimal system for their grand construction projects.

5. Art and Architecture

Architecture is best exemplified by the Pyramids, built as grand tombs for Pharaohs. The Great Pyramid of Giza is the largest, constructed over 20 years by thousands of workers. The Great Sphinx, a mythological figure with a man's head and a lion's body, stands as another architectural wonder.

6. Decline of the Civilisation

The empire began to decline during the reign of Ramesses-III due to:

  • Internal corruption and political instability.
  • Severe droughts, famine, and wars.
  • Weak leadership and exploitation of the lower classes.
  • Finally, the empire was captured by the Roman Empire in 30 BCE.
