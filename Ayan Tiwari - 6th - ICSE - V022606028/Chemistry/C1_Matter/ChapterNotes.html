MATTER

1. Introduction to Matter

In our daily life, we come across hundreds of things, both living and non-living. They have different shapes, colors, textures, and states. Everything tangible that we can perceive with our senses is made of some kind of material, which scientists collectively call matter.

Note: Perceptions like joy, love, hate, and pain, as well as heat, light, sound, electricity, vacuum, and shadow, are not considered to be matter.

Concept

Matter is defined as any material which has mass (weight) and occupies space (volume).

2. States of Matter

Matter primarily exists in three distinct states: Solids, Liquids, and Gases.

Properties of a Solid

  • Shape and Volume: Solids have a definite shape and a definite volume.
  • Flow: Solids do not flow; they can be heaped.
  • Containment: They do not need a container to hold them.
  • Compressibility: Solids cannot be compressed.
  • Surfaces: They can have any number of free surfaces.

Examples: Wood, stone, sand, iron.

Properties of a Liquid

  • Shape and Volume: Liquids have a definite volume but no definite shape. They take the shape of the containing vessel.
  • Flow: Liquids always need some container, otherwise they start flowing (from a higher to a lower level).
  • Compressibility: Liquids cannot be compressed (or are very slightly compressible).
  • Surfaces: They have only one definite free flat surface at the top.

Examples: Water, milk, fruit juice, alcohol.

Fact

Liquids such as honey, coconut oil, and animal fat (desi ghee) are sticky and difficult to pour because there is more friction between their molecular layers. Such liquids are known as viscous liquids.

Properties of a Gas

  • Shape and Volume: Gases have neither a definite shape nor a definite volume.
  • Expansion: They have a property to fill the entire space available to them.
  • Compressibility: Gases can be easily compressed.
  • Surfaces: They have no free surface.

Examples: Oxygen, nitrogen, carbon dioxide, steam.

Important

When gases are heated to tens of thousands of degrees Celsius, they change into another state of matter called Plasma. In this state, atoms break into positively and negatively charged particles. The matter in the sun and inside fluorescent tubes is in the plasma state.

3. Arrangement of Particles (Molecules)

Matter is made of very small particles called molecules. The characteristics of matter depend on two critical factors:

  • Intermolecular Force: The force of attraction between molecules.
  • Intermolecular Space: The space between the molecules. (Note: As space decreases, the force of attraction increases, and vice versa.)

In Solids

Molecules are very tightly packed. The intermolecular spaces are very small, resulting in a very strong intermolecular force of attraction. Molecules cannot interchange positions (they only vibrate), which is why solids have a definite shape, definite volume, and cannot be compressed.

In Liquids

Molecules are not very tightly packed. The intermolecular spaces are larger than in solids, leading to a weaker intermolecular force. Molecules can interchange their places, allowing liquids to flow and take the shape of their container while maintaining a definite volume.

In Gases

Molecules are very far apart. Intermolecular spaces are too large, and intermolecular forces are almost negligible. Molecules can move freely in all directions, allowing gases to fill available space and be easily compressed.

4. Interconversion of States of Matter

The state of matter can be changed by increasing or decreasing the temperature (heating or cooling), which alters the kinetic energy of the particles.

  • Melting (Fusion): The process in which a solid changes into a liquid on heating. The constant temperature at which this occurs is called the Melting Point (e.g., Ice melts at 0°C).
  • Boiling: The process of changing a liquid rapidly into a gas on heating. The constant temperature at which this occurs is called the Boiling Point (e.g., Water boils at 100°C).
  • Condensation: The process of changing a gas into a liquid on cooling (particles lose kinetic energy).
  • Freezing: The process of changing a liquid into a solid by cooling.
Fact

The higher the melting or boiling point of a substance, the stronger the intermolecular force of attraction between its particles. For example, the melting point of Tungsten is a massive 3414°C.

5. Particle Motion and Forces

Brownian Movement

Discovered by Robert Brown, this is the zig-zag, random movement of particles suspended in a liquid or gas. It is caused by the constant bombardment of fast-moving invisible particles of the medium. (Example: Dust particles moving in a beam of sunlight entering a dark room.)

Brownian movement is fastest in gases, slower in liquids, and negligible in solids.

Diffusion

Diffusion is the uniform intermixing of particles of two different substances on their own. (Examples: A drop of red ink spreading in water; the smell of an incense stick spreading across a room.)

Diffusion is fastest in gases and negligible in solids.

Cohesive and Adhesive Forces

Cohesive forces are the intermolecular forces of attraction between molecules of the same substance (maximum in solids, negligible in gases).

Adhesive forces are the forces of attraction between molecules of different substances.

6. Effect of Heat on Matter

  • Expansion (Physical Change): All three states of matter increase in size on heating. Solids expand the least, liquids expand more, and gases expand the most. (Example: Mercury rising in a heated thermometer.)
  • Change of State (Physical Change): Heating causes solids to melt and liquids to boil. No new substance is formed.
  • Chemical Change: Heating can cause chemical reactions where entirely new substances are formed. (Example: Heating limestone decomposes it into calcium oxide and carbon dioxide gas.)
Concept

Is there space between particles of matter? Yes. When common salt is dissolved in water, the water level does not rise. The salt crystals break into tiny particles and occupy the empty intermolecular spaces between the water molecules.
