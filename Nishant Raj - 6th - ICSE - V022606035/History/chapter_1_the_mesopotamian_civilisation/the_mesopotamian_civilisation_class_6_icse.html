The Mesopotamian Civilisation

Concept

Cradle of Civilisation: The Mesopotamian Civilisation (3500 BCE – 600 BCE) is known as the "Cradle of Civilisation." The word "Mesopotamia" is of Greek origin, meaning "land between two rivers" (the Tigris and the Euphrates), which corresponds to modern-day Iraq and Kuwait.

1. Society and Structure

Society was divided into three distinct classes:

  • Upper Class: The King, his family, and priests. They held the highest status.
  • Middle Class: Craftsmen, merchants, civil servants, farmers, and labourers.
  • Lower Class: Slaves, often prisoners captured in battle.

Family life was patriarchal, with the father as the head. Women enjoyed respect but were not treated equally to men and required permission for various tasks.

2. Town Planning and Architecture

Mesopotamian cities, such as Ur and Uruk, followed a uniform pattern with three main areas:

  • Sacred Area: Centered around the Ziggurat, a massive stepped-pyramid temple dedicated to the patron god.
  • Walled City: Residential and official areas on a mound.
  • Outer Town: Where farmers and artisans lived.
Fact

Code of Hammurabi: King Hammurabi compiled one of the first and most complete sets of written laws in history (1792–1750 BCE). Inscribed on stone pillars (steles), it followed the principle of "an eye for an eye, a tooth for a tooth" and regulated everything from property and trade to family life.

3. Religion

The Mesopotamians were polytheistic, worshipping nature forces like the Sun, Moon, and rain. They believed the King was a representative of God on Earth. Elaborate Ziggurats served as both religious centers and focal points for the community.

4. Occupations and Achievements

Occupations

Agriculture was the main occupation, supported by an advanced irrigation system involving canals, dykes, and reservoirs to control floods. They domesticated animals like cows, goats, sheep, and donkeys.

Achievements

  • Writing: Developed Cuneiform, a wedge-shaped script pressed into clay tablets using a stylus.
  • Mathematics: Utilized a base 60 (sexagesimal) system, which forms the basis for counting time (seconds/minutes) and degrees in a circle today.
  • Construction: Pioneered the use of arches, columns, and domes.
  • Literature: Created the "Epic of Gilgamesh," one of the world's oldest pieces of literature.
Important

Reasons for Decline: The civilisation faced decline due to a combination of: Constant warfare, soil salinization (salt buildup) which ruined agriculture, internal political instability, and successive invasions by empires like the Hittites, Assyrians, and Persians.
