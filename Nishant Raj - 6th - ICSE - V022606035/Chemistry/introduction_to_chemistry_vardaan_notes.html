INTRODUCTION TO CHEMISTRY

1. The Science of Chemistry

In our daily lives, we are surrounded by various materials. These materials can be broadly classified into two categories:

  • Natural Materials: Obtained from nature, such as air, water, stones, rocksalt, marble, petroleum, metals (gold, silver, copper), and plant/animal products (milk, cheese, wheat, oil seeds).
  • Man-Made Materials: Synthesized by humans, such as cement, glass, steel, plastic, bronze, and nylon.
Concept

Chemistry is defined as the branch of science which deals with the study of materials (substances), especially about their composition, methods of preparation, properties, and their reactions with other substances.

2. Importance and Applications of Chemistry

Chemists perform experiments under various conditions, make careful observations, and draw conclusions. This research has transformed almost every sector of human life:

  • Agriculture: Chemistry has led to the development of fertilizers to increase crop yield and insecticides/pesticides to protect plants from pests.
  • Food Preservation: It provides chemical preservatives which allow food to be stored for longer periods without spoiling.
  • Shelter & Household: Essential construction materials like cement, steel, and glass, as well as daily items like paints, cleaning products, soaps, and detergents, are products of chemical research.
  • Electronics & Space: Versatile materials such as silicon and germanium are used in making electronic components (transistors, computer chips). Furthermore, powerful rocket fuels enable space exploration.
Fact

Through chemistry, humans have learned to synthesise materials that are not available in nature. By mixing metals in fixed proportions, we prepare strong alloys like bronze and steel.

3. Chemistry in Daily Life

Food and Chemistry

Food Science deals with the production, processing, preparation, and utilization of food. Various specialists work in this field:

  • Animal Chemists: Ensure animal feed is nutritious and safe, which in turn benefits humans.
  • Flavour Chemists: Study the chemistry of food flavours to create new and improved tastes.
  • Food Chemists: Focus on processing, packaging, and developing synthetic foods.
  • Nutritional Chemists: Research the physical and chemical properties of vitamins and minerals.

Cosmetics and Chemistry

Cosmetics have been used since ancient civilizations. Today, chemical knowledge allows us to safely formulate and manufacture a massive range of products including talcum powders, skin creams, lipsticks, and dyes.

Clothing and Chemistry

Chemistry has revolutionized the clothing industry by introducing synthetic fibres (man-made fibres) created from raw materials derived from petroleum or natural gas. Examples include Nylon, Rayon, and Terylene.

Important

Advantages of Synthetic Fibres: Unlike natural fibres, synthetic fibres are much stronger, wrinkle-free (they do not require ironing), and have high resistance to wear and tear, making them long-lasting.

Chemicals as Medicine

Historically, man depended on medicinal plants. With the advancement of chemistry, active molecules were extracted from plants to prepare better medicines. Today, highly effective synthetic medicines such as Penicillin, Tetracycline (antibiotics), Sulphadiazine, and various analgesics (painkillers) and antimalarials are developed in laboratories.

4. Notable Scientists and their Contributions

The foundation of modern chemistry was laid by the relentless work of several great scientists. Their patience, perseverance, and ethical conduct continue to inspire us.

  • Henry Cavendish (1731–1810): Discovered hydrogen gas. He also researched the properties of water and determined the density of the Earth.
  • Dmitri Mendeleev (1834–1907): Formulated the Periodic Law. He arranged all known elements in a table based on their atomic weights and accurately predicted the properties of elements that were yet to be discovered.
  • Robert Boyle (1627–1691): Often called the pioneer of modern chemistry. He clearly defined elements and proposed Boyle's Law regarding the behaviour of gases.
  • John Dalton (1766–1844): Proposed the Atomic Theory, suggesting that all matter is made of tiny indivisible particles called atoms. He also proposed the Law of Multiple Proportions.
  • Antoine Lavoisier (1743–1794): Widely recognized as the Father of Chemistry. He named the elements oxygen and hydrogen, proved the role of oxygen in combustion, and formulated the Law of Conservation of Mass.
