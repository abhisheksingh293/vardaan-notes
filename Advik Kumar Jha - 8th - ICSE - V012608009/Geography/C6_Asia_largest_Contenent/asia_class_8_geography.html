Asia: the Largest Continent

1. Introduction, Location & Extent

Asia is the largest continent in the world, covering approximately one-third of the total land area of the Earth. It has the distinction of being the cradle of human civilisation and is known for its great river valley civilisations and natural diversity.

Geographic Boundaries

  • South: Bounded by the Indian Ocean.
  • East: Bounded by the Pacific Ocean.
  • North: Bounded by the Arctic Ocean.
  • West: Bounded by the continent of Europe. The Ural Mountains, the Ural River, and the Caspian Sea form the boundary between Europe and Asia.
Concept

Eurasia: The combined, continuous landmass of both Europe and Asia is commonly referred to as Eurasia.

Isthmus: A narrow strip of land with sea on either side connecting two larger landmasses. The Isthmus of Suez and the Red Sea link Asia with Africa.

Extent and Size

The continent extends from approximately 10°S to 80°N latitudes and from 24°4'E to 169°40'W longitudes. Because of this vast longitudinal extent, Asia possesses the greatest number of time zones in the world.

Fact

Asia Fact File:

  • Area: 44.58 Million sq.km
  • Highest Mountain: Mt. Everest, The Himalayas
  • Longest River: River Yangtze
  • Largest Country: Russia
  • Smallest Country: Maldives

2. Political Divisions

Asia is vast and consists of numerous nations. To study it efficiently, it is divided into five primary political and regional divisions:

  • East Asian Countries (The Far East): Includes Japan, North Korea, South Korea, China, Taiwan, and Mongolia.
  • South-east Asian Countries: Includes Myanmar, Malaysia, Thailand, Singapore, Cambodia, Vietnam, Laos, Indonesia, and the Philippines.
  • South Asian Countries: Includes India, Pakistan, Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Nepal, Bhutan, and Maldives.
  • Southwest Asian Countries (The Middle East): Includes Saudi Arabia, Iran, Turkey, Iraq, Jordan, Syria, and Israel.
  • Central Asia: Formed mostly after the collapse of the USSR. Includes Kazakhstan, Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan, Kyrgyzstan, and Tajikistan.

3. Physiography (Physical Features)

Asia's landscape is highly varied. It contains the highest mountains, immense plateaus, and some of the largest stretches of lowlands globally. The major physical divisions are:

A. Central Highlands

This is a vast area of lofty mountains and plateaus with rugged features radiating from the Pamir Knot located to the north of India.

  • The Great Himalayas and Karakoram Range extend southeastwards from the Pamir Knot.
  • The Hindukush and Sulaiman Ranges extend westwards.
  • The Himalayas are the highest in terms of elevation with many peaks exceeding 8,000 meters.
Important

Intermontane Plateaus: Plateaus completely surrounded by mountain ranges. A prime example is the Plateau of Tibet, which lies between the Himalayas and the Kunlun Ranges, often called the "Roof of the World".

B. Northern Lowlands

Lying to the north of the Central Mountain Belt, it primarily consists of two subdivisions:

  • The Siberian Plain: Drained by the Ob, Yenisey, and Lena Rivers. During summer, melted snow stagnates forming large marshes and swamps.
  • Central Asian Lowlands: A large area of inland drainage featuring the Caspian Sea and Aral Sea.

C. Plateaus

These are old, stable blocks of the Earth made up of old rocks. Major plateaus include the Plateau of Arabia, the Deccan Plateau of India, and the Yunnan-Guizhou Plateau.

D. River Basins

Located mainly between the Central Highlands and Southern Plateaus, these fertile alluvial valleys are crucial for agriculture and civilization. Important basins include:

  • The Mesopotamian Lowland drained by the Tigris and Euphrates rivers.
  • The Indo-Gangetic Plain drained by the Indus, Ganga, and Brahmaputra.
  • The Hwang Ho, Yangtze, and Si Kiang river basins in China.

E. Islands

Asia features numerous island archipelagos primarily off its east and southeast coasts, such as Japan, the Philippines, and Indonesia. Many of these have active volcanoes.

4. Climate of Asia

Asia experiences dramatic extremes of climate. It houses Jacobabad (Pakistan), one of the hottest places; Verkhoyansk (Siberia), one of the coldest; and Mawsynram (India), the wettest place on Earth.

Factors Affecting Climate

  • Latitudinal Extent: Spanning from 10°S to 80°N incorporates equatorial, tropical, temperate, and frigid climates.
  • Continentality: The central parts of Asia are completely landlocked and far from the sea, leading to an extreme continental climate.
  • Relief Features: East-West aligned mountain ranges act as barriers. The Central Highlands block cold Arctic winds from reaching India and block moisture-bearing winds from crossing north.
  • Jet Streams: Upper air currents influence the movement of monsoons and westerly depressions.

Types of Climate

  • Tundra Climate: Found in the extreme north (Siberia); characterised by long, brutally cold winters and extremely short summers.
  • Temperate Climate: Varies from Mediterranean type (Turkey) to Steppes type (Caspian Sea area) and Cool Temperate Continental type.
  • Tropical Monsoon Climate: Found in India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, and Southeast Asia. Features a clear division of seasons with heavy summer rainfall.
  • Tropical Hot Desert Climate: Found in the Arabian Peninsula and Thar Desert. Highly elevated temperatures throughout the year with minimal rainfall.
  • Equatorial Climate: Prevails close to the equator (Indonesia, Malaysia). Hot, humid, and rainy throughout the year with no distinct seasonal variation.

5. Natural Vegetation and Wildlife

Asia's immense climatic diversity directly results in a vast array of natural vegetation and wildlife habitats. Rainfall and soil type are the most critical determining factors.

  • Tundra Vegetation: Confined to the Arctic coast. Features snow cover most of the year. Flora includes mosses and lichens. Fauna includes reindeer, polar bears, and polar dogs.
  • Taiga (Coniferous Forest): A broad belt extending across the northern latitudes. Tall evergreen softwood trees like fir, spruce, and pine are dominant.
  • Tropical Deciduous Forest: Also known as monsoon forests (India, Southeast Asia). Trees shed their leaves once a year during the dry season. Key flora includes teak, sal, and sandalwood. Common fauna are elephants, tigers, and rhinos.
  • Thorny Desert Vegetation: Prevalent in the southwest and central deserts. Characterized by thorny scrubs, acacia, and date palms. Fauna includes the camel, gazelle, and cheetah.
  • Tropical Rainforest (Equatorial): Dense forests with thick canopies found in Malaysia and Indonesia. Features evergreen hardwood trees like ebony, rosewood, and mahogany. Wildlife is incredibly diverse, including monkeys, reptiles, and millions of insect species.
