ENERGY

Concept Work is said to be done only when the force applied on a body changes its position or moves it. No work is done if there is no motion produced, regardless of the physical or mental effort applied.

1. The Concept of Work

In Physics, the word "work" has a highly specific meaning. For work to be considered as done, the following two strict conditions must be fulfilled:

  • A force must act on the body.
  • The force must produce a change in position, motion, size, or shape of the body.
Fact If a boy pushes against a heavy wall and it does not move, no work is done. Similarly, a coolie standing still with a heavy box on his head does no work because there is no change in position. In these cases, energy is spent by the person, but mechanical work is zero.

Formula and Unit of Work

When an object changes its position strictly along the direction of the applied force, the amount of work done is calculated as the product of force and distance:

Work done = Force × distance moved in the direction of force

The S.I. unit of work is the joule (J). A bigger unit of work is the kilojoule (kJ) or Megajoule (MJ).

Important One joule of work is said to be done when a force of one newton acting on a body moves it by exactly 1 metre in the direction of the force.

2. Energy and Mechanical Energy

Energy is defined as the capacity of doing work. When work is done on a body, its energy increases, and when a body does work, its energy decreases. Because energy is effectively stored work, the S.I. unit of energy is also the joule (J).

Forms of Mechanical Energy

The energy possessed by a body due to its state of rest or state of motion is called mechanical energy. The total mechanical energy of a body is the sum of its potential and kinetic energies. It exists in two primary forms:

  • Potential Energy (P.E.): The energy possessed by a body due to its state of rest, modified shape, or position. For example, water stored in a dam, a stretched rubber band, or a compressed spring.
    The Gravitational Potential Energy of a body of mass m raised to a height h against gravity g is calculated as P.E. = m × g × h.
  • Kinetic Energy (K.E.): The energy possessed by a body strictly due to its state of motion. For example, a moving car, a falling stone, or a swinging pendulum. It depends directly on the mass and the speed of the body.

3. Conversion and Conservation of Energy

Energy can neither be created nor destroyed; one form of energy can only be converted into other useful forms. In the absence of friction, the sum of potential energy and kinetic energy remains constant at each instant. This is known as the Law of Conservation of Mechanical Energy.

Consider a swinging pendulum. The bob possesses maximum potential energy at its extreme positions (the highest points of its swing) and maximum kinetic energy at its mean position (the lowest point). The total mechanical energy remains conserved throughout the swing.

Real-life Examples of Energy Transformation

  • Hydroelectricity: The potential energy of water stored in a dam changes into kinetic energy as it falls. This motion rotates a turbine connected to a generator to produce electrical energy.
  • Electric Motor: Converts electrical energy into mechanical energy to run appliances like fans and washing machines.
  • Microphone: Converts sound energy into electrical energy in the form of varying signals.
  • Solar Cell: Converts light (solar) energy directly into electrical energy.

4. Power

Different people or machines may take different amounts of time to do the exact same work. The rate of doing work is called power.

Power = Work done / Time taken

The S.I. unit of power is the watt (W). One watt is equal to spending one joule per second. A common commercial unit used for electricity is the kilowatt-hour (kWh).

Concept While Energy is simply the capacity to do work and does not depend on time, Power is the rate at which work is done and strictly depends on the time taken to spend that energy.
