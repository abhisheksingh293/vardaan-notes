DETAILED SOLUTIONS: Physics Mock Test 02 (Class 8 ICSE)

SECTION A

Q1. The SI unit of moment of force is:
[2 Marks]
Answer: (b) Newton-metre
Explanation: The moment of force (or torque) is calculated as the product of Force and the perpendicular distance from the pivot. Since the SI unit of Force is Newton (N) and distance is metre (m), the SI unit is Newton-metre (N m).
Q2. When a ray of light passes obliquely from an optically denser medium to a rarer medium, it:
[2 Marks]
Answer: (b) bends away from the normal
Explanation: Light travels faster in a rarer medium than in a denser medium. As it exits a denser medium and enters a rarer one, its speed increases, causing the refracted ray to bend away from the normal line.
Q3. The anomalous expansion of water dictates that the density of water is maximum at:
[2 Marks]
Answer: (c) 4°C
Explanation: Unlike most liquids, water contracts on heating from 0°C to 4°C, resulting in minimum volume and maximum density at exactly 4°C (\(1 \text{ g/cm}^3\) or \(1000 \text{ kg/m}^3\)).
Q4. A substance has a mass of 50 g and a volume of 20 cm³. If placed in water, it will:
[2 Marks]
Answer: (b) sink in water
Explanation: Density of substance = Mass / Volume = \(50 / 20 = 2.5 \text{ g/cm}^3\). The density of water is \(1 \text{ g/cm}^3\). Since the density of the substance is greater than that of water, it will sink.
Q5. According to the Kinetic Theory, which state of matter has the highest kinetic energy among its particles?
[2 Marks]
Answer: (c) Gas
Explanation: In gases, the intermolecular forces of attraction are almost negligible. Hence, particles move randomly and freely at very high speeds, possessing maximum kinetic energy compared to solids and liquids.

SECTION B

Q6. Define atmospheric pressure. Why do deep-sea divers have to wear specially designed heavy-duty suits?
[3 Marks]

Atmospheric Pressure: It is defined as the pressure exerted by the weight of the atmosphere (air) on any surface on the Earth.

Reason for deep-sea divers: As we go deeper into the sea, the pressure exerted by water increases drastically due to the increasing height (depth) of the liquid column above. Deep-sea divers wear specially designed suits to counteract this immense crushing pressure and to prevent their bodies from collapsing.

Q8. A force of 50 N is applied at a perpendicular distance of 0.2 m from the pivot. Calculate the moment of force. What happens to the turning effect if distance is doubled?
[3 Marks]

Given: Force (\(F\)) = 50 N, Distance (\(d\)) = 0.2 m.

Step 1: Calculate Moment of Force

\[ \text{Moment of Force} = F \times d \] \[ \text{Moment of Force} = 50 \text{ N} \times 0.2 \text{ m} = 10 \text{ N m} \]

Effect of doubling distance: If the distance is doubled (\(0.4 \text{ m}\)), the turning effect (moment of force) will also double (\(50 \times 0.4 = 20 \text{ N m}\)). The turning effect is directly proportional to the perpendicular distance.

Q9. Explain dispersion of white light. Name the colors of the visible spectrum.
[3 Marks]

Dispersion: The phenomenon of splitting of white light into its constituent seven colours when it passes through a transparent medium like a glass prism is called dispersion.

Colors of Spectrum: The colors obtained are represented by the acronym VIBGYOR. In order from bottom (most deviated) to top (least deviated), they are: Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, and Red.

Q10. Give scientific reasons: (a) Balloon in hot water expands (b) Liquids exert pressure on container walls.
[3 Marks]

(a) When the balloon is placed in hot water, the air inside it absorbs heat. According to the kinetic theory, the kinetic energy of air molecules increases, causing them to move faster and further apart. This leads to the thermal expansion of the gas, making the balloon expand.

(b) The molecules of a liquid are in continuous random motion. During their motion, they constantly collide with the walls of the container. The combined force of all these collisions over the area of the wall results in the pressure exerted by the liquid.

SECTION C

Q11. (a) Experiment using Eureka can to measure density. (b) Find density in SI units for 120g mass displacing 40cm³ water.
[5 Marks]

(a) Experiment with Eureka Can:

  1. First, measure the mass of the irregular solid (stone) using a physical balance. Let it be \(M\).
  2. Place a Eureka can on a horizontal table and fill it with water until it starts overflowing from the spout. Wait until dripping stops.
  3. Place an empty measuring cylinder exactly below the spout.
  4. Tie the solid with a thin thread and gently lower it into the can until completely immersed.
  5. The solid will displace water equal to its own volume, which flows out of the spout into the cylinder.
  6. Record the volume of the displaced water in the measuring cylinder. This is the volume (\(V\)) of the solid.
  7. Calculate Density: \(D = \frac{M}{V}\).

(b) Numerical Calculation:

Given: Mass (\(m\)) = 120 g. Volume (\(V\)) = 40 cm³ (since it displaces 40 cm³ of water).

\[ \text{Density in CGS unit} = \frac{120 \text{ g}}{40 \text{ cm}^3} = 3 \text{ g/cm}^3 \]

To convert to SI units (\(kg/m^3\)), multiply by 1000.

\[ \text{Density in SI unit} = 3 \times 1000 = 3000 \text{ kg/m}^3 \]

Q13. (a) Density vs Relative Density. (b) Principle of floatation and ship vs iron nail.
[5 Marks]

(a) Difference:

Density: It is the mass per unit volume of a substance. It has a unit (\(kg/m^3\) or \(g/cm^3\)).

Relative Density: It is the ratio of the density of a substance to the density of water at 4°C. Since it is a ratio of two identical quantities, it has no unit.

(b) Principle of Floatation and Ship vs Nail:

Principle of Floatation: An object floats in a fluid if the weight of the fluid displaced by the immersed part of the object is equal to or greater than the total weight of the object.

Explanation: A solid iron nail is compact and its density is greater than the density of water. Therefore, the weight of the water it displaces is less than its own weight, causing it to sink. On the other hand, a ship is hollow from within and contains a large amount of empty space filled with air. This makes the average density of the ship much less than the density of water. Due to its large volume, it displaces a massive amount of water. The huge upthrust (buoyant force) provided by the displaced water balances the total weight of the ship, allowing it to float.
