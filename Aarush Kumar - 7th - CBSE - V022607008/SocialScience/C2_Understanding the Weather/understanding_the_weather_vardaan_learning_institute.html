Understanding the Weather

Concept Weather is the condition of the atmosphere at a particular place and time, whereas Climate is the average weather pattern over a long period. All weather changes take place in the atmosphere.

1. Layers of the Earth's Atmosphere

The atmosphere is the layer of gases that surrounds the Earth. It acts as a protective blanket essential for supporting life. From bottom to top, the main layers are:

  • Troposphere: The lowest layer where all weather changes occur.
  • Stratosphere: Contains the ozone layer, which protects Earth by absorbing harmful ultraviolet rays from the Sun.
  • Mesosphere: In this layer, most meteors burn up upon entering Earth's atmosphere.
  • Thermosphere: Contains charged particles where auroras occur, and the International Space Station orbits here.
  • Exosphere: The outermost layer where satellites orbit the Earth.
Fact The Karman Line is an imaginary boundary located 100 km above sea level, which is considered the dividing line between Earth's atmosphere and outer space.

2. Elements of Weather

Weather is described through several core elements that influence our daily lives and environment:

  • Temperature: A measure of how hot or cold the atmosphere is. It influences clothing, agriculture, and lifestyle.
  • Precipitation: Any form of water falling from the sky (rain, snow, sleet, or hail).
  • Atmospheric Pressure: The force exerted by air on the Earth's surface. It affects cloud formation, winds, and precipitation.
  • Wind: The movement of air from high-pressure to low-pressure areas. It distributes heat and moisture.
  • Humidity: The amount of water vapour present in the air.

3. Types of Weather

Common weather conditions we experience include:

  • Sunny & Cloudy: Clear skies vs. skies covered with clouds that block direct sunlight.
  • Rainy & Stormy: Water droplets falling vs. strong winds with thunder, lightning, and heavy rainfall.
  • Windy & Snowy: Fast-moving air vs. frozen water particles (snowflakes) falling in cold regions.
  • Foggy: Tiny water droplets suspended close to the ground, reducing visibility.
  • Hot, Cold & Humid: Variations in temperature and high water vapour making the air feel sticky.

4. Weather Warnings from Nature

Nature often signals upcoming weather changes without the need for instruments:

  • Animal Behaviour: Birds flying lower or ants moving eggs to higher ground often signal rain. The croaking of frogs is a natural sign of expected rainfall.
  • Plant Behaviour: Flowers like the morning glory close their petals before it rains. Pine cones open in dry weather and close in high humidity to protect seeds.
  • Cloud Patterns: White, fluffy cumulus clouds suggest fair weather. Dark, heavy nimbus clouds signify impending rain or storms.
  • Wind Direction: A sudden shift in wind speed or direction can indicate an approaching storm.
  • Smell of Earth: Petrichor is the pleasant, earthy smell produced when rain hits dry soil, signaling the arrival of rain.

5. Weather Instruments

Scientists rely on specialized instruments to collect accurate weather data:

  • Thermometer: Measures temperature. Fast-moving air molecules indicate a higher temperature. Recorded in Celsius (°C) or Fahrenheit (°F).
  • Rain Gauge: Measures precipitation levels. Water collects in a funnel into a measuring cylinder marked with a scale (usually in mm).
  • Barometer: Measures atmospheric pressure (often in millibars, mb). A sudden drop in pressure often signals a storm or cyclone.
  • Wind Vane & Anemometer: A wind vane shows wind direction, while an anemometer uses rotating cups to measure wind speed (km/h).
  • Hygrometer: Measures humidity (the percentage of water vapour in the air).
Important Air pressure decreases as altitude increases. In high altitudes like Khardung La, pressure is much lower (~650 mb) compared to sea level (~1013 mb). Trekkers must acclimatise (gradually adjust) to prevent breathlessness and dizziness.
Fact Mawsynram and Cherrapunji in Meghalaya, India, receive the highest average annual rainfall in the world, measuring around 11 metres.

6. Weather Stations & Forecasting

A weather station is a facility equipped to observe and record various elements of weather continuously.

Types of Weather Stations

  • Manual Weather Stations: Trained personnel manually observe and record data using traditional instruments.
  • Automatic Weather Stations (AWS): Use permanent sensors and computers to measure and transmit data in real-time without human assistance.
  • Satellite-Based Weather Stations: Satellites orbiting Earth offer a broad view of weather systems like cloud cover and cyclones (e.g., INSAT-3D and INSAT-3DR operated by ISRO).

Role of the India Meteorological Department (IMD)

Established in 1875, the IMD is the central agency responsible for studying and forecasting weather in India. They use advanced tools like Doppler weather radars, satellites, and the Meghdoot App (which gives location-specific agro-met advice to farmers). Accurate forecasts help evacuate people during disasters, like Cyclone Yaas in 2021.
